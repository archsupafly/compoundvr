In 2023, studios still weren't building VR modes for their biggest games, so modders built the tools to do it themselves. UEVR, REFramework, and a wave of ports turned flat-to-VR from a hobby into infrastructure, fueled by new headsets, better streaming, and a community that organized into something closer to a publisher.

## 1. UEVR: Universal Unreal Engine VR injection

praydog's UEVR moved from patron testing to public release in 2023, bringing 6DOF head tracking, stereo rendering, and OpenXR/OpenVR support to thousands of Unreal Engine titles. By year end, it covered UE 4.8 through 5.x and had hundreds of tested games, including *RoboCop: Rogue City*, *Lies of P*, *Hogwarts Legacy*, and *Star Wars Jedi: Fallen Order*. Motion controls landed for a smaller set of titles, and anti-cheat remained the biggest blocker. For most PCVR users, it was the single tool that made flat-to-VR feel scalable.

[Read the UEVR guide](/articles/uevr-guide/)

## 2. Runtime plumbing: OpenXR, Virtual Desktop, Steam Link

Mods only work if they actually run in a headset. In 2023, OpenXR standardized the runtime interface, OpenComposite let OpenVR mods run on OpenXR, and Virtual Desktop's VDXR gave Quest a solid wireless PCVR path. Valve's Steam Link for Meta Quest landed in November and became the adoption multiplier—free, official, and easy enough that Quest owners could run UEVR and REFramework without wrestling with workarounds.

[Read the Virtual Desktop guide](/articles/virtual-desktop-guide/)

## 3. Meta Quest 3 launch

Meta Quest 3 launched October 10, 2023 with pancake lenses, better performance, improved passthrough, and a stronger pitch for mixed reality. Timed with UEVR's maturity and Steam Link's arrival, it quickly became the headset most people used to try flat-to-VR mods. More power meant better odds with heavier injects, and its sales brought a wave of new users looking to play their PC libraries in VR.

## 4. REFramework: VR for RE Engine

praydog's REFramework shipped three updates in 2023 (v1.4 Feb 1, v1.5.1 Jun 4, v1.5.2 Oct 2), adding VR support to RE Engine games without Capcom lifting a finger. *Resident Evil 4 Remake* got REFramework VR in patron builds in 2023 (6DOF, gamepad-first at the time), and the loader also supported *RE2*, *RE3*, *RE7*, *Village*, *Devil May Cry 5*, *Monster Hunter Rise*, and *Street Fighter 6*.

## 5. Luke Ross R.E.A.L.: Paid AAA conversions

Luke Ross continued converting AAA games through his paid Patreon R.E.A.L. framework in 2023, adding *Hogwarts Legacy VR* and *Starfield VR* to a catalogue that already included *Cyberpunk 2077 VR*. The conversions drew huge attention, but also renewed debate over monetizing mods of others' IP.

## 6. Team Beef: Native Quest ports

Team Beef brought console-class flat games natively to standalone Quest in 2023 with *Jedi Academy VR*, *Quake 4 VR*, *Prey VR*, and *Doom 3 BFG VR*. No PC required—these were tuned for mobile silicon and showed standalone could carry serious VR content.

## 7. PSVR2 launch

Sony launched PlayStation VR2 on February 22, 2023. It was the most advanced console VR headset at the time, and launch discussions were huge on Reddit. But it was a closed ecosystem with no practical path for flat-to-VR injection, so it drove demand for VR content without feeding the PC modding wave.

[PSVR2 launch review](/articles/psvr2-release-review/)

## 8. Apple Vision Pro announcement

Apple announced Vision Pro at WWDC on June 5, 2023. It didn't ship until 2024, but it drove by far the biggest mainstream spike of VR interest that year (*r/virtualreality*'s Vision Pro thread was the highest-engagement VR post we pulled). It brought millions of newcomers to the space looking for things to do in VR, many of whom discovered PCVR and community tools like UEVR and REFramework.

## 9. Mixed reality takes hold

Quest 3 pushed mixed reality into the mainstream in 2023. Meta showcased passthrough and MR experiences, and the framing shifted from "VR headset" to "spatial computer". That mental shift helped normalize re-contextualizing flatscreen content in a headset, making flat-to-VR mods feel less foreign to newcomers.

## 10. Valve's Source engine mods

Community teams pushed VR conversions for *Half-Life 2* episodes, *Portal 2*, and *Left 4 Dead 2* in 2023. Decades-old games found new life in 6DOF, proving VR doesn't need new content to gain traction.

## 11. Beyond Unreal: CryEngine mods

Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. framework brought *Crysis* (CryEngine) and *Far Cry 6* (Dunia) to VR in 2023, proving the toolkit wasn't limited to Unreal. Each conversion was built per-engine rather than through a universal injector.

## 12. Flat2VR organizes the scene

The Flat2VR Discord became the hub for flat-to-VR, linked from the UEVR README, and Flat2VR acted more like a publisher than a forum in 2023. Paired with RoadToVR's modding coverage, the community's infrastructure started looking like a publisher's org chart—while studios stayed silent.

## 13. Notable VR mod releases in 2023

A handful of game mods actually shipped or matured in 2023:

- **Half-Life 2 VR / Episodes** – Community VR conversions gained traction throughout the year.
- **Portal 2 VR** – Playable VR conversion driven by the community.
- **Left 4 Dead 2 VR** – Continued work brought co-op zombie mayhem to VR.
- **Jedi Academy VR, Quake 4 VR, Prey VR, Doom 3 BFG VR (Team Beef)** – Native Quest ports with no PC required.
- **Crysis VR, Far Cry VR** – fholger's mods matured, with CryEngine coverage expanding via R.E.A.L.
- **RE4 Remake VR via REFramework (patron)** – 6DOF/gamepad VR support landed in patron builds during 2023.

## The platform was built by the community

In 2023, modders built the VR platform the studios wouldn't. UEVR, REFramework, Team Beef, and a growing toolkit made flat games playable in VR at scale. The layer is built—the question going into 2024 was whether studios would catch up.

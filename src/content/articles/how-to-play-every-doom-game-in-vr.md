There is no single "Doom in VR" button. id Software never shipped one unified VR mode across the series, so every game reaches a headset through a different mechanism — official VR releases for a couple of them, community source ports for the classics, native fan conversions for the modern entries, and, for one 2025 release, nothing at all. I went through each one so you don't have to guess which one is worth your time. Ranked below by how well each VR path actually holds up.

## #1: Doom 3 BFG Edition

![Doom 3 BFG Edition](/images/games/doom-3-bfg-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2012 · Native VR</div>

There is no cleaner win in Doom VR than Doom 3 BFG Edition. Sony shipped an official PlayStation VR edition worldwide on March 29, 2021, and on PC the Doom 3 BFG VR: Fully Possessed mod adds native Oculus Rift/Touch and OpenVR support — its repo was last touched in August 2025, so the project is alive, but its GitHub download page was returning 404s in early October 2026 — confirm a live download before you install. VR communities consistently rate this as one of the best full-length VR conversions of a flat AAA game: full campaign, real weapon handling, save anywhere. If you install one Doom in VR this year, install this one.

## #2: DOOM (1993)

![DOOM (1993)](/images/games/doom-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">1993 · Native VR</div>

On Quest, QuestZDoom is the move: Team Beef's LZDoom/GZDoom port, built for the Oculus Quest only and installed by sideloading through SideQuest. It's an engine, not a game — you bring your own WADs or grab FreeDoom free from inside the launcher — but once loaded it gives you two-handed weapon grips, haptics, smooth and teleport locomotion, and full LZDoom mod support, including Brutal Doom and Project Brutality. The repo was still being pushed on September 30, 2026, so it's actively maintained, not abandonware. On PC, GZDoom/UZDoom-family VR builds run the originals with Brutal Doom, and the classic-Doom-in-VR crowd on r/Doom is loud about how good it feels. There is no official id or Bethesda VR release for the 1993 game, and you don't need one.

## #3: Doom II

![Doom II](/images/games/doom-ii-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">1994 · Native VR</div>

Doom II rides the exact same QuestZDoom setup — Team Beef posted Quest 3 footage of Dead Simple on Ultra-Violence, so it runs, and it runs well. Bring the Doom II WAD, flip on Brutal Doom, and you've got what r/Doom literally calls "fucking amazing in VR": the definitive way to play the sequel on a standalone headset. Bethesda never released an official VR version, so the community path is the only path — and unlike most "only path" situations in this franchise, it's a genuinely good one.

[Read the full Doom II review](/games/doom-ii)

## #4: DOOM Eternal

![DOOM Eternal](/images/games/doom-eternal-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2020 · Community VR mod</div>

DOOM Eternal is the surprise of the modern trilogy. EternalVR is a free OpenXR alpha that drops true stereo 6DoF, hand-aimed motion controllers, and a physical punch for glory kills and Blood Punch into the full campaign and both DLC — single-player only, but all of it. It's been tested on Quest 3 through Virtual Desktop and SteamVR on RTX 4080 and 3080 Ti hardware, and a tester even ran it on PSVR2 with an RTX 4060. It's alpha, so expect bugs, but it's fully playable and the most complete modern-Doom VR experience on offer. Bethesda gave Eternal no official VR support, so this mod is the whole show.

## #5: DOOM (2016)

![DOOM (2016)](/images/games/doom-2016-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2016 · Community VR mod</div>

DOOM (2016) gets a real native conversion in KHARVOX: room-scale 6DoF, tracked motion controllers, and stereo rendering across the full campaign. It needs a legal Steam or GOG copy, an OpenXR runtime (SteamVR, Meta XR, or VDXR), and the KharvoxLauncher, with the SFS renderer preferred and AER as fallback. The caveats are real. Changing graphics quality mid-level can freeze or crash, AMD GPUs and uncommon headsets need extra setup, and Windows Defender may flag the launcher as a false positive. If a full native conversion sounds like too much, a depth-based ReShade path circulates too, but that's pseudo-VR — a stereo presentation, not true six-degrees-of-freedom weapon work.

## #6: DOOM VFR

![DOOM VFR](/images/games/doom-vfr-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2017 · Native VR</div>

DOOM VFR is the only Doom game built for VR from the ground up — id and Bethesda shipped it on December 1, 2017 for PlayStation VR and HTC Vive, set after the 2016 game. That's the good news. The reception landed mixed-to-negative: Zero Punctuation's takedown pulled nearly a million views, and "Hell of a Mess" and "Genuinely Terrible" headlines are easy to find. The locomotion model is still argued — teleport-plus-dash at launch, with smooth movement claimed by some players and disputed by others — so check the current store listing before buying. Beardo Benjo's "with adjustments" video shows it can work; most players won't think it was worth the price.

[Read the full DOOM VFR review](/games/doom-vfr)

## #7: DOOM: The Dark Ages

![DOOM: The Dark Ages](/images/games/doom-the-dark-ages-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2025 · No VR path</div>

DOOM: The Dark Ages is the 2025 prequel, and it has no VR path of any kind — no official mode from id, and no community mod has surfaced. id shipped it flat for PC and consoles in May 2025, and as of late 2026 nobody has bridged it to a headset. If VR Doom is the goal, this is the one entry to skip for now.

[Read the full DOOM: The Dark Ages review](/games/doom-the-dark-ages)

Seven entries, seven different answers. The classics and Doom 3 BFG are the safe bets, with mature native ports you can install today, while the modern trilogy lives or dies by fan mods that are impressive but uneven. The Dark Ages is the reminder that "Doom in VR" is still a moving target — and the best option today may not be the best option by the time the next mod drops.

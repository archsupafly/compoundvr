Only two Grand Theft Auto games are genuinely playable in VR right now: *Grand Theft Auto: Vice City* and *Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas*. *GTA V* has a limited 2026 PCVR runtime, a few older and handheld GTA titles can be run through emulator or wrapper setups, and *GTA 6* has no VR mode announced.

I've ranked **every major GTA release** by actual VR playability as of 2026-11-08 — not by how famous the game is. That means real 6DOF builds sit above emulator wrappers, and emulator/wrapper options sit above games with no verified route.

## #1: Grand Theft Auto: Vice City

![Grand Theft Auto: Vice City](/images/games/grand-theft-auto-vice-city-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2002 game · 🟢 Playable in VR (real VR build)</div>

Vice City is the GTA VR build I'd point a friend at in 2026. You've got a full 6DOF mod path built around OpenXR, a standalone Quest port path you can run without a PC, and UEVR options for the PC crowd. That spread is why it lands at the top — whatever headset you own, there's usually a route in, and it's the clearest “real VR” GTA option right now. The catch is the usual one for community VR: setup isn't one click, and the experience quality swings with your hardware. But if you want to walk the neon of 1986 Miami in six degrees of freedom, this is the title that earns the top slot.

## #2: Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas

![Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas](/images/games/grand-theft-auto-san-andreas-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2004 game · 🟢 Playable in VR (real VR build)</div>

San Andreas is real VR, but it's the fiddly one. The cleanest path is a first-person UEVR conversion of the Definitive Edition with motion controls for driving and shooting — playable and genuinely annoying to configure the first time. On top of that, late 2026 saw a standalone fan build reported for Quest 3 with no PC required, which is the build I'd watch if you own a headset and not a gaming rig. The official Meta Quest port that got announced back in 2021 is still on indefinite hold, so don't wait on that. San Andreas earns #2 because the playable builds exist and they're meaty, but nobody should call the setup painless.

## #3: Grand Theft Auto V

![Grand Theft Auto V](/images/games/gta-5-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2013 game · 🟡 Limited / compromised VR runtime</div>

GTA V is the one with a story. Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. VR mod turned the game into a stereoscopic drive-around back in 2019, then Take-Two sent a DMCA to Patreon in July 2022 and Ross pulled it — along with his Red Dead Redemption 2 and Mafia mods. Ross came back in 2026. On October 15, 2026 he shipped R.E.A.L. VR Early Access, build 2610.15.2, adding support for GTA V Enhanced, the current-gen PC re-release, with a next-day hotfix for broken shooting from vehicles. Ross says it works, but read the fine print. Controls are keyboard, mouse, or gamepad only — the headset handles your view and nothing else, no motion controllers by design. Getting it running means launching with `-nobattleye`, which disables BattlEye anti-cheat and quietly removes GTA Online from the equation, so it's single-player only. The Enhanced build lives in Ross's paid Patreon early-access tier, and Take-Two still hadn't commented on the return as of this writing, so the legal footing is "no guarantees." That's why it sits at #3: a working, limited curiosity, not a recommendation I'd stake a weekend on.

## The Rest of the Series: Emulator, Wrapper, or No Verified Route

This is where the “every GTA game” question gets honest. A lot of GTA titles can technically be launched through **emulators, virtual-screen wrappers, or generic injection drivers**. That’s why I’ve moved many older entries into 🟡 Limited / compromised — you may be able to *play* them while wearing a headset, but you’re usually looking at a flat game in a VR space, not a real VR conversion.

- **2D/top-down GTA (GTA, London, GTA 2, Advance):** emulator/wrapper is the realistic headset route. Expect a virtual screen, not VR presence.
- **PSP-era stories (Liberty City Stories, Vice City Stories):** emulator/VR wrapper territory. Vice City Stories has the clearest PPSSPP VR example; Liberty City Stories is in the same practical class unless a verified dedicated conversion appears.
- **3D-era PC games (GTA III, GTA IV, Episodes):** some community history exists around injection drivers, first-person camera mods, or source-port experiments, but I’m not calling those verified VR conversions. Treat them as limited/experimental at best, and be careful with old files and takedown history.
- **Handheld oddballs (Chinatown Wars):** emulator/wrapper is the plausible route, not a VR-native experience.
- **GTA Online and Trilogy Definitive Edition:** Online is not a supported/verified VR path (and R.E.A.L. disables anti-cheat). Trilogy has no official VR mode; any VR route is game/mod-specific.

If a working, verified VR build surfaces for any of these, we’ll cover it. Until then, the useful answer is: emulator/wrapper = limited, not real VR.

## GTA 6 in VR: The Honest Outlook

GTA VI is dated November 19, 2026, and it's console-only at launch — PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S, with no PC version listed. No VR mode has been announced, and no VR statement of any kind has come from Rockstar or Take-Two. Absence of an announcement isn't proof it'll never happen, but it's the only thing the record supports, so I'm not going to manufacture a "GTA 6 VR is coming" line out of hype.

If you're wondering why VR is a long shot here, the reasons stack up. The game runs on Rockstar's RAGE engine, a proprietary, closed codebase with no public rendering hook — the opposite of the Unreal titles that UEVR can inject into, which is why the San Andreas and Vice City builds exist at all. Rockstar has never shipped a first-party VR mode for a single GTA; the only first-party VR commitment in the franchise's history is the Meta Quest San Andreas port announced in 2021, and that's been on indefinite hold since August 2024 without a release. Then there's the Luke Ross precedent: when he built VR mods for GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2, Take-Two sent a DMCA and he pulled them. A VR GTA 6, whether official or modded, runs straight into that wall.

If Rockstar ever does VR, it would most likely be a first-party headset exclusive years after launch, not a day-one mode, and a community mod would depend on someone cracking RAGE's rendering in a way nobody has shipped yet. None of that is confirmed. What's confirmed is that on November 19, 2026, you'll be playing GTA VI flat, on a console, like everyone else.

## The Bottom Line

Every GTA game has *some* answer now, but not the same answer. **GTA: Vice City** and **GTA: San Andreas** are the genuinely playable VR picks. **GTA V** is a limited 2026 view-in-VR runtime with real constraints. **Vice City Stories** and many older/handheld GTA titles can be approached through emulator or virtual-screen wrappers, but that’s limited VR, not a conversion. **GTA Online, Trilogy, and GTA 6** have no verified VR route/mode. If you want GTA in a headset this year, start with Vice City, then San Andreas — and treat emulator/wrapper setups as nostalgia options, not the real thing.

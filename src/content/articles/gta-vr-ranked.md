Two GTA games are playable in VR today, and the rest of the series is a graveyard of announcements, dead mods, and emulator tricks that barely count. I've ranked all of it by what you can genuinely do in a headset right now — not by which game moved the most copies.

The answer, in one screen:

| Game | VR today? | What it runs on |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Grand Theft Auto: Vice City | Yes — best of the bunch | 6DOF mod (OpenXR), standalone Quest port, UEVR |
| Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas | Yes — real, but fiddly | UEVR first-person conversion + standalone Quest 3 fan build |
| Grand Theft Auto V | Sort of — revived late 2026 | Luke Ross R.E.A.L. runtime on the Enhanced PC build; paid, gamepad-only, single-player only |
| Grand Theft Auto: Vice City Stories | Barely | PPSSPP VR virtual-screen wrapper on Quest; not true VR |
| GTA III / IV / the rest | No | No verified build, no CompoundVR page |
| GTA 6 | No | No VR announced; console-only at launch |

If you own a headset and want a GTA you can stand inside, your choices are Vice City and San Andreas. Everything below them is a compromise, a corpse, or a flat screen wearing a VR costume.

## #1: Grand Theft Auto: Vice City

![Grand Theft Auto: Vice City](/images/games/grand-theft-auto-vice-city-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2026 · Multi-Route Coverage</div>

Vice City is the GTA VR build I'd point a friend at in 2026. You've got a full 6DOF mod built on OpenXR, a standalone Quest port you can run without a PC, and a UEVR injection path for the PC crowd. That spread is why it lands at the top — whatever headset you own, there's a way in, and it's the one build in this series that's still getting active development rather than sitting on a shelf. The catch is the usual one for community VR: setup isn't one click, and the experience quality swings with your hardware. But if you want to walk the neon of 1986 Miami in six degrees of freedom, this is the title that lets you.

## #2: Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas

![Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas](/images/games/grand-theft-auto-san-andreas-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2025 · Multi-Route Coverage</div>

San Andreas is real VR, but it's the fiddly one. The cleanest path is a first-person UEVR conversion of the Definitive Edition with motion controls for driving and shooting — playable and genuinely annoying to configure the first time. On top of that, late 2026 saw a standalone fan build hit Quest 3 with no PC required, which is the build I'd watch if you own a headset and not a gaming rig. The official Meta Quest port that got announced back in 2021 is still on indefinite hold, so don't wait on that. San Andreas earns the #2 spot because the playable builds exist and they're meaty, but nobody should call the setup painless.

## #3: Grand Theft Auto V

![Grand Theft Auto V](/images/games/gta-5-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2019 · Multi-Route Coverage</div>

GTA V is the one with a story. Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. VR mod turned the game into a stereoscopic drive-around back in 2019, then Take-Two sent a DMCA to Patreon in July 2022 and Ross pulled it — along with his Red Dead Redemption 2 and Mafia mods. Ross came back in 2026. On October 15, 2026 he shipped R.E.A.L. VR Early Access, build 2610.15.2, adding support for GTA V Enhanced, the current-gen PC re-release, with a next-day hotfix for broken shooting from vehicles. Ross says it works, but read the fine print. Controls are keyboard, mouse, or gamepad only — the headset handles your view and nothing else, no motion controllers by design. Getting it running means launching with -nobattleye, which disables BattlEye anti-cheat and quietly removes GTA Online from the equation, so it's single-player only. The Enhanced build lives in Ross's paid Patreon early-access tier, and Take-Two still hadn't commented on the return as of this writing, so the legal footing is "no guarantees." That's why it sits at #3 — a working, playable curiosity, not a recommendation I'd stake a weekend on.

## #4: Grand Theft Auto: Vice City Stories

![Grand Theft Auto: Vice City Stories](/images/games/grand-theft-auto-vice-city-stories-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Multi-Route Coverage</div>

Vice City Stories is on this list only because someone will ask about it. It's a PSP game, and the only VR build anyone has made is running it inside PPSSPP VR on a Quest — a virtual-screen wrapper that plants a flat rectangle in front of you in a VR void. You are not standing in 1984 Vice City; you're sitting in a virtual cinema watching a handheld game someone else is playing. That's not nothing if you want the story in a headset, but it is not a VR conversion, and I'm not going to dress it up as one. It earns the bottom slot because it's the weakest thing here that can technically still be called "GTA in VR."

## The Rest of the Series

Then there's everything we didn't rank, and I'm not going to invent one for it. Grand Theft Auto III, Grand Theft Auto IV, Liberty City Stories, Chinatown Wars, and the original top-down GTA and GTA 2 have no verified VR build that CompoundVR can point you to, and no page of ours covers them. GTA IV's community options were generic injection drivers and first-person camera mods, not a dedicated VR build, and the 3D-era PC titles carried reverse-engineered source ports that Take-Two DMCA'd in 2021. None of that adds up to something I'd tell you to go install. The Trilogy – The Definitive Edition has no VR mode either. If a working build surfaces for any of these, we'll cover it — but right now the answer is the one you don't want: it isn't there.

## GTA 6 in VR: The Honest Outlook

GTA VI is dated November 19, 2026, and it's console-only at launch — PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S, with no PC version listed. No VR mode has been announced, and no VR statement of any kind has come from Rockstar or Take-Two. Absence of an announcement isn't proof it'll never happen, but it's the only thing the record supports, so I'm not going to manufacture a "GTA 6 VR is coming" line out of hype.

If you're wondering why VR is a long shot here, the reasons stack up. The game runs on Rockstar's RAGE engine, a proprietary, closed codebase with no public rendering hook — the opposite of the Unreal titles that UEVR can inject into, which is why the San Andreas and Vice City builds exist at all. Rockstar has never shipped a first-party VR mode for a single GTA; the only first-party VR commitment in the franchise's history is the Meta Quest San Andreas port announced in 2021, and that's been on indefinite hold since August 2024 without a release. Then there's the Luke Ross precedent: when he built VR mods for GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2, Take-Two sent a DMCA and he pulled them. A VR GTA 6, whether official or modded, runs straight into that wall.

If Rockstar ever does VR, it would most likely be a first-party headset exclusive years after launch, not a day-one mode, and a community mod would depend on someone cracking RAGE's rendering in a way nobody has shipped yet. None of that is confirmed. What's confirmed is that on November 19, 2026, you'll be playing GTA VI flat, on a console, like everyone else.

## The Bottom Line

Two games deliver the thing you came for — Vice City and San Andreas, both genuinely playable in VR and both covered in depth on CompoundVR. GTA V is a revived, paid, gamepad-only curiosity that works but won't convert a skeptic. Vice City Stories is a PSP emulator wrapper wearing a VR costume. The rest of the series has no verified build, and GTA 6 has no VR mode announced. If you want GTA in a headset this year, it's Vice City, and San Andreas is right behind it.

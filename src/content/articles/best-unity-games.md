Unity has powered so many games that saying a game is “made in Unity” barely tells you what kind of game it is anymore.

It can mean *Rust*, a survival sandbox that has eaten entire years of people’s lives. It can mean *Hollow Knight*, one of the defining Metroidvanias of the last decade. It can mean *Among Us*, *Phasmophobia*, *Valheim*, *RimWorld*, *Subnautica*, *Cuphead*, *Cities: Skylines*, *Risk of Rain 2* or a dozen other games that have almost nothing in common beyond the engine underneath them.

That is exactly why most lists of “games made with Unity” are not very useful. They tend to be a grab bag of famous titles with no real explanation for why one ranks above another.

So I wanted to do this differently.

This is our ranking of the **50 biggest Unity games on PC**, weighted primarily toward lifetime popularity: how many people bought or played them, Steam review volume, peak player counts, longevity, cultural reach and whether people are still showing up years later. It is not a review-score ranking. A brilliant five-hour indie game does not automatically outrank a survival game that has held millions of players for a decade.

Then I added the part that makes this a CompoundVR list: **I checked what happens when you put a headset on.**

Some of these games have official VR modes. Some have community conversions so good that they feel like unofficial ports. Some have interesting but compromised experiments. Some have a separate VR adaptation rather than support in the original game. And plenty still have no VR route I would actually recommend.

If you came here because you simply wanted to know the best and most popular games built with Unity, the main ranking is for you.

If you came here because you want to know which of those games are secretly some of the best PCVR games you can play, skip down to [The Best Unity Games to Actually Play in VR](#the-best-unity-games-to-actually-play-in-vr).

## 1. Rust

![Rust](/images/games/rust-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

If I have to pick the biggest Unity success story on PC, I still start with *Rust*.

Facepunch turned a rough survival experiment into one of Steam's permanent fixtures. It has outlived survival trends, battle-royale trends and entire generations of competitors while continuing to pull huge player numbers and an absurd volume of user reviews. The important thing is not that *Rust* once had a big launch. It is that people are still losing weekends to it more than a decade later.

For VR modders, though, *Rust* remains one of the biggest white whales in Unity.

## 2. Among Us

![Among Us 3D](/images/games/among-us-3d-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">⚪ Separate VR game</div>

There was a period where you could not open Twitch, YouTube or a group chat without seeing *Among Us*. That cultural explosion was bigger than Steam alone can capture, but the PC numbers are still enormous: hundreds of thousands of concurrent players at its peak and one of the largest review counts of any Unity game on the platform.

It is also one of the cleanest examples of a flat hit generating a dedicated VR product.

[Read the full Among Us 3D VR review](/games/among-us-3d/)

## 3. Phasmophobia

![Phasmophobia](/images/games/phasmophobia-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">✅ Official VR</div>

*Phasmophobia* is the Unity game that makes the strongest case that VR does not need to be the main platform to matter.

The flat game became one of Steam's defining co-op horror hits. The VR mode was there from the beginning, and for all of its interaction jank, putting four people into a haunted house with proximity voice, physical equipment and a ghost that reacts to what you say is still a uniquely good use of a headset.

If all you cared about was overlap between **massive Unity popularity** and **actual playable VR**, this would be near the very top of the list.

[Read the full Phasmophobia VR review](/games/phasmophobia/)

## 4. The Forest

![The Forest](/images/games/the-forest-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">✅ Official VR</div>

Before *Sons of the Forest*, there was *The Forest*, and the original still has a ridiculous footprint on PC. It became one of the survival genre's evergreen recommendations rather than a game people simply remember from Early Access.

Endnight also did something I wish more studios had copied: it added VR to the actual game.

[Read the full The Forest VR review](/games/the-forest/)

## 5. Genshin Impact

![Genshin Impact](/images/games/genshin-impact-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

If this were a worldwide cross-platform popularity list rather than a PC-focused one, *Genshin Impact* could easily be even higher.

Its PC playerbase is difficult to measure cleanly because it lives outside Steam, but there is no serious argument that it belongs anywhere near the margins of a “popular Unity games” list. HoYoverse built one of the largest live-service RPGs in the world on Unity.

The dream of standing in Teyvat remains a dream.

## 6. Valheim

![Valheim](/images/games/valheim-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟢 Full 6DOF/mod</div>

*Valheim* is one of those PC launches that felt impossible while it was happening: a small survival game suddenly sitting near the top of Steam with half a million concurrent players, then refusing to disappear.

What matters more for this site is what the community did with it afterward.

For my money, this remains one of the best arguments for the entire Flat2VR scene: take a deep game that already has hundreds of hours of systems and give it a body.

[Read the full Valheim VR review](/games/valheim/)

## 7. Unturned

![Unturned](/images/games/unturned-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟡 Limited/experimental</div>

It is easy to underestimate *Unturned* because it is free and visually looks like somebody crossed *Roblox* with a zombie server. The numbers say otherwise. It has been one of Steam's most widely played survival sandboxes for years.

And as of late 2026, it suddenly became much more interesting for us.

This is one I would watch closely.

[Read the full Unturned VR review](/games/unturned/)

## 8. Fall Guys

![Fall Guys](/images/games/fall-guys-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

At launch, *Fall Guys* was everywhere. Its Steam version peaked at more than 170,000 concurrent players before the game eventually became free-to-play and moved its main PC distribution to Epic.

The total footprint is much larger than its current Steam chart suggests.

## 9. Hollow Knight

![Hollow Knight](/images/games/hollow-knight-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟡 Limited/experimental</div>

*Hollow Knight* is the perfect counterexample to the idea that popularity rankings have to be dominated by multiplayer games. It started as an indie Metroidvania and became a genuine PC classic, with an enormous Steam review count and a playerbase that surged all over again around *Silksong*.

Its VR solution is also one of the stranger ones on this list.

That is not “full VR” in the usual CompoundVR sense. It is still very cool.

![7 Days to Die](/images/games/7-days-to-die-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟢 Full 6DOF/mod</div>

Few Early Access stories are as long or as stubbornly successful as *7 Days to Die*. It has been rebuilt, renamed, overhauled and argued about for more than a decade while keeping a massive survival audience.

![Hollow Knight: Silksong](/images/games/hollow-knight-silksong-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟡 Limited/experimental</div>

After years of becoming a meme for not being released, *Silksong* finally arrived in 2025 and immediately justified its place near the top of any Unity popularity list. Its launch peak and first-year review volume were enormous.

It is a headset-enhanced way to experience the art, not a motion-controlled remake.

[Read the full Hollow Knight: Silksong VR review](/games/hollow-knight-silksong/)

![R.E.P.O.](/images/games/repo-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟢 Full 6DOF/mod</div>

*R.E.P.O.* is the newer co-op phenomenon I am most comfortable putting this high because the numbers have already stopped looking temporary. The review count and estimated ownership put it in the same conversation as games that have been around for years.

It also has one of the most exciting newer Unity VR conversions.

## 15. Subnautica

![Subnautica](/images/games/subnautica-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟢 Full 6DOF/mod</div>

*Subnautica* is one of the defining survival-exploration games on PC. It sold the fantasy of going somewhere genuinely alien without needing a map full of quest icons to keep you interested.

It also has one of the messier but more rewarding VR histories on this list.

It is still a work in progress. It is also one of the best worlds on this entire list to physically inhabit.

[Read the full Subnautica VR review](/games/subnautica/)

![Bloons TD 6](/images/games/bloons-td-6-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

Do not let the cartoon monkeys fool you. *Bloons TD 6* is a monster.

*Raft* turned a tiny square of wood and a plastic hook into one of PC's biggest co-op survival games.

![PEAK](/images/games/peak-game-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟢 Full 6DOF/mod</div>

*PEAK* is another reminder that the Steam co-op machine can make a game enormous almost overnight. The climbing loop, social chaos and streamer friendliness did the rest.

![Risk of Rain 2](/images/games/risk-of-rain-2-vr-hero.jpg)

*Risk of Rain 2* has one of the strongest long tails of any modern roguelite. The playerbase keeps returning because the underlying loop — movement, loot synergies and escalating chaos — remains ridiculously good.

## 20. Sons of the Forest

![Sons of the Forest](/images/games/sons-of-the-forest-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟡 Limited/experimental</div>

The sequel to *The Forest* was one of Steam's largest survival launches, peaking at more than 400,000 players.

The painful part was watching it ship without the official VR mode that made the original so memorable.

This is the project I want to turn green next.

## 21. Schedule I

![Schedule I](/images/games/schedule-i-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟡 Limited/experimental</div>

*Schedule I* exploded in 2025 with one of the largest indie Steam launches in recent memory. It is the kind of game that makes a popularity list move quickly: more than 450,000 concurrent players at peak and hundreds of thousands of reviews in a remarkably short time.

That makes it interesting rather than definitive. If the motion-control work lands, it becomes an immediate CompoundVR re-review candidate.

## 22. People Playground

![People Playground](/images/games/people-playground-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

*People Playground* is one of the strangest mega-hits on Steam: a brutally simple physics sandbox with a review count that dwarfs many AAA releases.

And yes, I know what half of you are thinking. A room-scale *People Playground* where you physically grab, wire and catastrophically misuse everything would be horrifying. It would also probably be incredible.

## 23. Cities: Skylines

![Cities: Skylines](/images/games/cities-vr-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">⚪ Separate VR game</div>

For years, *Cities: Skylines* was effectively the modern city builder. Its longevity, mod scene and huge ownership numbers make it an easy inclusion.

The original also makes an interesting target for Unity stereo frameworks because a city is naturally readable as a 3D tabletop, but that is a different proposition from having a true VR port.

[Read the full Cities: VR review](/games/cities-vr/)

## 24. Vampire Survivors

![Vampire Survivors](/images/games/vampire-survivors-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

*Vampire Survivors* is one of the most influential small games of the decade, spawning an entire wave of imitators and proving that a tiny price and a brutally readable loop can be more powerful than production scale.

The game migrated to a Unity-based engine implementation, so the current PC game belongs in this conversation even though its earliest history is more complicated.

A third-person or tabletop VR treatment could be fun, but the game's appeal is already so immediate on a flat screen that VR would have to bring more than novelty.

## 25. Escape from Tarkov

![Escape from Tarkov](/images/games/escape-from-tarkov-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

*Tarkov* is another game whose true PC scale is hidden by the fact that it spent most of its life outside Steam. It helped define the modern extraction shooter before the genre had a universally agreed name.

There are plenty of VR shooters that borrow pieces of *Tarkov*. What we do not have is *Tarkov* itself in a form I would tell you to install.

## 26. RimWorld

![RimWorld](/images/games/rimworld-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

*RimWorld* is the kind of PC game that does not need a gigantic launch spike because people simply never stop playing it. Years of expansions, workshop mods and emergent stories have made it one of the definitive colony sims.

Generic stereo experiments are possible in Unity games, but *RimWorld* is exactly where I draw the line between “technically displayed in a headset” and “VR adds enough to justify wearing one.”

## 27. Human: Fall Flat

![Human: Fall Flat](/images/games/human-fall-flat-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">⚪ Separate VR game</div>

*Human: Fall Flat* became a giant because its physics comedy survives language, age and platform. Watching someone fail to climb a ledge is universal.

Its path into VR is unusually interesting because a fan VR prototype helped inspire an official product.

[Read the full Human Fall Flat VR review](/games/human-fall-flat/)

## 28. Cuphead

![Cuphead](/images/games/cuphead-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

*Cuphead* is one of Unity's most famous examples simply because it does not look like what people imagine when they hear “Unity game.” Its hand-drawn animation and boss design made it a phenomenon.

You could imagine a layered diorama approach similar to *Hollow Knight*, but there is currently no mature VR route I would put in front of a normal reader.

## 29. Hearthstone

![Hearthstone](/images/games/hearthstone-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

Blizzard's card game became one of the defining digital CCGs and has had a huge PC audience for more than a decade.

This is one of those cases where a native tabletop VR interface makes conceptual sense, but the actual live game has never been rebuilt around that idea.

## 30. Goose Goose Duck

![Goose Goose Duck](/images/games/goose-goose-duck-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

A 700,000-plus Steam concurrency peak looks absurd until you remember how large *Goose Goose Duck* became in parts of Asia.

Its social-deduction structure makes the existence of *Among Us 3D* an obvious comparison, but there is no equivalent mature VR edition here.

## 31. Honkai: Star Rail

![Honkai: Star Rail](/images/games/honkai-star-rail-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

Like *Genshin*, *Honkai: Star Rail* is much larger than Steam-centered PC metrics can show. It is a global live-service RPG with a major PC audience and a Unity foundation.

Again: live service plus anti-cheat plus unofficial injection is not where I send readers looking for a reliable VR night.

## 32. Muck

![Muck](/images/games/muck-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟡 Limited/experimental</div>

*Muck* is free, silly, brutally simple and much more popular than a lot of “prestige” games that would normally appear above it on a best-of list.

That makes it a curiosity, not one of the Unity VR mods I would build a headset purchase around.

## 33. Content Warning

![Content Warning](/images/games/content-warning-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟢 Full 6DOF/mod</div>

Landfall has a talent for games that become instant group-chat events, and *Content Warning* did exactly that.

The conversion makes sense because *Content Warning* is already about physically getting too close to something terrible while your friends make bad decisions. VR just removes the safe distance.

## 34. Slime Rancher

![Slime Rancher](/images/games/slime-rancher-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟢 Full 6DOF/mod</div>

*Slime Rancher* has quietly become one of the most beloved first-person games built in Unity. It is friendly, readable and built around a tool — the Vacpack — that begs to be held in your hand.

This is exactly the kind of conversion I want more of — a rethink of the interaction that matters most, not a graphics hack.

## 35. Totally Accurate Battle Simulator

![Totally Accurate Battle Simulator](/images/games/totally-accurate-battle-simulator-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

*TABS* grew from a joke prototype into one of the most recognizable physics sandboxes on PC.

Like *Cities: Skylines*, it is almost begging for a tabletop-scale VR view. That remains potential rather than a finished recommendation.

## 36. Stumble Guys

![Stumble Guys](/images/games/stumble-guys-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

The PC version is only one slice of *Stumble Guys'* much larger cross-platform success, but even on Steam it has enough reach to belong in the lower half of this ranking.

## 37. Crab Game

![Crab Game](/images/games/crab-game-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

Dani's free multiplayer riff on elimination-game chaos pulled huge player numbers and an even larger amount of YouTube/Twitch attention.

The first-person perspective makes it look tempting, but there is a difference between “this should work in VR” and an actual maintained conversion.

## 38. Ori and the Will of the Wisps

![Ori and the Will of the Wisps](/images/games/ori-and-the-will-of-the-wisps-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

*Ori and the Will of the Wisps* is one of the best-looking games ever shipped on Unity and one of the strongest Metroidvanias on PC.

There are old stereoscopic/injection paths people have experimented with, but nothing I would put in the same category as the purpose-built *Hollow Knight* depth mods.

## 39. Inscryption

![Inscryption](/images/games/inscryption-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟡 Limited/experimental</div>

*Inscryption* is a smaller commercial hit than the games above it, but its cultural footprint is bigger than the raw player count suggests. It is exactly the sort of game people keep discovering years after release.

It is also newly interesting for VR.

But the cabin section is so naturally suited to VR that this project is absolutely worth watching.

## 40. Kerbal Space Program

![Kerbal Space Program](/images/games/kerbal-space-program-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">🟡 Limited/experimental</div>

*KSP* is one of the great “I accidentally learned something while playing a game” success stories. Its modding community is a large part of why it still matters.

This is not the cleanest “entire game becomes a room-scale VR title” conversion on the list, but sitting inside a cockpit you assembled yourself is a very specific kind of magic.

[Read the full Kerbal Space Program VR review](/games/kerbal-space-program/)

## 41. BattleBit Remastered

![BattleBit Remastered](/images/games/battlebit-remastered-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

For a while, *BattleBit* was the answer to “what if the giant multiplayer shooter was cheap, readable and actually ran on everything?” Its launch was enormous.

A 254-player firefight is already enough of a technical and sensory problem without asking a community mod to render it twice.

## 42. Oxygen Not Included

![Oxygen Not Included](/images/games/oxygen-not-included-vr-hero.jpg)
<div class="rank-meta">❌ No VR route</div>

Klei's colony sim has the sort of long-tail PC audience that makes Steam rankings deceptively stable. People who love it tend to love it for hundreds of hours.

This is another case where the interface cost would probably outweigh the benefit of stereoscopic depth.

## 43. V Rising

## How I ranked the most popular Unity games

This is not a disguised “my favorite games” list.

For the Steam-heavy part of the PC market, the clearest public signals are lifetime review volume, estimated ownership, peak concurrent players and whether the game still has a meaningful audience years later. Those numbers are not interchangeable — a free game can generate enormous owner estimates, while a single-player game can sell millions without ever putting 300,000 people online at the same time — so I did not pretend there is one magic number that sorts everything perfectly.

A few examples show the scale we are dealing with. As of this update, *Rust* has more than **1.4 million Steam reviews**. *Phasmophobia* is over **840,000**. *Among Us* is over **750,000**. *The Forest*, *Valheim*, *Unturned* and *Hollow Knight* are all well into the half-million-review range. Newer games such as *R.E.P.O.*, *Silksong* and *Schedule I* have reached absurd numbers in a fraction of the time.

The awkward cases are games that do not live primarily on Steam. *Genshin Impact*, *Hearthstone*, *Honkai: Star Rail*, *Escape from Tarkov* and *Albion Online* all have large PC audiences that Steam cannot fully measure. *Fall Guys* is another weird one because its original Steam run was huge before the game moved its center of gravity to Epic.

That is why I would read the exact order as an **editorial popularity ranking**, not a forensic sales chart. The top 20 are fairly easy to defend. Once we get into the 30s and 40s, there are a dozen games close enough that one big update, giveaway or breakout year could shuffle the order.

## The Best Unity Games to Actually Play in VR

Now throw the popularity ranking away.

If the question changes from **“What are the biggest Unity games?”** to **“Which Unity games are actually best in a headset?”**, the order changes completely.

This is the ranking I would use for a VR player. I care about the quality of the underlying game, how much VR adds, whether the conversion has proper spatial presence, whether controls make sense, setup friction and whether I would genuinely choose the headset version over playing flat.

A few of these games are not even in the popularity Top 50 above. That is the point.

### 1. Outer Wilds — NomaiVR

[Outer Wilds in VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/outer-wilds/) is still one of the benchmark Flat2VR conversions.

NomaiVR gives the game full 6DOF and motion controls, but the technical achievement is not why it ranks first. *Outer Wilds* is fundamentally about scale, distance and realizing that the tiny thing in the sky is a place you can actually go. VR weaponizes that design. Planets stop being images and start being objects with size.

It can be intense. It can run heavy. It is still one of the purest examples of VR making an already brilliant game feel newly understood.

### 2. Firewatch — Two Forks VR

[Firewatch VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/firewatch/) is almost the opposite of *Outer Wilds*: calm, linear, grounded and easy to run.

Two Forks VR puts the map, compass and radio into your hands and lets the Wyoming wilderness do the rest. There is no combat system to fight with and no UI-heavy RPG layer to rebuild. The game is walking, looking, listening and being somewhere.

That makes it one of the cleanest VR conversions I know.

### 3. Valheim — VHVR

[Valheim VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/valheim/) is what happens when a huge survival game gets the physicality its systems were always missing.

Chopping, blocking, bow shooting, building and sailing all benefit. More importantly, it stays *Valheim*: the progression, bosses, co-op and hundreds of hours of survival systems are still underneath the headset.

### 4. Risk of Rain 2 — Resurrected VRMod

The resurrection of the [Risk of Rain 2 VR mod](https://compoundvr.com/games/risk-of-rain-2/) is one of my favorite developments of 2026.

The old mod was brilliant but increasingly trapped behind version compatibility. The new OpenXR rebuild targets the current Steam game again and restores the full-motion chaos without asking normal players to become depot archaeologists first.

It is fast, intense and absolutely not the game I would use to introduce your aunt to VR.

For experienced players, it rules.

### 5. Lethal Company — LCVR

[Lethal Company VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/lethal-company/) is one of the easiest conversions to recommend because the game's physical comedy gets better when you are physically involved.

The shovel is in your hand. The terminal is in front of you. Your friend is screaming over proximity chat. Something is moving in a room you do not want to enter.

Mixed flat/VR multiplayer is the final piece. Your friends do not all need headsets.

### 6. The Forest — Official VR

[The Forest](https://compoundvr.com/games/the-forest/) is old VR by modern standards, but it is still the whole survival game with tracked controls.

The age shows in performance and interaction roughness. The caves also still make me understand why somebody might decide that civilization is good, actually.

### 7. 7 Days to Die — 7DVR

[7DVR](https://compoundvr.com/games/7-days-to-die/) wins on depth.

Most native VR survival games cannot compete with the sheer volume of crafting, building, looting and progression that already exists in *7 Days to Die*. The mod does not need to invent a game; it needs to give that game a body.

### 8. R.E.P.O. — RepoXR

RepoXR is one of the strongest new-generation Unity conversions: 6DOF, motion controls, OpenXR and mixed multiplayer.

It also benefits from the fact that *R.E.P.O.* is already a game about moving objects badly while under pressure. Tracked hands fit the joke.

### 9. Phasmophobia — Official VR

[Phasmophobia VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/phasmophobia/) is janky. I am not going to pretend otherwise.

It is also one of the few horror games where the VR version changes the social dynamic. Holding the equipment, physically opening doors and speaking into the dark are not cosmetic additions. They make you more vulnerable, which is exactly what this game wants.

### 10. Subnautica — SubmersedVR

[Subnautica](https://compoundvr.com/games/subnautica/) has a world that deserves VR more than its old official implementation deserves credit for.

SubmersedVR is the route I would take because it is trying to turn the game into modern motion-controlled VR instead of preserving the old gamepad-first mode. Expect mod roughness. Also expect the first leviathan encounter to become a much more personal problem.

### 11. Neon White — HeavenVR

[Neon White VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/neon-white/) sounds like a motion-sickness challenge invented as a dare.

Instead, HeavenVR is one of the sharper community conversions around, with full motion controls and the kind of direct aiming that fits the game's speedrunning obsession perfectly.

You need VR legs. Bring them.

### 12. GTFO — GTFO VR

[GTFO VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/gtfo/) gives a genuinely hard co-op shooter room-scale presence and real weapon handling.

There are technically cleaner conversions. There are very few that create the same feeling of four people moving through a dark facility knowing that one stupid noise can ruin the run.

### 13. Gunfire Reborn — VR Mod

[Gunfire Reborn VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/gunfire-reborn/) turns an already-good roguelite FPS into something much closer to a native VR shooter.

The catch is version management. The reward is actual hand-aimed gunplay inside a game with far more progression and replayability than most purpose-built VR shooters.

### 14. Slime Rancher — SRVR

SRVR is exactly the upgrade *Slime Rancher* needed — tracked hands and a Vacpack you actually aim and use.

It is cheerful, readable, mechanically appropriate for VR and far deeper than the old official VR side experience suggested.

### 15. Raft — RaftVR

[Raft VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/raft/) is not the flashiest conversion on this list, but the hook alone earns it a spot.

Throw, reel, build, paddle, panic about the shark. The flat verbs become physical verbs with very little conceptual friction.

### 16. Outward — OutwardVR

[Outward VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/outward/) is for the player who wants an actual open-world RPG in a headset and accepts that community-mod jank is the entrance fee.

The maintained conversion has proper 6DOF, hands, weapons and motion-controlled combat. Underneath that is still a strange, punishing RPG with survival systems, factions and co-op.

### 17. DREDGE — Dredge VR

*DREDGE* is one of those conversions that makes more sense the longer you think about it.

The community mod provides 6DOF and full motion-controller support and can be played from start to finish. Being physically surrounded by black water while something moves under the boat is a much stronger pitch than “cozy fishing game.”

### 18. Content Warning — CWVR

CWVR does for *Content Warning* what LCVR did for *Lethal Company*: it takes a co-op game about terrible judgment and makes the consequences more physical.

Full 6DOF, motion controls and mixed multiplayer get it over the line.

### 19. PEAK — PeakVR

PeakVR is still young, but it already has the important pieces: OpenXR, 6DOF and tracked hands.

I want more physical climbing before I call it native-feeling, but as a way to stand inside one of the biggest co-op games of the moment, it is absolutely worth following.

### 20. Unturned — UnturnedVR

This is the speculative pick.

UnturnedVR only arrived in late October 2026, and I would not call it polished yet. But it already has the things I care about: true stereo VR, tracked hands, two-handed guns, body holsters and a wrist HUD.

If development keeps moving at this pace, this may be much higher the next time we update the list.

### Separate official VR adaptations worth playing

Two massive Unity games deserve their own category because their VR versions are **separate products**, not VR modes for the original PC game:

- [**Among Us 3D**](https://compoundvr.com/games/among-us-3d/) — a proper first-person VR/social-deduction rebuild with motion controls. It crossplays with the 3D PC version, not the original 2D *Among Us*.
- [**Human Fall Flat VR**](https://compoundvr.com/games/human-fall-flat/) — an official VR rework built around motion-controlled puppeteering while keeping the third-person comedy intact.
- **Cities: VR** — a dedicated VR interpretation of the city-builder concept rather than the original *Cities: Skylines* PC game and its giant mod ecosystem.

## The most interesting new Unity VR mods to watch in late 2026

This is the section I would bookmark if you follow Flat2VR development rather than just finished releases.

### UnturnedVR

The biggest surprise of this update. It is brand new, experimental and already attempting full motion-controller interaction. The BattlEye limitation keeps it away from normal public-server play, but the foundation is much more ambitious than a camera injector.

### Risk of Rain 2: Resurrected VRMod

This is not merely a new mod. It fixes a real historical problem: the famous original *Risk of Rain 2* VR mod had fallen behind the game's Unity upgrade. The community rebuild moves it to OpenXR and current Steam builds again.

### RepoXR

R.E.P.O. became huge, and the VR conversion did not stop at stereoscopic rendering. Full 6DOF, motion controls and mixed multiplayer make this one of the more important modern Unity Flat2VR projects.

### SonsVR

Still alpha, still imperfect, still the best chance we currently have of getting the *Sons of the Forest* VR mode Endnight never shipped.

### Schedule I VR

The current release is the halfway point: true stereo and 6DOF, but gamepad controls. If the promised motion-control layer arrives and works, the game's popularity means this becomes a very high-value VR project overnight.

### V Rising VR

I like this project because it does not make the usual mistake of assuming every game needs a first-person conversion.

V Rising VR keeps the top-down camera and turns the game into a stereoscopic tabletop world. No motion controls. No fake first-person sword arms. Just a perspective that respects the game that already exists.

### Inscryption VR

Still experimental and currently limited to Act 1 and Kaycee's Mod, but the cabin is practically a VR level already. If the project grows to encompass more of the game, it could become one of the most natural conversions on this page.

### The Long Dark VR

The current full-motion project is not publicly ready yet, so I am not giving it a green check. The footage and development notes are enough to make it one of the projects I most want to see released.

## What about UUVR? Can it make every Unity game VR?

No.

This is where people understandably mix up **UEVR** and **UUVR**.

UEVR is the famous Praydog framework for Unreal Engine games. It does not magically work on Unity games.

For Unity, the relevant community framework is **UUVR**. It can be useful for forcing stereoscopic VR rendering and head tracking into compatible Unity titles, and for the right third-person, exploration or tabletop-style game that can be surprisingly effective.

But I do **not** count “UUVR can potentially render this” as equivalent to a real VR mod.

A game-specific project such as NomaiVR, VHVR, LCVR, RepoXR or 7DVR can rewrite cameras, move UI into world space, bind tracked hands, change weapon aiming, add physical interactions and fix the thousand little assumptions a flat game makes about your body not existing.

Generic injection cannot know all of that automatically.

If you want to experiment with the framework, read our [UUVR guide](https://compoundvr.com/articles/uuvr-guide/). Just go in with the right expectation: **stereoscopic presence is not the same thing as a full VR conversion.**

## The biggest Unity games that still need proper VR mods

This is where the list gets fun.

If I could hand five Unity games to talented Flat2VR developers tomorrow, I would start with:

### Rust

The obvious one. The survival loop is physical enough to make sense in VR, the world is huge and the audience is enormous.

The problem is not imagination. It is multiplayer security, anti-cheat and the reality of maintaining a mod against a live service.

### Escape from Tarkov

People have spent years building VR shooters inspired by *Tarkov* because the fantasy is so obvious: physically checking a weapon, leaning around a doorway, opening a backpack under pressure.

The actual game remains a terrible injection target for all the online/anti-cheat reasons already discussed.

### Sons of the Forest

This one is cheating because SonsVR exists.

What I want is for that project — or another one — to reach the point where we can stop saying “promising alpha” and start saying “this is the unofficial VR version.”

### Genshin Impact

Teyvat would be spectacular at scale. I also have no interest in recommending people fight a live-service anti-cheat layer to get there.

### Cult of the Lamb

Not first person.

Put the little cult village on a table in front of me, let me lean down into it, keep the controller-based combat readable and turn the management side into a living diorama.

VR mods do not all need to become shooters.

## Final take

Unity's reputation has always been weirdly disconnected from the games made with it.

People talk about “Unity games” as though they share a look or a level of ambition. Then you put *Rust*, *Hollow Knight*, *Genshin Impact*, *Cities: Skylines*, *Subnautica*, *Cuphead*, *RimWorld* and *Phasmophobia* next to one another and the idea falls apart immediately.

For VR players, that diversity is the opportunity.

Unity does not have one universal equivalent of UEVR that turns the entire catalog into polished room-scale games. What it has instead is a scattered, fast-moving community of people solving individual games: NomaiVR, VHVR, LCVR, RepoXR, 7DVR, SubmersedVR, HeavenVR and a growing group of newer projects.

That approach is slower.

It is also why the best Unity VR mods are so good.

They are not just making two cameras render instead of one. They are deciding what the game should feel like when your head and hands suddenly exist inside it.

And every time one of those mods lands on a game with millions of players, the PCVR library gets something native VR almost never has the budget to build from scratch: **a huge, proven game that already has years of content behind it.**

That is the part of Flat2VR I still find exciting.

PlayStation VR2 hit shelves on February 22, 2023, at $549.99, and it did not arrive empty-handed. Sony shipped roughly 40 titles across launch day and the first weeks. This is not a backward-compatible library — nothing from the original PSVR carries over — so every name below is a game you could actually put on the headset in those first weeks. I spent the launch window in this thing, and this is the ranked list of the 20 that mattered, from the headline draws down to the deep cuts.

---

## #1: Resident Evil Village

![Resident Evil Village VR](/images/games/resident-evil-village-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2021 · Official Hybrid</div>
Resident Evil Village is the single best argument for buying a PSVR2 at launch, and Capcom handed it to you for free. The flat game shipped in 2021; the VR Mode dropped onto it on day one, turning the whole campaign into a first-person horror you play with your own hands. This is the headline third-party get of the launch — the game that makes non-believers understand why a headset matters. If you own a PS5 and like being scared in your own living room, the library starts here.

[Read the full Resident Evil Village review](/games/resident-evil-village)

## #2: No Man's Sky

![No Man's Sky VR](/images/games/no-mans-sky-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2016 · Official Hybrid</div>
No Man's Sky also arrived as a free VR update rather than a new purchase, and it's the launch's great escape hatch. Hello Games built one of the largest universes in gaming, and in VR the scale stops being a number and starts being a place. The PSVR2's OLED panel and 2000×2040-per-eye resolution make the nebulae and derelict freighters read with real depth. It's the game I'd hand someone who asks what VR is for beyond shooting things.

[Read the full No Man's Sky review](/games/no-mans-sky)

## #3: Gran Turismo 7

![Gran Turismo 7 VR](/images/games/gran-turismo-7-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native VR</div>
Gran Turismo 7 is the racing showcase, and again it cost nothing extra — the full game became fully playable in VR through a free mode at launch. Sitting in the cockpit of a GT3 car on the PSVR2's screen is the clearest proof the headset's HDR and contrast are not spec-sheet trivia. For a racing fan with a PS5, this is the title that justifies the cable running to the console.

[Read the full Gran Turismo 7 review](/games/gran-turismo-7)

---

## #4: Horizon Call of the Mountain

![Horizon Call of the Mountain](/images/games/horizon-call-of-the-mountain-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2023 · Framework Only</div>
Horizon Call of the Mountain is the game Sony built to sell the hardware, and it does exactly that job. As the flagship first-party launch title — sold both standalone and in a bundle — it's a constant tech demo: climbing, bow-and-arrow combat, and vistas that exist to show off the panel. The community's knock is fair, though: pretty, but repetitive.

[Read the full Horizon Call of the Mountain review](/games/horizon-call-of-the-mountain)

## #5: The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners - Chapter 2: Retribution

![The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners - Chapter 2: Retribution](/images/games/the-walking-dead-saints-and-sinners-chapter-2-retribution-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native VR</div>
The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners - Chapter 2: Retribution brings the most physically believable melee combat of the launch to PSVR2. The first Saints & Sinners was the gold standard for VR survival on Quest and PC, and Chapter 2 carries that weight into Sony's ecosystem. Stabbing, blocking, and scavenging all land with the tactile feedback the Sense controllers were built for. It's the deepest single-player VR action game in the early lineup.

[Read the full The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners - Chapter 2: Retribution review](/games/the-walking-dead-saints-and-sinners-chapter-2-retribution)

## #6: Moss: Book II

![Moss: Book II](/images/games/moss-book-ii-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native VR</div>
Moss: Book II is the rare port that feels handmade for the hardware. You guide the mouse Quill through storybook dioramas, and the PSVR2's headset haptics add a rumble to the world that the original never had. The puzzle-platforming is gentle, the art direction is gorgeous, and it's the most approachable thing on this list for someone who isn't sure they like VR yet.

[Read the full Moss: Book II review](/games/moss-book-ii)

## #7: Star Wars: Tales from the Galaxy's Edge - Enhanced Edition

![Star Wars: Tales from the Galaxy's Edge - Enhanced Edition](/images/games/star-wars-tales-from-the-galaxys-edge-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2020 · Native VR</div>
Star Wars: Tales from the Galaxy's Edge - Enhanced Edition is the licensed VR adventure that finally looks like the movies on Sony's panel. The Enhanced Edition polish — sharper visuals, smoother performance — sits well on the PSVR2's OLED. Lightsaber and blaster work is straightforward but satisfying. If a Star Wars fan in your house just bought the headset, this is the one they'll reach for first.

## #8: Pistol Whip

![Pistol Whip](/images/games/pistol-whip-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2019 · Native VR</div>
Pistol Whip is the rhythm shooter that turns gunplay into a music video, and the PSVR2 port makes it sharper than it's ever looked on a PlayStation. You move down rails, fire on the beat, and dodge in time with the track. It's the game you'll keep coming back to for a five-minute session that becomes an hour.

## #9: Creed: Rise to Glory

![Creed: Rise to Glory](/images/games/creed-rise-to-glory-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2019 · Native VR</div>
Creed: Rise to Glory is the boxing game that will have you sweating inside twenty minutes. The PSVR2's tracking keeps your gloves honest through jabs and hooks, and the Sense controllers' haptics make landing a clean cross feel like something connected. It's a launch-window fitness staple — the closest thing the early library offers to a workout you'll actually finish.

## #10: Beat Saber

![Beat Saber](/images/games/beat-saber-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2018 · Native VR</div>
Beat Saber is the VR rhythm game everyone already knows, and on PSVR2 it runs on the best screen the genre has ever had on a console. Slice the blocks, follow the beat, feel the haptics tick on each hit.

[Read the full Beat Saber review](/games/beat-saber)

## #11: Tetris Effect: Connected

![Tetris Effect: Connected](/images/games/tetris-effect-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2018 · Native VR</div>
Tetris Effect: Connected takes the most familiar game on Earth and wraps it in synchronized light and sound until it becomes a meditation. On the PSVR2's OLED, the zones bloom in a way flat screens flatten. Zone mode — slowing time to clear a screen — feels like a superpower when the world is closing in around your head. It's the calmest thing on this list, and sometimes that's the game you want.

[Read the full Tetris Effect: Connected review](/games/tetris-effect)

## #12: After the Fall

![After the Fall](/images/games/after-the-fall-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2021 · Native VR</div>
After the Fall was a confirmed launch-day title, and it's the lineup's left-4-dead-style co-op shooter. Four players move through a frozen, zombie-ravaged Los Angeles with cross-play support. The gunplay is solid, the set pieces are loud, and it's the most social thing you can do on PSVR2 at launch. Not the deepest, but a reliable group night.

## #13: Song in the Smoke: Rekindled

![Song in the Smoke: Rekindled](/images/games/song-in-the-smoke-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2021 · Native VR</div>
Song in the Smoke: Rekindled is the survival-crafting game that asks you to read an alien world instead of shoot it. The Rekindled edition brought it to PSVR2 with the visual bump the hardware deserves. You track beasts, craft tools, and learn the rules of a place that doesn't explain itself. It's the quiet deep cut for players who want systems over set pieces.

![Kayak VR: Mirage](/images/games/kayak-vr-mirage-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native VR</div>
Kayak VR: Mirage is the launch's pure technical showcase — a kayaking sim built to show the panel what HDR water and distant mountains look like up close. It's gorgeous, peaceful, and thin on reasons to return once you've paddled the set pieces. It's the demo you show guests, not the game you live in.

## #15: The Light Brigade

![The Light Brigade](/images/games/the-light-brigade-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2023 · Native VR</div>
The Light Brigade is the launch's roguelike, and the one that rewards patience over reflexes. Each run drops you into a WWI-style front where a single wrong step ends the loop and sends you back to learn the layout. The PSVR2's tracking and haptics make the bolt-action rifle feel deliberate.

## #16: Rez Infinite

![Rez Infinite](/images/games/rez-infinite-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2001 · Native VR</div>
Rez Infinite is Tetsuya Mizuguchi's synesthetic rail shooter, and it's the launch title that most justifies the headset as a creative instrument. The music, the visuals, and the flow of shooting through a digital body all lock together in VR in a way no flat screen reproduces. Among the ports, it's the one that feels born for this hardware rather than dragged onto it.

## #17: Before Your Eyes

![Before Your Eyes](/images/games/before-your-eyes-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">2021 · Native VR</div>
Before Your Eyes is the rare launch game built around the PSVR2's eye tracking as its actual mechanic. You advance the story by blinking — closing your eyes to move time forward — and the headset's cameras read that gesture natively. It's a short, emotional narrative piece, the closest thing the launch library has to a use of the hardware no other headset can claim.

Demeo is the digital board game that turns your living room into a tabletop dungeon. Up to four players move pieces, roll, and fight through a campaign that plays like a physical RPG session. On PSVR2 it's the most relaxed co-op option in the launch window — the game you play sitting down with friends who think they don't like VR.

Runner is the launch's fast, arcade endless-runner — the kind of pick-up-and-play reflex game that fits a five-minute break or a score-chase session. ## The takeaway

PSVR2's launch library was a strange beast: three of the best reasons to own the headset — Resident Evil Village, No Man's Sky, and Gran Turismo 7 — cost you nothing extra, while the one game built to show off the hardware, Horizon Call of the Mountain, proved the screen before it ran out of ideas. The rest was ports, rhythm games, and a deep bench of smaller experiences that aged at different speeds. Buy the headset for the free updates and the Sense controllers; build your shelf from the 20 above.

2022 is the year VR stopped pretending its scenes lived in separate worlds. The flat-to-VR mod crews shipped PC gaming's biggest names into headsets while native studios finally put out productions that match flat-game scale. I've played across both lanes all year, and the year's best is one list, not two. Free mods and paid natives, judged on the same scale. Here are the 25 VR games that earned my headset time.

---

## #1: Half-Life 2 VR Mod

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Full VR Mod</div>

The Source VR Mod Team's free HL2VR dropped on Steam Workshop on September 16, the youngest thing on this list and still the one I'd install first. I drop into City 17 in full 6DOF with the gravity gun in my actual hands, and the game that defined 2004 shooters finally plays like it was built for a headset. Ravenholm's slow dread and the Citadel's set-piece chaos hit harder when you're standing inside them.

This is a full mod, not an injection: 6DOF head tracking plus motion controls, and it covers the episodes so you get Gordon's complete arc. Combat reads clearly at close range and the vehicle sections survive better than I expected. [Read the full Half-Life 2 review](/games/half-life-2)

---

## #2: Firewatch

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Full VR Mod</div>

Raicuparta's TwoForksVR brought Firewatch into headsets and hit its 1.0 milestone on April 2. I walk the Wyoming lookout towers in full stereoscopic presence, and the quiet between Henry and Delilah lands differently when the forest is wrapped around me. The mod is a Unity rebuild, not a profile slapped on the flat game. What sells it is the stillness — Firewatch was always a walking-and-talking game, and VR gives the isolation real weight. You crane your neck at the smoke on the horizon and the mystery finally has space to breathe. [Read the full Firewatch review](/games/firewatch)

## #3: Red Matter 2

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native</div>

Red Matter 2 is the visual showcase of the year that also remembers to be a game. Vertical Robot shipped it on Quest 2 and PCVR on August 18, and the cold sci-fi stations look better in a headset than most flatscreen games I played this year.

I played it on Quest 2 and the draw distance holds. The gravity scenes and the quiet planetary vistas are the pitch, but the toolkit puzzles keep you moving. The puzzle work is real, not a tech demo with a lock on the door.

This is the title I hand a non-believer first. [Read the full Red Matter 2 review](/games/red-matter-2)

## #4: Left 4 Dead 2

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Full VR Mod</div>

sd805's l4d2vr dropped on April 24 and turned the best co-op shooter of its era into a headset panic. I'm in the middle of the horde with both hands on the shotgun, and the Valve pacing reads entirely differently when the infected are around me — the calm before a Witch, the scream of a Tank.

Full 6DOF with motion controls and working multiplayer, which is the part that matters: you can drag friends in.

The Screaming Oak level alone justifies the install. The aiming depth I couldn't fully push in a single session, but the foundation is solid and the set-piece scale survives the jump. [Read the full Left 4 Dead 2 review](/games/left-4-dead-2)

## #5: Moss: Book II

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native</div>

Polyarc's Moss: Book II is storybook platforming with a publisher's polish behind it. It landed on PSVR on March 31 and reached Quest 2 in July and PCVR in October. I guide Quill through the pages as a giant presence in her world, and the living-book framing finally makes sense when she's looking up at me.

The VR here is native throughout, built for the headset rather than ported to it. The puzzle-platforming is tight and the combat, where Quill draws her sword and you clear the path, is the right kind of cooperative. Skip the first game and you lose the setup, but this one stands alone well enough. [Read the full Moss: Book II review](/games/moss-book-ii)

## #6: The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners – Chapter 2: Retribution

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native</div>

The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners — Chapter 2: Retribution shipped on Quest 2 on December 1, and it's the AAA survival continuation the first chapter promised. I'm back in the flooded Quarter with the same weighty melee and crafting, and the zombie pressure reads as survival rather than spectacle when the bodies are in my space — all of them inside your guard. The physics-based weapon work is the reason this series matters in VR: snap a bat, jam a spear, feel the weight land.

Chapter 2 extends the loop without losing the grit. The flooded sections made me nauseous enough to keep sessions short — plan yours the same way. [Read the full TWD S&S Ch.2 review](/games/the-walking-dead-saints-and-sinners-chapter-2-retribution)

## #7: Cyberpunk 2077

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Full VR Mod</div>

Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. VR mod put Night City into headsets on February 28, and it's the most ambitious flat-to-VR injection of the year. I walk the streets of Watson in stereoscopic 3D with head-tracked 6DOF, and the scale of the city finally has the presence the flat launch promised but never delivered.

This is an AER injection mod, gamepad at release, not a motion-control rebuild. You look and move through Night City with your head while your hands stay on a controller, which caps how deep combat goes but leaves the atmosphere untouched — neon, rain, and verticality you can actually occupy. The closest VR gets to a modern open-world RPG this year. [Read the full Cyberpunk 2077 review](/games/cyberpunk-2077)

## #8: Wanderer

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native</div>

Wanderer launched on January 27 as a time-travel adventure that uses VR the right way. I move through decades, a WWII bunker then a future ruin, solving puzzles by handling objects in real space, and the chronology gimmick lands because I'm physically turning the mechanism.

Built for the headset from the ground up, and the set-pieces — a falling building, a pod race — are spectacle that earns its place.

The pacing sags in spots and the late sections are the weakest thing here, but the ambition is real and the motion-controlled interaction is the draw. For a January release to still feel like a standout in December says plenty. [Read the full Wanderer review](/games/wanderer)

## #9: Star Wars Jedi Knight II: Jedi Outcast

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · VR Port/Mod</div>

Team Beef's JKXR brought Jedi Outcast to OpenXR devices, Quest, Pico 4, and PCVR, in September 2022. The reason it ranks is the one promise VR delivers: lightsaber combat with motion controls. I draw the blade and swing it, and Kyle Katarn's arc from gunfighter to Jedi finally clicks.

The method is an OpenXR standalone port plus PCVR, with 6DOF and motion-control aiming. The blaster sections are a solid 2002 shooter wrapped around the saber work, and the force powers gain weight when you push or choke by hand — provided you don't mind a limited moveset.

How deep the saber duels go past the core swings, I haven't fully verified. [Read the full Jedi Outcast review](/games/jedi-knight-2)

## #10: Hubris

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native</div>

Hubris shipped on PC VR on December 7 as a VR-only action title with AAA aspirations. I climb, shoot, and swing through its sci-fi arenas with motion controls, and the sets and creature work sit a step above the usual native crop even where the edges show — a real spaceship to stand in beats a corridor every time.

It's native and uncompromising about the headset, but rough in the way ambitious projects are. The production leans on spaceship sets and long sightlines that flatter a headset, and the mission structure is thin once you've seen the trick. A studio swinging for something bigger than a comfort-zone demo.

[Read the full Hubris review](/games/hubris)

## #11: Green Hell VR

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native Port</div>

Incuvo's Green Hell VR landed on Quest 2 in April and PCVR on June 9. I gather, build, and starve in the Amazon with my hands on every tool, and the survival loop of wound care, food, and weather finally has the proximity that makes it tense — you can hear the insects before you can see them.

It's a rebuild of the flat game for VR rather than a reskin, and the crafting is fiddly in the right way. The jungle closing in around you does most of the work. Some players get badly sick in the first hour; push past it and the isolation is what makes this one of the year's heaviest experiences.

The flat Green Hell was a management spreadsheet; this is the panic version, and the gap is the thickness of the trees.

[Read the full Green Hell VR review](/games/green-hell)

## #12: Kayak VR: Mirage

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native</div>

Kayak VR: Mirage launched on PC VR on July 12 as the year's purest stand-inside-it experience. I paddle through Antarctica, Costa Rica, and Norway in full presence, and the water, light, and scale are the entire pitch. There's a kayak and a coastline, and nothing is shouting at you for failing — that absence is the design.

Built around presence rather than systems. No objectives, no fail state, no HUD. The best argument in VR this year for a headset being a place to go rather than a system to operate.

[Read the full Kayak VR: Mirage review](/games/kayak-vr-mirage)

## #13: Among Us VR

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native</div>

Among Us VR shipped on Quest 2 and PCVR on November 10 with crossplay, and it's social VR at its most accessible. I stand in the circular task rooms, watch crewmates move, and the paranoia of who's the impostor lands because the people are actually in the room with me — not avatars on a flat screen.

The simplification of tasks for VR works. You swipe and weld with your hands, and the meetings are chaos in the best way. Crossplay means a flatscreen friend on PC or mobile can join the same lobby, which is how most of the people I dragged in actually tried it.

[Read the full Among Us VR review](/games/among-us-3d)

## #14: F1 22

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Official Hybrid</div>

F1 22 shipped with day-one PC VR support at the flat launch on July 1, and it's the sim-racing headset case of the year. I sit in the cockpit at Spa with the wheel in my hands, and the sense of speed and proximity that flat racing loses is the entire reason to strap in.

First-party VR support inside the flatscreen sim, not a VR-only title. PC VR through SteamVR and OpenXR only, no console headset. If you already own a racing rig, this is the most complete VR motorsport you can run in 2022.

On a gamepad it's a novelty, and the VR frame budget wants a 3080-class card to stay smooth in the rain — but with a wheel it's the only motorsport that feels like racing.

[Read the full F1 22 review](/games/f1-22)

## #15: Neon White

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Full VR Mod</div>

Raicuparta's HeavenVR mod brought Neon White into headsets in September 2022. I run the afterlife's parkour-fps levels in 6DOF stereo with motion controls required, and the snappy card-gun movement translates to VR better than a game this fast had any right to.

A Unity build for headset rather than an injection, on OpenXR. The speed is the draw — you fling yourself across roofs with your hands, and the score-chase loop survives the transition intact. The pace is brutal on the stomach if you turn the comfort settings off, so treat the first session as a test, not a run. Nobody expected a card-shooter to be the year's best arcade VR run, and it is.

[Read the full Neon White review](/games/neon-white)

## #16: Wolfenstein: The New Colossus

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Full VR Mod</div>

Helifax's Vk3DVision VR mod put The New Colossus into headsets in April 2022. I fight through the Nazi occupation in Vulkan stereo injection with 6DOF head tracking and gesture-based motion-controller aiming, and the gunplay finally has the weight the flat game hinted at.

A stereo injection mod on the existing Vulkan pipeline rather than a from-scratch rebuild. The head tracking and controller aiming make B.J.'s rampage land in your space, and the result is louder and more physical than a flatscreen playthrough on a big TV — once the pipeline's tuned. Budget an afternoon for setup: the stereo image wants a decent GPU to hold 90Hz, and Helifax's config assumes you can read a settings file.

## #17: Espire 2

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native</div>

Espire 2 launched on Quest 2 on November 17 as the stealth co-op sequel that delivered. I move through its spy set-pieces with motion controls, and the sequel's big addition — a second agent, played by a friend — turns the flat stealth loop into a headset heist.

Quest 2 and Quest Pro, and the co-op is the reason it ranks above its predecessor's rough launch. The stealth reads clearly when you're the one peeking the corner, and the gadget work stays in your hands.

The first Espire stumbled out of the gate; this one sticks the landing, and the co-op heist is the rare VR stealth that works with a friend instead of despite them.

## #18: Deep Rock Galactic

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Full VR Mod</div>

The VRG (Virtual Rock Galactic) mod hit beta on May 27 and put Deep Rock's dwarven co-op into headsets. I drill, shoot, and holster weapons on my actual body, and the cave panic — a swarm pouring in from a real direction — is the flat game's best quality finally felt.

UE4, 6DOF, motion controllers, and the killer feature is cross-play: your flat friends join the same run without installing anything. It's a beta mod, so expect the occasional desync and a setup that assumes you can follow a Discord guide. The co-op shooter of the year, mod or not — beta friction and all.

## #19: Bonelab

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Native</div>

Bonelab shipped on Quest 2 and PCVR simultaneously on September 29. Stress Level Zero built a moddable physics sandbox where I grab, throw, and fight with a fidelity their previous work only pointed at, and the avatar swapping turns the body into a toy — pick a body, wear it, break it.

The mod tools are the point. This is a platform as much as a game, and the set-pieces are fun but secondary. Quest 2 players should expect early wobble in the larger spaces; the sandbox earns its keep anyway, and the mod scene will do the rest.

## #20: Resident Evil 2

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Full VR Mod</div>

Praydog's REFramework VR mod brought the RE2 Remake into headsets in 2022. I walk Raccoon City's police station in 6DOF with motion controls, and the survival-horror pressure is the remake's intent finally delivered — a zombie in your personal space, Mr. X's stride down the hall.

RE Engine via REFramework, distributed through Nexus and Patreon rather than a store, so budget time for the setup. ## #21: Ultrawings 2

Ultrawings 2 launched on Quest 2 and PCVR on February 3 as the open-world flight sequel VR needed. I pilot across its islands with motion-control sticks in my hands, and the free-flight loop — racing buoys, hauling cargo, just banking over the water — is the headset flying fantasy without a cockpit cage.

The sequel expands the map and the aircraft in ways the first game only hinted at. The controls are the draw: you physically hold the yoke. Two years on, it's still the only VR flight game where the yoke is actually in your hands and the map is worth the fuel.

## #22: Dark Souls

<div class="rank-meta">2022 · Full VR Mod</div>

Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. VR mod brought Dark Souls Remastered into headsets in June 2022. I stand in Lordran in stereoscopic 3D with head-tracked 6DOF, and the architecture — the Undead Burg's descent, Anor Londo's impossible height — finally has the scale the series is famous for.

This is a Luke Ross AER injection, so the controller stays in your hands while your head owns the camera: no motion sword swings, no room-scale dodging. What that gives you is the ritual. Souls combat is deliberate enough that gamepad-only never fights the rhythm the way it would in a twitch shooter — you're meant to be methodical, and seated is honest to that. The trade is real, but every "you died" lands in your space rather than on a screen, which is the entire reason to run the injection over the flat replay.

Setup is patreon-tier tooling and a GPU that can push 4K per eye without dropping frames; Lordran is gorgeous and hungry.

## Honorable Mentions

These didn't make the top 25 but belong in your library or your watchlist this year.

**Elden Ring** — Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. VR mod brought the Lands Between into headsets in April 2022. Gamepad injection, no motion controls, but the open world in stereoscopic 3D is the year's biggest stand-inside-it tease — and the map is five times bigger than Lordran's. It sits just below the cut because Dark Souls does the same trick on a tighter map and you already know the rituals.

**RuinsMagus** — Native anime dungeon crawler on Quest 2 and PCVR, shipped July 7. Hand-tracked spellcasting in a hand-drawn JRPG shell, and the art direction makes it the year's most stylish native Quest dungeon crawl — at 72Hz, in a headset.

**MOTHERGUNSHIP: FORGE** — Native VR-only spinoff of the flat roguelite shooter on PCVR and Quest 2, shipped June 16. You build absurd guns from parts on a workbench in your hands, then test them against waves. The weapon-crafting loop is the whole pitch.

**Resident Evil 3** — Praydog's REFramework VR mod brought the RE3 Remake into headsets in 2022. 6DOF with motion controls on RE Engine, same treatment as the RE2 mod, and the Nemesis pressure reads harder still when he's filling your personal space.

**Tentacular** — Native physics playground on Quest 2 and PCVR, shipped in 2022. You play a kraken with two enormous tentacles, smashing and stacking a cartoon world. Grabbing ships and buildings at that scale is the silly VR power fantasy the year needed, and nothing else in VR lets you do it.

**Cities: VR** — Native Quest 2 adaptation of the city-builder, shipped in 2022. The touch controls rebuild the management loop for VR rather than porting the mouse, and running a metropolis with your hands is the headset's civ-scale pick.

**Little Cities** — Native Quest 2 city-builder, shipped in 2022. A lighter, cozier take than Cities: VR. You drop districts and watch them grow in diorama scale, and the low-pressure loop is the Quest's chill sim option.

**Ultimechs** — Native VR sports-fps on Quest 2 and PCVR, shipped in 2022. Rocket-league-meets-mech-dueling with motion-controlled rocket punches, and the 1v1 arena reads as pure reflex when the ball is in your space.

**Nock** — Native VR sport on Quest 2 and PCVR, shipped in 2022. Air hockey crossed with soccer, batting a glowing orb with your racket. The physically readable ball physics are the whole game and they land cleanly.

**NFL Pro Era** — Native VR NFL experience on Quest 2 and PSVR, shipped in 2022. You stand under center and read the field in 360 with real NFL footage wrapped around you — the quarterback-eye view is the one thing flat football games never gave us.

**Dyschronia: Chronos Alternate Ep.1** — Native VR mystery adventure on Quest 2 and PCVR, shipped in 2022. A time-bending detective story with hands-on scene investigation. The first episode sets up the world without overstaying, and the tactile clue-hunting is the draw.

**Drums Rock** — Native VR rhythm game on Quest 2 and PCVR, shipped in 2022. You bash drums with both sticks against a rock tracklist. The full-body drumming is the year's most physical rhythm pick, with no guitar-band restraint.

**Tea For God** — Native roomscale VR roguelite, still an early-access build through 2022. It maps your real room into endless virtual space so you walk everywhere with your own legs, and the no-artificial-locomotion trick is the year's most honest answer to VR movement — your real legs are the whole system. Playable, half-built, and still the best demonstration of the idea.

**Zenith: The Last City** — Native VR MMO on Quest 2, PCVR, and PSVR, shipped January 27. The anime-styled open world finally put a persistent MMO in a headset. Other players actually around you is the pitch VR promised for years.

**Gunfire Reborn** — PureDark's BepInEx VR mod brought the roguelite shooter into headsets in November 2022. 6DOF with motion controls on the flat game's loop, and the gun-feel and co-op runs carry over well enough to make it the year's modded looter surprise.

---

2022's hardware choice decides where you start. PCVR owners picked up a free mod backlog this year that flat gaming never offered, while Quest owners finally got native games worth the shelf space. Cross the lanes if you can. This was the first year the two stopped feeling like separate hobbies.

There are two kinds of people who play [GORN](/games/gorn): the ones who bought it, ripped a gladiator's arm off, beat his friend with the wet end, and were completely satisfied; and the ones who then asked the obvious question — *what else can I do with this?*

The answer, for the last couple of years, has been a small but devoted modding scene. GORN doesn't ship with Steam Workshop support and never had the official mod tooling of a big studio title, so the community built its own. It started with a mod loader named MemeLoader, added a string of custom weapons, custom arenas, and quality-of-life fixes, and turned an already absurd gladiator sandbox into something genuinely unhinged.

Here are the best GORN mods, and the tools you need to run them.

## The Foundation: MemeLoader and the Weapon Extender

Before anything else, you need the loader. **MemeLoader** (via MemeLauncher) is the injection tool that makes all GORN modding possible. It drops into GORN's main assembly and lets other mods hook in — the base that virtually every weapon and mode mod in the scene requires. Install it once, and the rest of this list falls into place.

For most weapon mods you'll also want the **Meme Weapon Extender**. GORN's default weapon system is built to a specific structure, and custom weapons that add genuinely new behavior — new swing types, new damage models, weird physics toys — need the extender to hook in properly. It's a requirement for most of the weapons below, so grab it alongside the loader.

## The Custom Weapons

**The Energy Carbine** is the one weapon mod that's more self-contained than the rest — it runs without the extender, and it's the closest thing GORN has to a real ranged, sci-fi weapon. Instead of a rubbery sword, you get a proper gun that fires energy projectiles with real stopping power. It changes the entire feel of the arena, letting you plink gladiators at range instead of always closing to melee. It's the weapon mod that feels least like a GORN joke and most like a genuinely new way to play.

**Lightsaber packs** are the other essential category. The physics-driven melee already makes GORN the best-feeling "do anything" sword fighter on the market, and a lightsaber mod takes that to its natural conclusion. A proper lightsaber in GORN doesn't just reskin a sword — it slashes through limbs cleanly, dismembers on contact, and glows in the cartoon gore the way it should. It's the single most satisfying custom weapon type, and it's the one most people install first.

**Custom blunt and blade weapons** fill out the rest of the catalog — heavier mauls that make the physics engine flinch, absurd oversized swords, and single mods that add whole packs of new weapons in one drop. These are the meat of the scene. The weapon mods list is constantly changing, so browse the [GORN modding community](https://www.reddit.com/r/Gornmodding/) for the current crop.

## The Gameplay and Enemy Mods

**Spawn Config** is the quiet workhorse of the scene. GORN's arena spawner decides which enemies appear and how often, and Spawn Config gives you direct control over it. Want only brutes? Done. Want the arena flooded with the weakest fodder to set up a one-hit-kill frenzy? Done. Want to remove the enemies that annoy you entirely? Done. It's a small mod with a huge quality-of-life payoff — it's the difference between fighting the same scripted waves and actually designing the chaos you want.

**Weapon Loader** is the technical fix that unlocks magic-based weapons — the Magic Tomes and similar spell-casting weapons that don't work in the base game. The magic tomes in GORN are otherwise inert, a flashy idea with no actual effect. This mod makes them function, and it opens up a whole category of "cast spells in the arena" gameplay that the base game never really supported.

## What Not to Expect

A few honest caveats. GORN's mod scene is active and beloved, but it's never been as big as the modding communities around flat-screen games or the official-modding VR titles. The 2020 loadout is a small but growing catalog of weapons, an enemy config, and a handful of modes — not the sprawling thousands-of-mods ecosystem you'd find on a big AAA flat game. Most mods require MemeLoader and the extender, and the loader's tooling can be finicky on game updates.

GORN's mod scene also doesn't change the core game's shape. You're not getting new campaigns, full level packs, or story expansions. What you get is a better, weirder, more varied arena sandbox: new ways to murder, new weapons to test the physics engine with, and new control over the enemies that stand between you and the crowd's approval.

## Is It Worth It?

If you've already torn through GORN's campaign and want more, absolutely. A modded GORN is a materially better game for it. The custom weapons make a nonsense arena feel fresher, the enemy config gives you real control over the wave chaos, and the whole thing is built on an installer that, once you fight it into submission, quietly does its job.

GORN mods won't turn the gladiator pit into a different game. They'll turn it into the most unhinged, physics-driven version of itself — and for a game built on physics-driven, slapstick violence, that's exactly the right kind of modding.

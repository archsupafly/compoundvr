The Quest 1 was a $399 brick with a Snapdragon 835, a 72Hz OLED, and 1440×1600 per eye. It shipped in May 2019 with 53 games and an audience that wasn't sure standalone VR was more than a gimmick. Eighteen months later Meta shipped the Quest 2 and moved on. The Quest 1 kept getting games anyway.

By the time support started thinning, the library had grown to include survival horror, rhythm games, puzzle boxes, MMOs, and a sideloading scene that brought Doom, Half-Life, and Wolfenstein to a headset that fit in a backpack. None of it required a PC. None of it required a wire. You just put it on and played.

This is the 50 games that earned their place. Not every good Quest game — just the ones that justified the hardware. The store exclusives that sold headsets, the sideloaded ports that proved the community could do what Meta wouldn't, and the cross-platform titles that ran fine on hardware everyone said was too weak.

## #1: Beat Saber

![Beat Saber](/images/games/beat-saber-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">May 21, 2019 · Native VR</div>

Two sabers, one beat map, and the simplest reason to own a headset. Blocks fly at you and you slice them on the downbeat, and your body learns the language faster than your brain does. The custom-song scene keeps the library endless, and after twenty minutes your shirt is soaked. I still hand newcomers Beat Saber first, because nothing else explains the appeal in ten seconds flat.

## #2: The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners

![The Walking Dead: Saints & Sinners](/images/games/the-walking-dead-saints-and-sinners-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">Oct 13, 2020 · Native VR</div>

Survival horror that sweats. You scavenge a flooded New Orleans, craft a shiv, and decide whether the stranger in the stairwell deserves your last bandage. Melee has actual weight, so a fire axe to the skull is a commitment, not a tap. The stamina and the backpack force you to ration every run. That moment when a walker groans behind you and you have to physically turn is the whole game, and it works.

## #3: Moss

![Moss](/images/games/moss-vr-hero.jpg)

<div class="rank-meta">May 21, 2019 · Native VR</div>

Quill does the fighting; you do the reaching. You hover above a storybook diorama as the reader, moving blocks and yanking levers while a tiny mouse knight swings her sword below. That distance between guiding and watching gives the partnership its warmth. Combat is light and the puzzles are gentle, but the scale tricks and the mouse-sized boss fights are clever. Short, tender, and the rare VR story I finished in one sitting.

## #50: Onward

<div class="rank-meta">Jul 30, 2020 · Native VR</div>

Military tactical shooting before anyone else did it this seriously. You move with a team, communicate on a radio, and one bullet ends the round, which makes every corner a decision. ## Honorable Mentions

**Rec Room** — The social playground that proved VR could be a hangout space. Build, play, chat — Rec Room is less a game than a place, and on Quest 1 it was where a generation of VR users made their first friends. See [Rec Room](/games/rec-room).

**Echo VR** — Zero-g multiplayer arena that was essential during the Quest 1's life. The servers are gone now, but for a window in time, Echo VR was the most futuristic multiplayer experience in VR. See [Lone Echo](/games/lone-echo).

**Zenith: The Last City** — The biggest new VR MMO of early 2022. Ramen VR built a world that justified the headset as a social platform, with crafting, combat, and a community that kept players coming back. Quest 1 support ended in September 2023.

**Swarm** — Spider-man meets geometry wars. A 4.83-rated arena shooter that combined grapple-based movement with bullet-hell combat, proving the Quest 1 could handle fast-paced action without compromising on feel.

**QuakeQuest** — Team Beef's native Quake port. The fast-paced, run-and-gun gameplay translates beautifully to VR, and the mod support makes it infinite. See [Quake VR](/games/quake).

**QuestZDoom** — Classic Doom with full mod support. The Quest becomes a portable Doom machine, running thousands of community-made WADs at full speed. See [Doom](/games/doom).

**Contractors** — The military FPS with mod support that made it the closest thing to Call of Duty in VR. The multiplayer scene was strong throughout the Quest 1's life. See [Contractors VR](/games/contractors).

**The Climb** — Crytek's vertigo simulator, and the game that convinced a lot of people a virtual ledge could genuinely frighten them. The Climb 2 later did it better, but the tension started here. See [The Climb](/games/the-climb).

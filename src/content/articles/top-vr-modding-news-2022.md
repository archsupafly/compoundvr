The first flat-to-VR story of 2022 landed on January 1, when praydog published the first VR-capable REFramework build and put four Resident Evil games into headsets before most people had finished their holiday leftovers. That single release set the rhythm for the entire year: one person, one framework, AAA games running in VR before the studios that owned them had a chance to object.

By December, the scene looked nothing like a hobby. Luke Ross shipped four of the biggest games on the planet — Cyberpunk 2077, Elden Ring, Dark Souls: Remastered, Final Fantasy VII Remake Intergrade — through his Patreon-backed R.E.A.L. framework. A July profile in The Verge reported he was making $20,000 a month. Six days after publication, Take-Two DMCA'd his Grand Theft Auto V and Red Dead Redemption 2 mods. Real money, real lawyers, real consequences.

[Half-Life 2 VR](https://compoundvr.com/games/half-life-2/) — the community project that had been in development for nearly eight years — finally launched its public beta on Steam on September 16, free, with full motion controls. The same month, praydog revealed UEVR, a universal Unreal Engine VR injector, and Flat2VR went public as the scene's central hub, with RoadToVR launching its first "VR Modding Monthly" column to track it. Ghost Ship Games worked with a modder to add VR support plugins to Deep Rock Galactic. Team Beef put Jedi Knight II on a $300 standalone headset.

By December, flat-to-VR modding was paying rent, drawing legal attention, shipping on Steam, and organizing itself like an industry. 2021's edition argued the mods set the agenda. 2022 sent the invoice.

Here are the flat-to-VR modding stories that mattered in 2022.

## 1. REFramework Drops in January — Four Resident Evils in VR

The year opened with a shock. On January 1, 2022, praydog published the first VR-capable build of [REFramework](https://compoundvr.com/articles/reframework-vr-guide/) — his mod loader and scripting platform for Capcom's RE Engine — and suddenly [Resident Evil 2](https://compoundvr.com/games/resident-evil-2-2019/), [Resident Evil 3](https://compoundvr.com/games/resident-evil-3/), [Resident Evil 7](https://compoundvr.com/games/resident-evil-7/), and Resident Evil Village were all playable in PCVR. Full stereoscopic 6DOF, with motion-controller aiming arriving within months (v1.2 landed in May with full 6DOF motion controls and OpenXR).

This was the first time modern AAA horror was fully playable in a headset, and it set the tone for everything that followed. The press cycle ran for a week straight — Wccftech, TechSpot, DSOGaming all covered it in the first days of January. The message was unmistakable: 2022 was going to be the year of the AAA conversion, and it started on day one.

REFramework kept growing all year (RE4 and the rest would follow), but January was the detonation. Everything else on this list happens in its wake.

## 2. Luke Ross Ships Four AAA Games — Then the Money and the Lawyers Arrive

No single person defined 2022 modding like Luke Ross. His Patreon-only R.E.A.L. framework converted four of the biggest games in the world to VR in a single year: Cyberpunk 2077 in February, Elden Ring in May (three months after launch), [Dark Souls: Remastered](https://compoundvr.com/games/dark-souls/) in June, and Final Fantasy VII Remake Intergrade in late August. Our [Luke Ross R.E.A.L. VR guide](https://compoundvr.com/articles/luke-ross-real-vr-guide/) covers the full catalog. One guy out-shipped the entire VR industry's AAA output.

Then July happened. On July 1, The Verge published Adi Robertson's profile — "Meet the modder making $20,000 a month bringing the biggest games to VR" — reporting Ross earned $20k+ a month from Patreon backers, roughly $150,000 total at the time. (MIXED had run the $10,000-a-month version of the story in April. The income doubled in three months.) It was the first mainstream validation that flat-to-VR modding was a real business, not a hobby.

Six days later, Take-Two DMCA'd his Grand Theft Auto V and Red Dead Redemption 2 mods off the internet.

That two-punch week is the whole 2022 story in miniature: the money arrived, and then the lawyers arrived. Modding had become important enough to be worth both paying for and shutting down.

## 3. Deep Rock Galactic Gets the Dev-Blessed Treatment

The open beta of the Deep Rock Galactic VR mod ("VRG," by Nicolas Aubinet) launched May 27, 2022, with real 6DOF tracking and full motion-controller support — holsters, gestures, hand-aiming — and full co-op compatibility.

What makes it a 2022 story isn't just the quality. It's the cooperation. Ghost Ship Games added the VR plugins to the shipping Unreal Engine build at the modder's request, making DRG the template for the "dev-blessed" motion-control conversion: a studio that didn't build VR support itself, but cleared the path for someone who would.

Most 2022 conversions happened despite the rights-holders. This one happened with them. That's the model worth remembering.

## 4. Raicuparta's Speedrun Year — Neon White in Six Weeks

Rui "Raicuparta" Miguel spent 2022 proving that a shared open-source Unity VR toolkit could convert a brand-new hit in weeks. The run: Firewatch VR completed in April, The Stanley Parable: Ultra Deluxe VR in May, Hardspace: Shipbreaker VR in June — and Neon White VR in late July, roughly six weeks after the game's June 16 launch. Eurogamer covered it in September: "the perfect 'just one more go' VR game."

The framework was the story, not any single mod. Camera-reparent utilities, a VR patcher, an installer — reusable tooling that turned each conversion into a faster repeat of the last. It's the speedrun model: mod days after launch, ship through a manager, move to the next game. That model would later scale into UUVR and Rai Pal, and eventually into official ports.

2022 was the year the toolkit beat the one-off.

## 5. Half-Life 2 VR Finally Launches on Steam

After nearly eight years in development — greenlit in 2017, practically abandoned, then revived by new blood — the [Half-Life 2 VR mod](https://compoundvr.com/games/half-life-2/) launched its public beta on Steam on September 16, 2022. Free, full motion controls, the 2004 classic standing up in a headset at last.

It was the year's biggest single flat-to-VR release and the emotional center of the September crush. The community had been waiting the better part of a decade to stand on the Borealis docks. When it finally happened, it happened on Steam, with Valve's implicit blessing, for free.

Some mods launch. This one arrived.

## 6. UEVR Announced — The "Soon" Story of the Fall

In September, praydog revealed UEVR: a universal Unreal Engine VR injector — a drop-in DLL set that restores engine-native VR to UE4 and UE5 games and auto-fixes the usual flat-to-VR breakage (UI, camera). The GitHub repo went up September 1; by September 19 the Flat2VR crew was demoing games booting in VR through it; RoadToVR's modding column called it the month's biggest breakthrough.

It didn't launch in 2022 — the public beta wouldn't ship until January 2024. That doesn't matter for this list. UEVR's announcement was the "soon" story that defined fall coverage: the promise that thousands of Unreal games could become VR-playable turned every UE release into a potential VR title overnight. Our [UEVR guide](https://compoundvr.com/articles/uevr-guide/) covers what it became.

2022 was the year the future of flat-to-VR got a name.

## 7. Flat2VR Goes Public — The Scene Gets a Headquarters

September again: Flat2VR launched publicly as the central Discord hub and press home for flatscreen-to-VR modding, with RoadToVR's first-ever "VR Modding Monthly" column debuting September 1 as its press beat. UEVR experiments, Raicuparta's Unity conversions, Team Beef ports — all of it under one roof, with a monthly column tracking the scene.

The infrastructure mostly predated the brand. What changed in September 2022 was the going-public: scattered Discords and solo modders got a name, a headquarters, and a regular press cycle. That's the institutional moment — the month "mods" became a "movement" with a front door.

Every scene in this article passed through that door within a year.

## 8. Team Beef Takes Jedi Knight to Quest

While the PC modders chased AAA, Team Beef owned the other end of the spectrum. In the September–October window, [Jedi Knight II: Jedi Outcast](https://compoundvr.com/games/jedi-knight-2/) entered Patreon early access for Quest 2 (October 10), with [Jedi Academy](https://compoundvr.com/games/jedi-academy/) announced as the next port weeks earlier. Decades-old classics, running natively on a standalone headset, swinging a lightsaber with motion controls.

It's the Quest side of the 2022 story, and it matters for reach: flat-to-VR wasn't just for expensive PCVR rigs. A $300 standalone headset could put you inside Kyle Katarn's boots. (The full free Outcast release followed in April 2023 — outside this year's scope, but proof the model worked.)

## 9. PSVR2 Goes Real at CES

January's other shoe: Sony used CES to give the PlayStation VR2 its name, its specs, and its flagship — Horizon Call of the Mountain. (Our 2021 edition covered the 2021 commitment; this was the hardware going real.)

It belongs on a modding list for one reason: every official VR commitment raises the stakes for the modders. PSVR2 meant Sony was serious about VR's second generation, which meant the flat-to-VR scene was converting games for a market that was about to get much bigger. The platforms build the audience; the mods fill it.

## 10. October: High-End VR Gets Serious

October was the month high-end VR stopped apologizing. Meta launched the Quest Pro at $1,500 — pancake lenses, face and eye tracking, an enterprise-priced preview of where mainstream headsets were headed. Pico 4 launched across Europe and Asia as the first credible Quest competitor, with ByteDance's weight behind it. And NVIDIA's RTX 4090 landed October 12, instantly becoming the GPU that heavy VR games and ambitious mods were built around.

None of these are modding stories directly. All of them are the ground the modding stories stand on: better lenses to see converted worlds through, real standalone competition, and the raw horsepower to brute-force unoptimized conversions into smooth framerates. The mods provide the destinations; October 2022 upgraded the vehicles.

## Honorable Mentions

**Half-Life: Alyx: Levitation + the Alyx mod scene.** November's Levitation campaign was the biggest [Half-Life: Alyx](https://compoundvr.com/games/half-life-alyx/) mod to date, atop a growing scene of custom campaigns and Gunman Contracts-era experiments. Scope note: these are mods *for* a VR game, not flat-to-VR — which is why they're here and not numbered. The distinction matters, and the scene deserves the nod.

**NomaiVR's quiet upkeep year.** No 2022 launch, no milestone — the [Outer Wilds](https://compoundvr.com/games/outer-wilds/) conversion shipped in 2020. But Raicuparta's team kept the gold-standard Unity conversion alive all year, including Game Pass support in January and compatibility patches through October. The best flat-to-VR experience many players know needed no 2022 release. It just kept working.

## The Year Modding Went Pro

Look at the shape of 2022: a framework in January, four AAA conversions by one man, twenty grand a month, a DMCA, a dev-blessed co-op shooter, a speedrun toolkit, an eight-year project shipping free on Steam, a universal injector announced, a community headquarters opened, Jedi Knight on a standalone headset — and the hardware to run all of it getting serious in October.

2021's edition argued the mods set the agenda. 2022 proved it, then sent the invoice: mainstream money, mainstream lawyers, mainstream press columns, mainstream hardware. The scene that spent the 2010s begging for scraps spent 2022 acting like an industry. Because it had become one.

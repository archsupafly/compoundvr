I've lost count of how many times Peppy told me to do a barrel roll. But the first time he said it while I was physically craning my neck to watch a laser zip past the canopy? That landed different.

## What you're actually installing

There's no Nintendo-made VR edition of Star Fox 64 — never was. What exists is a community mod, and it's a good one. RaYRoD TV built StarFox64-VR on top of Starship, the community PC port of the N64 original. You don't compile anything or chase dependency lists. You grab the free Multiverse VR Hub, a small Windows app that pulls the official Starship release, applies the VR patch, and keeps itself updated. You bring your own ROM, which assumes you already own a copy. That's the whole setup: hub, ROM, headset, go.

What you get is the real thing, not a flat screen floating in a virtual living room. Per-eye stereo. A HUD that sits out in the world instead of painted on glass. Motion controllers. And five ways to frame it: first-person, third-person, diorama, theater, and the one that matters — cockpit view.

## Cockpit view is the whole point

Cockpit view is where this mod earns its keep. You're in the Arwing. The dashboard sits low in your lap, the canopy frame edges your vision, and the battlefield opens up in front of you in genuine 3D depth. When a squadron banks in from the left, your instinct is to turn your head, and the world turns with you — because it's actually rendered around you now, not faked with a widescreen crop.

Aiming uses the motion controllers. You point where you want the reticle, the lasers follow, and the Arwing's roll and pitch respond as you sweep the sticks. It isn't a flight-sim scheme — this is still the 1997 on-rails shooter where the path is fixed and your job is to shoot, dodge, and keep your wingmates alive. But the head-tracked look-around changes the texture of every dogfight. In the flat version, a bogey coming up your six is a tiny sprite in the corner. In here, you whip your head around and there's an actual ship filling your view, guns blazing.

Seated and on-rails, there's no locomotion to fight, so comfort stays a non-issue — you're strapped into the cockpit, not roaming. Performance is a joke in the best way: this is a 1997 game running in VR, so any VR-ready PC eats it for breakfast and holds 90Hz without breaking a sweat.

The opening run over Corneria — ducking under the searchlights, watching the water and cliffs fall away beneath you — reads completely differently when the ground is actually below your feet. And the series' signature forked paths mean you're not just riding one rail; you're peeling off from formation and discovering which way your squad bails. In VR, picking a branch feels like actually breaking away from the flight.

## Where it sings

The presence is the sale. Star Fox 64 always had personality — Falco's sneer, Slippy's panic, the ridiculous "good line" banter — but it played like a movie you shot. Putting your face in the cockpit makes the movie feel like your movie. The trench runs on Venom, the asteroid fields, the boss ships rearing up in front of you: the spatial read you get from real depth makes the chaos legible in a way the original never managed.

Five views is a genuinely generous touch. Cockpit is the keeper, but third-person gives you that toy-ship-on-a-string nostalgia hit, and diorama or theater are fun novelty angles for showing the game to someone on a flatscreen. The whole thing is free, the hub handles updates, and the entire campaign is playable — this isn't a tech demo of the first level.

## The honest catch

Here's the thing: it's still Star Fox 64. The rails are the rails. You fly the same paths, fight the same waves, and the whole campaign wraps in a single sitting. The VR doesn't add depth — it adds spectacle and a cockpit to sit in. If you go in expecting a remade game, you'll be disappointed; if you go in wanting to finally be inside the Arwing, you'll get exactly that.

The mod is one person's project, which cuts both ways. RaYRoD TV is clearly active and shipping, but a single-developer hobby port means support is whatever he has time for. And it's PCVR only for this title right now — no standalone Quest build yet, though he's porting other entries in the series to Quest, so Quest-only players are either waiting or falling back to emulation (the 3DS version in a Citra VR fork or a virtual arcade screen via EmuVR — those are gamepad virtual-screen experiences, not this per-eye mod).

One more practical note: you need your own ROM, and the hub fetches the PC port rather than shipping anything itself. That's standard for this class of decompilation project, but it does mean the "free" experience assumes you bought the game sometime in the last thirty years.

## Sitting inside the Arwing beats watching it

If you loved Star Fox 64, this is the version you always filed under "someday." The cockpit view alone is worth the twenty minutes it takes to point the hub at your ROM, and the dogfights hit harder when the lasers are actually streaking past your ears. It's a short, on-rails throwback, not a reinvention — but as throwbacks go, being in the cockpit clears watching it from the couch every time.

I've lost more weekends than I'll admit to Horizon Chase Turbo. There's a reason: it takes the clean, sundrenched rush of Out Run and Top Gear, strips out the simulation homework, and just lets you chase the horizon with a grin. So when a community mod promised to put that exact feeling inside a headset, I had to know if the magic survived the jump to 3D.

## What the mod actually is

The HorizonChaseTurboVR mod by Astienth doesn't reinvent the game. It takes Aquiris's 2018 arcade racer — already a love letter to 16-bit racing — and renders the whole thing in stereoscopic 3D inside your headset. Flat-screen game logic and controls stay exactly as they were. You're still holding a gamepad; the mod just gives the world real depth.

It's a BepInEx-based Unity mod, which means it slots into the game's install rather than replacing it. Distribution is Discord-gated: the mod lives on the FarmerTrueVR server, not a public store page, and you grab one of two ZIPs depending on which store sold you the game — an x86 build for Steam, an x64 build for the Epic copy. Get that match wrong and it won't hook in, so the version you pick matters. A community installer hub walks the rest: join, accept the rules, pick your store, and it auto-locates the install. It defaults to OpenVR but flips to OpenXR with a one-line config edit if your rig prefers it. No ViGEmBus, no controller-emulation layer — just the game, rendered in VR.

Comfort is a non-issue for the usual reason racing games dodge it: this is a third-person chase cam, so you're watching the car from behind rather than riding a motion-sim cockpit, which keeps the stomach settled through the high-speed stuff.

## What it actually feels like

Boot into a race and the first thing that lands is the depth. Horizon Chase Turbo was always a flat, bright, poster of a game — cars and tracks laid out like a magazine spread. In stereo 3D, the track actually falls away from you. Corners you used to read as a 2D bend become real distances, and braking points that were guesswork on a monitor turn into something your eyes can measure. The upgrade is quiet but real: the mod doesn't add systems, it adds spatial truth. You stop watching a racing game and you watch your machine carve a 3D track from just behind it.

The base game's charm does the heavy lifting. Barry Leitch's soundtrack — the same composer behind Top Gear's SNES tunes — pumps out synth-and-guitar hooks that feel better with the bass wrapping your ears in headphones. The tracks are neon-soaked, palm-lined, sunset-everything, and they were built to be admired at speed. In VR you finally get the scale they were always hinting at. ## Where it stops short

There are no motion controls, no cockpit presence, no rebuilt systems. You're not sitting inside a touring car feeling the wheel — the camera still rides behind the car, exactly like the flat game, and you steer with the same gamepad. That's the honest pitch: the mod is a stereoscopic 3D renderer bolted onto a flat game, not a ground-up VR reimagining. The README states it plainly, and it's the right call for a chase-cam arcade game where the fun was never about inhabiting the driver's seat.

The docs note some of the menu screens glitch in the headset — text and panels that render fine on a monitor occasionally misbehave in stereo. It never touched the actual racing when I ran through it, but expect to do your menu work with one eye on the flat screen or accept the occasional garbled options page. That's the trade for a mod that took the shortest honest path to "playable in VR."

Setup is the other honest wrinkle. The Discord gate means you can't just click install — you join a server, accept the rules, and pick the right store ZIP. It's not hard, but it's a step further than a one-click download, and the x86-versus-x64 match is the kind of detail that bites if you rush it. Once it's in, though, the thing is stable and quiet: no dependencies to babysit, no controller layer to configure.

## How hard does it push your PC?

Not hard. Horizon Chase Turbo is a Unity game from 2018 with art that's bright but lightweight — low-poly cars, clean tracks, no ray-traced anything. Rendering it in stereo is a modest lift on top of a title that already ran on a potato. A mid-range PC will hold 90Hz without breaking a sweat, and even budget hardware should keep it smooth. The mod doesn't add graphical heft, so the performance story is the same as the flat game plus a small stereo tax. If your rig runs the base game, it runs this.

## The bottom line

Here's who this is for: arcade-racing fans who already own Horizon Chase Turbo and want to watch their favorite tracks gain real depth without surrendering the gamepad feel they love. It's not a simulation, it won't put you in the driver's seat, and the menu glitches are a small tax on an otherwise clean conversion. But the stereoscopic jump tightens your read on corners and braking points in a way the flat version never could, and the Barry Leitch score has never sounded better than it does wrapped around your head.

If you've never played Horizon Chase Turbo, buy the flat game first — the VR mod is a great reason to revisit it, not a reason to discover it. And if you're holding out for an official VR mode or a motion-control rebuild, don't wait on it; this community mod is the ceiling, and for a chase-cam arcade racer, it's a good one.

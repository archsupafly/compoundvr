I opened the game inside the headset expecting a tech demo with a Nazi hat on it. What I got was the entire campaign of The New Colossus — the wheelchair intro, the submerged submarine base, the slow build to BJ tearing across an occupied America — rendered in stereo 3D with my head actually inside the room. MachineGames never shipped a VR mode for this game. Helifax did, through his Vk3DVision Vulkan driver, and it's a full VR mod, not a profile that paints a flat frame onto a floating rectangle.

## Helifax's Vk3DVision mod is the only way in

The New Colossus has no official VR support. The only VR title Bethesda put out for the franchise is Wolfenstein: Cyberpilot, a separate 2019 spin-off where you pilot a mech — not this game, not BJ's story. The real way to play The New Colossus in VR is Helifax's community mod. Helifax is the same person who did 3D-conversion work on Doom Eternal, and this was his first full VR mod: a Vulkan stereo driver that hooks the renderer, splits the frame for the HMD, and adds 6DOF head tracking plus motion-controller aiming.

It is not a UEVR profile — the game runs on id Tech 6, not Unreal, so the UEVR injector can't touch it. There's no VorpX path either; the forums show users couldn't even find a working cloud profile for it. This is one way to play it, and it's the mod.

## Aiming with a gesture, not a modeled rifle

The aiming model is the part you need to understand before you install anything. Rather than full 1:1 tracked hands or a pure gamepad scheme, the mod uses a gesture system: your right motion controller stands in for the right thumbstick. You aim by moving the controller, and your head owns the full 6DOF — look around, lean, peek. It's physical without pretending to be a light-gun sim. After ten minutes my brain stopped caring that I wasn't holding a modeled rifle and started caring that I was sighting down a scope by tilting my head. It's an intense ride — fast, head-tracked gunplay with no comfort hand-holding — and the opening sequence on the resistance base will tell you quick whether your stomach signed up for this.

Performance is the other wall. Pushing id Tech 6 into stereo 3D at a steady 90Hz is heavy work. You want a high-end PC, not a potato, and even then you'll be managing settings. When it holds framerate, the world locks in — the steel corridors of the Eva's Hammer, the ruined Manhattan skyline — and the stereo depth on the wide exterior shots is the kind of thing flat screens flatten into nothing.

## The campaign is the entire reason to do this

The campaign is the whole point, and it's all here. This is one of the best shooters of the 2010s: a story with actual teeth, characters you remember (Grace Walker, Anya, the Kreisau Circle), and set pieces that escalate from a wheelchair gunfight to leveling a fascist broadcast tower. In the headset, the scale of those moments lands differently. Standing in the command deck while the sub surges through the water, or walking a devastated Washington with BJ's internal narration in your ears — that's presence the flat version can't hand you. The gunplay feels right: weighty, loud, and the head-tracked aiming makes clearing a room feel like you're actually clearing it.

The mod is also remarkably complete for community work. Full game, full story, no "first two levels then it breaks." Helifax keeps the driver on GitHub with an installer and a tutorial video, so you're not compiling anything by hand.

## The setup tax and the aiming seam

Setup is the honest catch. This isn't a one-click Steam release. You need an owned PC copy of the game, you install the Vk3DVision driver, and you run the mod through Helifax's tooling. If you've never touched a Vulkan stereo driver before, budget an afternoon and read the guide twice. It's advanced, not expert — there's an installer — but it is not beginner territory.

The gesture aiming is the other trade. It works, and it feels better than I expected, but the aiming is gesture-driven motion control rather than 1:1 native tracked hands — you move the controller to aim, the game reads it as stick input. You won't holster a pistol or pump a shotgun with your hands; you're emulating a stick with a controller. For a game this kinetic, that abstraction is the one thing keeping it from feeling native. You adapt fast, but the seam is always there.

If you already love this game, the mod is the best excuse to replay it you'll find — being inside BJ's war beats watching it. If you've got a PCVR rig, the patience for a driver install, and you can handle a fast shooter, this is a genuine way to spend a weekend. It's PCVR-only and it'll test your VR legs, but that's the reality of the mod, not a flaw in the game. Skip it if you want pick-up-and-play or native motion controls; the gesture aiming and setup friction will wear on you.

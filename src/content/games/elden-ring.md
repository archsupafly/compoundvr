I stepped out of the Stranded Graveyard and into Limgrave, and for the first time in a hundred hours with this game, I was standing in it instead of watching a puppet from behind.

There's a tree. A big one. And I'm looking up at it with my own head while my thumbs rest on a gamepad in my lap, and a demigod's army somewhere past the fog. Elden Ring was always a world I admired from a polite third-person distance. VR takes half that distance away — the scale half. The hands half is still coming.

Two mods get you here, and they are different sports. Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. VR framework — out since May 2022, maintained ever since, free now — renders the game in stereoscopic 3D with head-look aiming and keeps the original gamepad controls. That is the stable way in: the whole game, Erdtree included, playable start to finish if your rig and stomach hold up. The second is Ilya's motion-controls build, first-person with real 6DOF swings, blocks and parries, and a floating HUD that works. It is alpha-grade, shown running on a short list of rigs, the kind of thing you watch as much as you install.

## How you actually get in

PCVR only, either way — through SteamVR. On the R.E.A.L. route you grab the framework release from Luke Ross's GitHub, launch Elden Ring with Easy Anti-Cheat disabled (offline mode, standard practice for any Elden Ring mod and the step that trips up first-timers), tune the stereo to your eyes, and play with a gamepad. On the Ilya route you find the listing on Nexus, read every install note twice, and check the Flat2VR Discord for your headset. Expect version-matching fuss either way. Quest 3 over SteamVR is the documented working client, and there is no standalone Quest build.

## What it feels like to fight

Fighting through the stable mod feels like the flat game with your head glued to the Tarnished — lock-on still does the spatial work, your thumbs do the killing, and your neck supplies the vertigo. Gaze-aimed everything takes about an hour to stop feeling wrong, and then the scale starts paying rent: bosses that were set pieces become architecture you are standing under. The third-person Souls formula does not translate gently. A pack of soldiers that was a manageable blob on a TV becomes a genuinely disorienting wall of threats when they are swinging at your face, and VR channels have titled their footage around the game being harder this way. You trade the tactical overview for being a person on the ground.

The alpha is a different sport. Swinging a greatsword with a full arm motion, catching a blow on a raised shield, answering with a parry you timed with your own hands. The motion input reads cleanly, 6DOF rather than waggle. When it lands, the Lands Between stops being a game you are good at and becomes a place you are surviving. When it doesn't, you fall back to the stable path.

## What VR adds to the world

Elden Ring's world is exceptional — the architecture, the silence between fights, the way a ruin implies a thousand years of story you will never be told. Put that world in first person with your head actually in it and you get the best argument either mod has going. I have walked past the same cliff in flat mode a dozen times. In VR I stopped and looked, because the scale finally meant something. A great world with a great game under it, made physically present, is worth the faff for the right player.

The scale is genuine on both paths. The R.E.A.L. build covers the whole game including Erdtree, stable and finishable. The alpha's motion melee is the real thing where it runs — decoupled hands, working menus, open world and legacy dungeons alike.

The honest catches: the stable path is gamepad-only, so your hands never leave your lap and presence stops at the neck. It is performance-hungry; the flagship footage I have seen was running on a 4090, and dense areas with crowds or particle-heavy bosses will punish a mid-range rig. Setup is advanced on both paths — the EAC bypass, SteamVR streaming if you are on a Quest, careful version matching. The alpha is alpha, so stability varies by machine, crashes happen, and you should back up your saves. None of that is a reason to stay away if you are already a Tarnished. It is the price of admission, and you should walk in knowing it.

## Who this is for

If you already love Elden Ring and you have a PCVR headset, the R.E.A.L. mod is a finished, free way to stand inside the Lands Between — gamepad and all. If you are the tinkering kind, the motion-controls alpha is the most interesting thing happening in flat-to-VR melee right now. Follow it, maybe test it, do not marry it yet. If you have never touched a FromSoftware game, start flat; neither mod teaches you the dance. And if motion sickness is your constant companion, the intensity here will wear you out fast.

Gamepad first, sword second.

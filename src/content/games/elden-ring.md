I stepped out of the Stranded Graveyard and into Limgrave, and for the first time in a hundred hours with this game, I was standing in it instead of watching a puppet from behind.

There's a tree. A big one. And I'm looking up at it with my own head while my thumbs rest on a gamepad in my lap, and a demigod's army somewhere past the fog. Elden Ring was always a world I admired from a polite third-person distance. VR deletes half that distance — the scale half. The hands half is still coming.

Two mods get you here, and they're different sports. Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. VR framework — out since May 2022, maintained ever since, free now — renders the game in stereoscopic 3D with head-look aiming and keeps the original gamepad controls. It's the stable way in: the whole game, Erdtree included, playable start to finish if your rig and stomach hold up. The second is Ilya's motion-controls build — first-person, real 6DOF swings, blocks, and parries, a floating HUD that works — and it's alpha-grade, shown running on a short list of rigs, the kind of thing you watch as much as install.

## How you actually get in

It's PCVR-only either way, running through SteamVR. The R.E.A.L. route: grab the framework release from Luke Ross's GitHub, launch Elden Ring with Easy Anti-Cheat disabled (offline mode — standard practice for any Elden Ring mod and the step that trips up first-timers), tune the stereo to your eyes, and play with a gamepad. The Ilya route: find the mod listing on Nexus, read every install note twice, check the Flat2VR Discord for your headset, and expect version-matching fuss — Quest 3 over SteamVR is the documented working client, and there's no standalone Quest build on either path.

## What it feels like to fight

Fighting through the stable mod feels like the flat game with your head glued to the Tarnished — lock-on still does the spatial work, your thumbs do the killing, and your neck supplies the vertigo. Gaze-aimed everything takes an hour to stop feeling wrong, and then the scale starts paying rent: bosses that were set pieces become architecture you're standing under. The third-person Souls formula does not translate gently — a pack of soldiers that was a manageable blob on a TV becomes a genuinely disorienting wall of threats when they're swinging at your face, and more than one VR channel has titled its footage around the game being harder this way. They're not wrong. You trade the tactical overview for being a person on the ground.

The alpha is a different sport. Swinging a greatsword with a full arm motion, then catching a blow on a raised shield and answering with a parry you timed with your own hands — that's not a reskin of the flat game, it's a different game. The motion input reads cleanly, 6DOF not waggle. When it lands, the Lands Between stops being a game you're good at and becomes a place you're surviving. When it doesn't — alpha-grade stability, the occasional rough edge — you fall back to the stable path, which is exactly why this article leads with it.

## The world is the point

Here's where the rating stops being about any mod's rough edges. Elden Ring's world is exceptional — the architecture, the silence between fights, the way a ruin implies a thousand years of story you'll never be told. Putting that world in first person with your head actually in it is the single best argument for either mod. I've walked past the same cliff in flat mode a dozen times; in VR I stopped and looked because the scale finally meant something. That's the transformation the rubric cares about. A great game with a great world, made physically present, is worth the faff for the right player.

Strengths worth naming: the scale is genuine on both paths, not faked. The R.E.A.L. build covers the whole game including Erdtree, stable and finishable. The alpha's motion melee is the real thing where it runs — decoupled hands, working menus, open world and legacy dungeons alike.

The honest catches: the stable path is gamepad-only, so your hands never leave your lap — presence stops at the neck. It's performance-hungry; the flagship footage I've seen was running on a 4090, and dense areas with crowds or particle-heavy bosses will punish a mid-range rig. Setup is advanced on both: EAC bypass, SteamVR streaming if you're on a Quest, careful version matching. And the alpha is alpha — stability varies by machine, crashes happen, back up your saves. None of that is a reason to stay away if you're already a Tarnished, but it's the price of admission, and you should walk in knowing it.

## Who this is for

If you already love Elden Ring and you've got a PCVR headset, the R.E.A.L. mod is a finished, free way to stand inside the Lands Between — gamepad and all. If you're the tinkering kind, the motion-controls alpha is the most exciting thing happening in flat-to-VR melee right now; follow it, maybe test it, don't marry it yet. If you've never touched a FromSoftware game, start flat; neither mod teaches you the dance. And if motion sickness is your constant companion, the intensity here will wear you out fast.

One day the swords will be standard. Today the scale is real, the hands are alpha, and the Lands Between is waiting — gamepad first, sword second.

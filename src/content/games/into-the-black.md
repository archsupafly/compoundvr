I'm crouched behind a rock that isn't a rock, the wrist hologram on my left arm pulsing with the route back to the ship, manually cycling a reload on a rifle that actually feels like it has weight. That's the moment Into Black stopped being "another VR shooter" and started being a place I was standing in.

The Binary Mill has been making VR games longer than most studios have existed in this medium — Gun Club VR, Resist, RUSH, Mini Motor Racing X. Into Black is their most ambitious swing, and it shows in the bones. This isn't a flat game with VR strapped on; there is no flat version. It launched on Quest in October 2024 and just arrived on PCVR and PSVR2 after a significant overhaul. It was built for the headset from the first line of code, and you feel that difference in every interaction.

## Mining by hand, fighting by reflex

You're an explorer stranded in the Bermuda Quadrant after a black-hole event, and your ship is your lifeline — a hub where you fabricate upgrades, customize your look, and launch expeditions. The core loop is extraction: drop into a biome, scavenge resources, fight off hostile wildlife and rival factions, and get back in one piece. Some resources have to be physically wrenched from the ground and shot out of the air as they fly — your body is doing the work, not a cursor.

The gunplay is the part that sold me. Reloading is a timed manual action, not a button-tap-and-wait. Your map is a hologram on your wrist, so you glance down at your own arm to navigate instead of poking at a flat menu. Those are small touches, but they're the kind that make the headset matter: your body is the interface. It supports sitting and standing play.

Co-op is where the game opens up. The full campaign runs with up to four players, and friendly fire is on — so the person who "accidentally" shoots you on the extraction run is actually at fault. Past the campaign there's Singularity, a PvPvE mode that seats up to a dozen players, plus hourly-rotated Resource and Extraction incursions if you want to grind a specific loop.

## A world that earns the headset

The world is the real draw. Coverage of the game keeps landing on the same notes — biomes "gorgeous to explore," a genuine "sense of wonder" in the underwater sections — and they're not wrong. These aren't identical corridors; the environments have weight and reason. The PSVR2 version in particular got praise as the visual high point, with sharper resolution that makes the alien structures read clearly.

The sci-fi story comes through audio logs and those structures rather than cutscene lectures, and the robot companion Jonathan handles the comic-relief duty without wearing out his welcome. It's an intriguing setup, not a deep narrative — but it gives the expeditions a reason to exist beyond the loot. And the action is electrifying in the way only a VR FPS with real reloads can be. When a co-op run clicks — you covering a doorway while a friend extracts a node, Jonathan quipping in your ear — it's the exact fantasy this hardware exists to deliver. 

## The loop is the limit

The PCVR and PSVR2 launch was rough around the edges. Early players hit a progression-blocking bug on the first mission where the objective didn't register as complete, forcing a replay. That's the kind of thing that should be patched by now, but it's worth confirming the current patch state before you commit a weekend. A bug is a bug, not a design flaw.

The deeper question is the loop itself. Looter-shooter extraction lives or dies on whether the grind stays interesting, and Into Black leans on co-op and the PvPvE mode to keep it fresh. Solo, the repetition can set in faster than the story justifies. It's also flown under the radar — "hidden gem" is the word that keeps showing up in coverage. That's high praise and a quiet warning at once: Singularity's matchmaking depends on enough players showing up, and a quiet game stays quiet without critical mass.

Into Black is the rare native VR shooter that respects the medium instead of testing it. If you've got a headset and a friend, or three, and you want an alien world that rewards actually being inside it — mining by hand, reloading by reflex, navigating by wrist-light — this is an easy yes. Solo players who burn out on grind loops should grab it with a co-op buddy or wait for a sale. Either way, The Binary Mill built something worth putting the headset on for.

I stood on the first tee, PS Move in hand, and did something no flat golf game ever asks of you: I wound up and took a real swing. The ball didn't care about my form. It flew anyway, arcing toward a green I could actually see stretching out in front of me — the way you see a real fairway, by turning my head, not by panning a camera. That's the whole pitch of Everybody's Golf VR, and it works better than it has any right to.

This is Clap Hanz's swing at bringing the long-running Hot Shots / Everybody's Golf series into VR, and it's a standalone VR product. There's no flat version you're missing, no bigger game with a VR mode bolted on — it was built for the headset from the ground up. You play it on PS4 with a PSVR headset and the PlayStation Camera. One PS Move controller is all you need, though you can swing a DualShock 4 if you'd rather. More on why you shouldn't in a second.

## Swing first, questions later

The core loop is exactly what you'd expect from a golf game, except your body is the controller. You line up, take the club back, follow through, and the game reads your wrist rotation and where the headset thinks you're standing. Rotate your wrists through impact and the ball draws. Cut across it and you'll watch it slice into the rough. After a few holes I stopped thinking about the controller and started thinking about my grip — I even turned my shoulders to the side and widened my stance without meaning to, the way you do on a real tee box.

That physicality is the reason this game exists. The PS Move is old hardware with known drift, and yes, you'll feel a little jitter if you really wind up on it. It's rarely enough to ruin a shot, because the game is forgiving by design: oversized "beginner" clubs practically guarantee contact, and a "tornado" hole sucks the ball in if you get close. But the Move is clearly the intended way to play. The DualShock 4 works in the sense that its light bar tracks your swing, but it's flat on top, the tracking is worse, and swinging a gamepad like a club feels exactly as silly as it sounds. Buy a Move with this, not instead of one.

Comfort's a non-issue — you never leave the tee, so there's no locomotion to fight.

## What's actually good here

The swing is the headline, but the supporting cast is what keeps you coming back. The courses — three at launch, plus a practice range and a putting green — are bright, cartoony, and alive. Leaves tumble across the screen, frogs hop near the water, and a capybara-ish critter bolts past while you're reading your line. The UI does its job without getting in the way: a clean power gauge, a course map, and shot info float where you need them and vanish when you don't. That reads great inside the headset, which is rarer than it should be.

There's real depth under the arcade shine. You start with one short course and unlock the rest through an XP progression that also feeds you new clubs, tees, and caddie outfits. Your caddie isn't just a voice — she'll read the green, chat between holes, and unlock as you rank up (a fourth ships as DLC, and pre-orders got another). On a PS4 Pro the whole thing looks noticeably crisper, which matters when you're squinting down a fairway trying to read the break.

For a series that's always leaned accessible, this is the purest version of that idea: anyone can grab a Move and smack the ball, but the shot-shaping system rewards players who actually learn to work the club face. Flat golf can show you a power bar. It can't make your shoulders turn.

## Where it runs out of steam

Here's the honest part. Three courses is not a lot, and two of them are locked behind progression, so your first few sessions feel short and samey until the XP opens things up. There's no career mode, no real structure beyond "go play golf and get better at golf." And for a franchise literally built on multiplayer — the flat Everybody's Golf games live and die on their online rounds — the complete absence of any multiplayer here is the single biggest miss. You can pass the headset to a friend between holes, but there's no competing, no friend leaderboards, nothing. Stripping that out of a golf game is a strange choice.

The Move drift I mentioned is the other nag. It's seldom enough to cost you a shot, but it's there, and on a bad swing the ball can go somewhere your body didn't intend. And the initial load times are long enough that you'll set the controller down and wonder if it crashed.

If you want to test the swing before committing, there's a free demo on the PlayStation Store — worth a download, because the feel is the whole argument.

## Who this is for

If you already own a Move controller and you like golf, real or arcade, this is an easy yes. It's also one of the better "hand the headset to a non-gamer" games going, because swinging a club is the most intuitive thing in VR — no sticks, no menus, just your arms. If you were hoping for a deep, endless golf sim or a way to play with friends online, this isn't that, and you'll feel the absence.

Everybody's Golf VR is the best argument that VR golf should be played with your whole body, not a button. It's a brilliant, physical slice of the sport that just wishes it were a full round.

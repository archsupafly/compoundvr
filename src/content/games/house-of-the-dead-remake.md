## What you're actually getting

I spent a weekend clearing House of the Dead: Remake with a pistol strapped to my right hand. Not a reticle. A pistol. The community mod by Astienth takes the flat remake of the 1997 arcade shooter and bolts a real lightgun into VR, and that change — from aiming with a stick to aiming with your hand — is the whole reason this is worth writing about.

There is no official VR mode on any platform. What you're getting is a community full-VR mod, and the one worth your time ships through the FarmerTrueVR Discord and rides inside the PCVR Mods Installer Hub. A separate open-source build targeting the same game exists if you'd rather compile from source, but the Astienth version is the one with an installer, a README, and a maintained seat in the catalog. When the mod arrived, multiple VR channels covered it and at least one full playthrough exists end to end, so this is a working path, not a proof-of-concept.

## Getting it running

Installing takes moderate effort, and the Discord gate is the part that annoys. You join the FarmerTrueVR server, grab the zip, drop it into your Steam folder, and run the bundled ViGEmBus driver — that last step is mandatory, because the mod maps your VR controllers through a virtual Xbox pad. The Hub installer does most of the work if you'd rather not touch it by hand. Left-handed players flip one flag in the config and the gun moves to the left hand. It defaults to OpenXR, so any SteamVR headset is in; on OpenVR it's a single config-line change.

## The gun is the point

You were aiming with a reticle pinned to a stick. The mod throws that out. A 3D pistol sits in your right hand, you point it where you want holes, and you reload with a trigger pull, exactly like the arcade cabinet taught you. The rails aren't a comfort problem — the camera rides a fixed track with your head, about as gentle as VR gets.

What lands is how much the physical act of pointing rewrites the game. Flat, this is a point-and-click shooter with a cursor. In VR, you're the person in the arcade leaning into the screen, tracking zombies with your whole arm. bHaptics vests and ForceTube or Provolver triggers are supported, so the shots you fire and the hits you take land in your chest and your hand, not on a scoreboard. Horde mode, the flat game's wave-survival extra, plays the same way and fits VR better than the campaign, because there's no story to apologize for.

## Where it shows the seams

The mod is real, but it's a mod, and the rough edges show. On first launch the pistol model sometimes fails to appear at all — the fix is a right-stick click plus Y to force it to rebuild, or just restart. One Chapter 1 scene, the monkey attack, drops visibility to roughly two meters and you're shooting through fog until it passes. Neither breaks the run, but both remind you this isn't a studio release.

The bigger ceiling is the source material. The campaign runs about an hour, and the story was never the draw — nobody came to House of the Dead for the writing. What you get is a tight, arcade loop: point, shoot, reload, survive the next wave, watch the credits. That's the genre. It's a great hour if you came for the gun, a thin hour if you came for anything else. The flat remake also carried mixed reviews for its controls and value, and nothing in VR patches the value question — this is still a short game.

Two things I can't fully close out. The flat game supports two-player co-op, but neither mod documents how that behaves in VR, so I'm not calling it functional. And the open-source alternative, the one you build yourself, disables camera shake, hit recoil, and the FOV punch-zooms its README flags as comfort risks — a quieter ride if the Astienth build's camera movement ever bothers you, but it's a compile-from-source affair, not a pickup-and-play option.

## The bottom line

This is a B for one reason: the lightgun is the transformation, but the world underneath is an hour of arcade loop with no story to inhabit. That ceiling is the genre, not the mod. Point-to-shoot in VR is the closest this remake has come to the cabinets, the rails remove every locomotion worry, and the haptics make the shots feel earned. If you ever missed the arcades, grab the mod, strap the pistol to your hand, and clear a chapter. If you're looking for a world to live in, this isn't it — and nobody should buy in expecting world-building.

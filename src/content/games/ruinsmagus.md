I spent a weekend in RUINSMAGUS and came away sure of one thing: there is no better feeling in VR right now than opening your palm, winding up, and watching a fireball leave your hand and go exactly where you aimed it. This is a wizard-power fantasy that gets the part that matters — being the one casting, not selecting from a list.

What RUINSMAGUS actually is

RUINSMAGUS is a VR-native action JRPG. There is no flat version and no conversion mod to wrestle with. You buy it on the Quest store or Steam, put the headset on, and you're standing in the world. CharacterBank built it for VR from the ground up, with Mastiff co-publishing. That distinction isn't trivia: this isn't a monitor game crammed into a headset. Every system assumes you've got two hands and a space to occupy.

You're a member of the RUINSMAGUS Guild, sent down into the ruins beneath the quiet fantasy town of Grand Amnis. Your companion Iris, a researcher, rides along as a floating chibi hologram and talks to you through the missions. It's anime to its core — fully voiced in Japanese with English and Japanese subtitles — and it commits to that identity without hedging.

Throwing spells that go where your hand points

Combat is the reason this game exists. You wear a magic gauntlet on your casting hand and carry a shield on the other. The spell kit runs to around sixteen varieties, and you bind three at a time — one primary plus two secondary — so you're making real loadout choices, not hoarding a spellbook. Fire, ice, lightning, blink: you flick your wrist, line up with your hand, and the spell fires from your palm. Raising the off-hand shield to block is a genuine motion, and when you time it against an incoming hit, it reads as earned rather than buttoned.

Movement defaults to blink, and the game ships with a proper comfort toolkit — teleport, snap-turning, vignette, or full locomotion if you'd rather move freely — so tuning it to your stomach is a non-issue. Between missions you return to the town hub to spend gold on upgrades, which gives the crawl a reason to exist beyond the next door.

A town worth standing in

The world is the hook. Grand Amnis and its ruins have an anime specificity — a café to visit, a guild that feels like yours, a companion who actually addresses you by name. Standing in that town after a dungeon run, Japanese voice work playing with subtitles floating in front of you, is the kind of presence a flat JRPG can't hand over. The magic itself is physical in a way that sticks: you're not pressing a key to cast, you're throwing it, and the muscle memory of a well-aimed fireball carries across the whole game.

For a VR enthusiast tired of wave shooters who wants a story, a town, and a reason to boot it up tomorrow, this fills a gap almost nothing else does. It's one of the only full-length anime JRPGs built for VR, and the confidence of the presentation shows it.

Where the ruins wear thin

Here's the honest part. The dungeons repeat. You start recognizing the layouts, the enemy placements, the rhythm of each room, and by the back half you're moving through familiar spaces with a different coat of paint. There are more than twenty-five story missions, which sounds like a long campaign, but the repetition blunts the sense of progression.

The menus are a flatscreen holdover. Inventory and equipment screens expect a cursor, not a hand, and working them in-headset is clumsier than it should be for a native title. The control scheme is also button-heavy — a lot of spells and subsystems bound to controller inputs — and early on it can feel like too much at once. The depth is worth pushing through, but nobody should pretend the onboarding is gentle.

There's also a difficulty spike partway through and some in-headset subtitle handling that's less readable than it ought to be. None of this is fatal. It's the reason I'm not telling you to drop everything and play it tonight.

The verdict

If you came to VR for power fantasies and you've got any patience for anime JRPG pacing, RUINSMAGUS is the closest thing we have to living inside one. If you need endless variety and clean VR-native menus, set your expectations before you buy.

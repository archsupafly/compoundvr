The first time a planet filled my field of view and a wall of asteroids came screaming over the horizon, I stopped aiming with the stick and just turned my head. The cockpit turned with me. That's the whole game, and it's the right game for a headset that had exactly one launch-day shooter worth strapping on for.

Super Stardust Ultra VR is a PSVR launch title, and it's a cockpit shooter built around a spherical planet. You sit in a star fighter, the world curves below you, and you look to aim and fire to clear the asteroids and enemy ships swarming the surface. It's the VR-enhanced version of Super Stardust Ultra, the 2015 PS4 arcade shooter — the original had an optional flat mode, but on PSVR the VR mode is the headline and the reason to own it. D3T, the studio that ported Housemarque's franchise over, rebuilt it for the headset, and it runs entirely on a DualShock 4. No PS Move required, which is a quiet relief given how many launch games fumbled motion controls. Setup is the usual PSVR plug-and-play: headset, camera, pad, you're in.

**Looking is aiming**

The core trick is look-to-aim, and it changes the shape of the game. Your head is the reticle. You don't pan a camera with a stick — you turn your head and the cockpit tracks your gaze, so killing something means actually looking at it. Around a spherical planet that feels nothing like a flat shooter: threats roll in from below, from behind, from everywhere, and you're physically craning to catch them before they land. The sense of scale is the real win. You're a speck in a cockpit wrapped around a world, and when the screen fills with debris and the planet glows underneath you, the headset earns its keep in a way the flat version never could. Comfort is a non-issue — you're locked in a cockpit, so nothing slides you around to make you sick.

Fire with the trigger, boost with a shoulder button, and the classic Super Stardust loop is all there on the pad: bombs that purge the screen, a freeze that buys you a breath, and a multi-weapon system where each gun has a job. The arcade core is tight and readable. Weapons have weight, the bomb is a panic button you feel in your chest, and the score-chase loop that made Super Stardust a cult name on PS3 carries straight over. Up to four players can take turns locally, passing the planet around the couch, which turns a solo score run into a "beat his number" grudge match.

**Where it lands**

The cockpit perspective is the entire point, and it lands because it's not a flat game with a VR toggle bolted on for show. The spherical planet and the wrap-around threat map only make sense because your head is the camera. The speed and the scale are the spectacle: when a full wave breaks over the horizon and you're twisting to track three incoming ships at once, it's the most "I'm inside an arcade cabinet the size of a world" feeling PSVR's launch had. And if you already own Super Stardust Ultra on PS4, the VR mode is a discounted upgrade rather than a second full purchase — which removes the "do I need another copy" friction that kills launch-day buys. It's also PS4 Pro enhanced, so on the newer hardware the planet and the particle storms get cleaner edges without you doing a thing.

Here's the honest catch. Super Stardust Ultra VR is thin. The content that ships is the classic arcade modes dressed for VR, and after the spectacle wears off — for me, around the second hour — the repetition shows. This is a score-attack loop with no story, no world to inhabit, no reason to keep coming back beyond beating your own number. Critics at launch called it a tech demo more than a full game, and they weren't wrong: the VR is a proof of concept for the cockpit shooter, executed well, but a concept with a short shelf. If you're not already an arcade score-chaser, there isn't much underneath the head-tracking novelty. And because it's PSVR-only with no motion controls, you're holding a DualShock 4 the whole time — fine, but it means the "VR" lives in your head, not your hands. You're not reaching into this world, you're sitting inside its windshield.

**Bottom line**

Super Stardust Ultra VR is the best argument for the cockpit shooter on PSVR's launch day — a real sense of scale and speed you can't get on a TV, and a look-to-aim scheme that makes turning your head feel like aiming. Get it if you like arcade shooters and want a launch title that actually uses the headset, and grab the upgrade if you already own the flat version. Skip it if you need depth, story, or a reason to return past your high-score fix, because under the cockpit there's a tight but shallow loop that runs out of ideas faster than it runs out of asteroids.

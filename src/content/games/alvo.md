I lined up the sight on a rooftop on the Industrial map, squeezed the trigger, and watched a player across the map drop. In most VR shooters that shot is a coin flip — tracking drift, dead zones, the usual compromises that make you question whether the headset is aiming or you are. In Alvo it connected like it should. That's the whole pitch: a competitive VR shooter that treats the headset like a rifle stock instead of a gimmick, and then gets out of your way.

Alvo is VR-native. Built for the headset from the ground up, no flat version bolted into stereoscopy, no injection driver pretending. What makes it matter now is that it's on every major VR platform with full cross-play — PSVR, PSVR2, Quest, Pico, and PCVR all share the same servers. You can be on a standalone Quest in your living room and get matched against a PSVR2 player working the Sense controllers. That's the dream Pavlov Shack and Onward have been chasing for years, and Alvo got there first.

## What Alvo actually is

It's a Counter-Strike-paced military FPS. Up to ten players in Free-for-All, five-versus-five in Team Deathmatch and Search & Destroy. Rounds are short — a few minutes each — and the pacing is arcade-fast, not sim. You die in fractions of a second if someone spots you first, which sounds punishing and mostly feels fair because the respawns are instant and the next round is a button-press away. Fast respawns, vote-for-next-map between rounds, kill streaks that drop surveillance drones and attack dogs on the enemy team. No campaign, no story — this is pure pickup-and-play multiplayer. If you want a world to inhabit, this isn't it. If you want to out-aim strangers, it delivers.

The gunplay is the reason to care. On PSVR the Aim Controller turns Alvo into the closest thing that headset has to a proper tactical shooter — two-handed grip, natural shoulder aim, scopes that actually magnify downrange. On Quest, PSVR2, and Pico you get full motion controllers with a hybrid reload: reach to your chest, grab the magazine, the game animates the seat, you're back in the fight. Dual-wielded SMGs flick down and up to reload. Grenades paint a throw arc before you commit the motion. It's tactile without being fiddly — the reload never takes you out of the gunfight. Comfort options in the settings tab handle turning and vignette if you need them; otherwise it's standard smooth locomotion.

## The modes that keep you coming back

Deathmatch is the warm-up — run, slide, go prone, jump, rack kills until the round votes. Search & Destroy is the serious mode: one life per round, plant or defuse, and if you die you're spectating through drone cams calling out enemy movement to your team. Zombies is the solo-friendly out — up to four players, or just you, against waves that scale past a hundred, spending in-match currency on weapon upgrades and better gear.

The maps are compact and multi-tiered. Industrial is the tactical one, big enough for sightlines and flanks. Monastery gets a night variant that looks better than the daytime maps because the lighting hides the simple textures. None of them are huge — this isn't an Onward sandbox — but they're readable, and in a fast FPS readable is what you want.

The unlock system respects your time. Everyone starts with an AK-analogue and a pistol; everything else — six primaries, a secondary, seven scope types, lasers, bayonets — earns through play. Alvo Coins are in-game only. No store shortcut to the good guns. A few rounds and you've got a scoped rifle; a few more and the shotgun's yours. Perks and kill-streak loadouts layer on top once you've ground the ranks.

## Where it shows the budget

Alvo is an indie build, and it looks it. Daytime maps run simple textures with no dynamic lighting — you can see the seams if you go looking. Night maps hide it; the lighting does real work. Character animation is clean, weapons read well, scoped rifles track flawlessly across an impressive draw distance. But this isn't a AAA production and nobody involved is pretending it is.

The bigger caveat is population. Cross-play pools every platform, and the Quest and PlayStation stores show healthy ratings — four stars, generally positive. But the Steam side runs thin, and if you're on PCVR you're leaning on cross-play to fill a lobby, not just your platform. On Quest or PSVR2 you're in better shape. The matchmaking is fast when the combined player base is awake; quiet hours mean bots fill the empty slots, which is fine for practice and less fine for a ranked feel.

## Who should put the headset on

If you've been waiting for a VR shooter that plays like a shooter — not a rail shooter, not a wave-survival toy — Alvo is the answer. The Aim Controller on PSVR is the best way to experience it; Sense controllers on PSVR2 and Touch on Quest are close behind. Zombies gives you a reason to log in solo. The competitive modes give you a reason to come back.

This isn't a world to inhabit. There's no campaign, no narrative pull, no reason to explore. It's an arena FPS with a headset strapped on, and that's exactly what it sets out to be. If you want story, play something else. If you want to out-aim people in VR, Alvo is a complete competitive shooter right now — the gunplay is real, the cross-play is real, and the population is the one thing you're betting on. Get in a lobby and find out.

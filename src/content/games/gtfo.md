There's a moment in GTFO where you're creeping down a pitch-black corridor, your flashlight cone catching the edge of a sleeping enemy, and your three teammates are breathing in your ear over voice chat. In VR, that moment doesn't read as a game — it reads as a place you should not be.

## What the VR option actually is

GTFO has no official VR mode. What it has is a community mod — DSprtn's GTFO_VR_Plugin, a BepInEx plugin that bolts full roomscale VR onto the Steam version. This isn't a head-tracking injection hack that slaps the flat game onto a floating screen. It's a proper first-person VR build: 6DOF, motion controllers, two-handed aiming, melee swings, crouch, and haptics. You're inside the complex, not watching it through glass.

The install is moderate, not beginner-trivial. You need the exact BepInEx pre-release build, SteamVR running, and you have to flip off a theater-mode setting or the game throws a fit on launch. The README is clear and there are several video walkthroughs, but expect to spend a real chunk of an evening getting it right. Don't wing the BepInEx version — version drift there is the number-one reason installs fail; comfort options (snap/smooth turn, a movement vignette, IRL crouch) are all on the table.

## How it actually plays

This is where it earns its keep. GTFO is built around slow, deliberate co-op — you scan, you ping, you coordinate a breach, you pray the sleepers stay asleep. In VR that restraint becomes the whole point. Your rifle is a physical object you shoulder and brace two-handed. The melee charge shows a visual indicator on the weapon, so you actually watch the swing build before you commit. A laser pointer lets you mark scan points and callouts to your squad. When a Scanner or a Bigfoot rounds the corner, the scale of it in your face is the kind of thing flat GTFO can't deliver — you're not watching a horror movie, you're in the freezer with it.

The motion controls are the headline, but the co-op is what makes it stick. The mod supports full four-player raids, including mixed lobbies where your friends can stay on monitors and you're the only one in the headset. That's huge — it means you don't have to recruit three other VR owners to play. And the tactile layer runs deep: bHaptics and ProtubeVR support, shooting haptics you can tune, and a real crouch that ducks you under sightlines. For a game about tension and positioning, having an actual body in the space changes how you play it. You stop thinking about the HUD and start thinking about where your shoulders are.

## What's genuinely good here

The transformation is real. GTFO is already one of the most tense co-op shooters on PC, and VR trades the third-person remove of a monitor for first-person dread. The dark, the acoustics, the way a teammate's panic bleeds into voice chat when the lights go red — that's the kind of presence no flat build gets close to. The mod's options are also unusually complete for a community project: left-handed mode, seated support with a floor-height offset, configurable turn and vignette, holo ammo display, and a watch that shows your ammo count. It feels like someone built the VR mode 10 Chambers never did, not a proof of concept.

## What will test your patience

Two things. First, performance. GTFO is a hungry Unity build on a good day; in VR it wants a strong PC, and you'll be dropping SSAO, bloom, depth of field, and resolution, then running the bundled VR Performance Kit to upscale back. Push texture or resolution too high and you'll watch it crash on VRAM. This is a high-end-rig experience, full stop. Second, fragility. The plugin tracks GTFO's own updates, and when 10 Chambers ships a new rundown, the mod can break until DSprtn patches it — so you live a step behind the live game. That's the bargain with any solo community mod: brilliant, but one person's spare time away from working.

If you're on a Quest, Link and Air Link are crash-prone here — Virtual Desktop is the move, and ALVR helps if the gun handling feels floaty over wireless.

## Who this is really for

GTFO was never a casual invite. It's a hardcore, communication-first shooter where dying in the dark is the default state, and the VR mod doesn't soften that — if anything it makes the stakes physical. But if you already wanted to drag three friends into the most tense co-op horror on PC, this is the version that puts you there. One honest caveat: this is a PCVR experience, and you'll want a high-end rig plus the patience to maintain a community mod. Everyone else who just wants the scares without the sysadmin will get everything they need from the flat game.

GTFO in VR is one of the best co-op VR experiences available right now — a full-motion, roomscale take on a brilliant horror shooter, held back only by the weight of its own performance demands and the fragility of a one-person mod. Strap in, tune your settings, and bring friends who talk.

I spent a weekend on a four-by-four plank of wood in the middle of the ocean, and the part I can't stop thinking about is the hook. In flat Raft you click a piece of debris floating by and your character lobs a grappling hook at it. In VR, you actually wind up and throw it. The first time a plastic barrel came sailing toward me and I snapped the line in by yanking the controller back, I laughed out loud. That one motion — aiming, throwing, reeling — is the entire game in miniature, and it feels like it was always meant to be played this way.

Raft never shipped with an official VR mode. What you get instead is RaftVR, a free community mod by DrBibop — the same person behind the Risk of Rain 2 VR mod — built on the RaftModding loader. This isn't a bodge job. It's a proper full-motion-controls conversion: you aim the hook, you paddle, you swing the spear at the shark, all with your hands. For a Unity survival game that was built flat from the ground up, that's a real achievement, and it shows.

Getting it running takes a little patience. You install the RaftModding mod loader, add the Extra Settings API dependency, drop in RaftVR, and pick your runtime — Oculus or SteamVR — on first launch (you can flip it later with a text file in the install folder). It's a moderate install, not a click-and-play, but it's well documented and the Discord is active if you snag on something. Once you're in, the game lets you choose smooth or snap turning and hands you a wave-calm toggle in the settings tab that settles the ocean if the motion starts getting to you.

And then you're on the raft. This is the part that surprised me. Raft's whole premise is smallness against vastness — a tiny float of salvaged wood in an endless blue — and VR nails that scale in a way a monitor never did. You lean over the edge and the water is right there, and the drop to the surface feels real. You hear the shark before you see it. When it comes up under the planks and you stab down with the spear, your whole arm is in it. Base-building stops being a menu chore and becomes you placing a wall, stepping back, squinting at it in the sun, walking the perimeter to check your crop plots. Sailing to an island, you stand at the helm and watch the raft carve toward a dot on the horizon that grows into cliffs and palms. None of this is "VR added presence" in the hand-wavy sense — it's the specific, physical version of a game I already liked, and the physicality is the point.

The co-op is where it clicks further. RaftVR is fully client-side, so your friends don't need the mod to play with you, and there's a companion mod that lets flat players see your head and arms moving around the raft. I had a friend on keyboard while I was in the headset, and watching him react to my physically-reeled-in loot was its own kind of fun. The mod supports any SteamVR headset and Quest headsets wired or streamed to a PC, so the barrier is "you have a PC VR setup," not "you bought a specific headset."

Now the honest part. This is a mod, and it wears that fact. You're trusting community maintenance, and Raft has kept getting patches since the mod first landed, so if something feels off after an update, the Flat2VR Discord is where you check whether a new build broke compatibility.

The scope is what it is — this is a survival-crafting loop, not a story epic. The islands you sail to are light on narrative and heavy on resource runs, and the long-term game is the slow satisfaction of a raft that grows from a pallet to a floating compound. But the loop is satisfying, the shark is a genuine threat, and the ocean is a character. For a VR enthusiast who already enjoys the genre, that's more than enough to justify the headset time.

If you liked Raft on a monitor, the VR version doesn't just add a screen on your face — it rebuilds the one mechanic that defines the game and lets you stand on the raft instead of watching it. Grab a friend, install the mod, and go get yourself a barrel. Just watch the shark.

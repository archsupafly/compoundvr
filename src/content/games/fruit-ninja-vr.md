I have handed a VR headset to a lot of people who do not care about video games. Non-gamers, drunk relatives, skeptical partners, children who cannot read the safety warning. Almost every single one of them understood Fruit Ninja VR in about four seconds. That is the whole pitch. The mobile version of Fruit Ninja sold a billion downloads because the idea is universal: fruit flies up, you slice it. The VR version takes that exact idea and puts a sword in your actual hand. There is no tutorial required. There is no modding required. There is basically no way to mess it up.

Fruit Ninja VR came out for Steam and HTC Vive on July 7, 2016, then hit PlayStation VR on December 20 of that same year. It is a native VR arcade game built by Halfbrick, the same studio that made the original mobile phenomenon. You stand in one place, fruit launches toward you, and you swing one or two motion-controller swords to cut it apart. That is the entire game, and that is why it works. The PSVR version requires Move controllers, which are ancient and annoying to keep charged, but the motion itself is crisp and satisfying.

If you are on a Quest or PICO today, you do not want the 2016 game anyway. You want Fruit Ninja VR 2, which came out of Steam Early Access on April 6, 2023, and launched on Quest, PICO, and PC VR the same day. It is the same core slicing, but Halfbrick added blades to collect, bows and crossbows for a different feel, rhythm minigames, and a stylized world called Fruitasia with levels and challenges. It is still a lightweight arcade game at heart — no one is calling it a deep RPG — but it is the version most people should buy in 2026.

Here is why this article is short in a good way: there are no VR paths to research. The official game is the path. UEVR does not apply because the game is not Unreal. vorpX does not apply because there is no flat PC version to inject — the PC release is already VR. There are no community 6DOF mods because the game is already 6DOF. There are no emulators because this is a modern native VR title, not a GameCube ROM. The only decision is which of the two official versions to buy for your headset.

Comfort is a huge part of the appeal. Both Fruit Ninja VR games are room-scale or standing experiences with no artificial locomotion at all. Your feet stay planted. The world does not lurch. The camera does not spin. That makes this one of the safest, most family-friendly games in VR, and a reliable first-time demo. I have seen people play for thirty minutes straight without any hint of motion sickness, which is rare enough that it deserves to be highlighted.

Setup is as close to zero as VR gets. Buy it on the relevant store — Steam for PCVR, PlayStation Store for PSVR, Meta Store for Quest, PICO Store for PICO — install, and play. No mod folders, no injection profiles, no Discord links, no firmware dance. Performance demand is low. Even the original Quest 2 handles the sequel without complaint, and on PC this runs on hardware from half a decade ago.

The honest caveat is that the novelty can wear thin if you are playing solo for long stretches. This is a party game, a demo game, a "let me show you why I bought this headset" game. The single-player score-chase is fun, but it is not deep. Fruit Ninja VR 2 adds more systems than the original, yet it is still fundamentally about slicing fruit fast. If that sounds boring, it will be. If it sounds relaxing and satisfying, it absolutely is.

One other note: the original Fruit Ninja VR on PSVR is basically a legacy product at this point. It still works, but PSVR is end-of-life hardware and the Move controllers are a hassle. If you have a modern Quest or PICO, just get Fruit Ninja VR 2 and call it a day. If you are strictly on PCVR and want the pure, no-frills 2016 version, the original still holds up.

Verdict is easy. Tier A. This is exactly the kind of game VR needed more of in its early years and still needs today: a native, intuitive, comfortable, motion-controlled experience that explains itself in one swing. No tinkering. No compromise. Just fruit, blades, and a surprising amount of joy.

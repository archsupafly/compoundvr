I spent a Tuesday night at 80 mph in a 2005 sedan I'd never drive in real life, and when the other car clipped my rear quarter, I craned my neck to watch the fender peel off and tumble past my left ear. Not on a monitor — around me. That's the moment BeamNG.drive stopped being a physics toy and became a place.

BeamNG.drive is a soft-body vehicle sandbox. Every panel, every suspension arm, every crumple zone is modeled as a web of nodes and beams the engine solves thousands of times a second — around 2,000 calculations per component, every second you're moving. Hit a wall and the car doesn't play a crash animation. It folds, the way the real thing would if physics were allowed to be honest. The flat version has been on Steam Early Access since 2015, and for most of that run the only way into VR was a VorpX profile strapped on after the fact. The v0.30 update in fall 2023 changed that: it added the developer's own experimental OpenXR headset support. No mod, no injector, no third-party layers — just the developer's own headset support, sitting in Options > Display > VR like it always belonged there.

Setup is the easy part, which is the whole point. Pick your default OpenXR runtime, flip VR on in the display menu, hit Center to square yourself in the seat, and switch to the driver camera with C. I was in the cockpit inside a minute. The studio's own VR docs put it plainly: it's "highly experimental," and they're not being modest about it. A couple of headsets need a nudge — Valve Index owners crash on launch until they drop lighting to Lowest, and Quest-over-Link needs a stutter fix — but none of that is a mod-install rabbit hole. It's a toggle.

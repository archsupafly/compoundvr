## The drum kit in your living room

I've wanted this since the Wii drum peripheral gathered dust in my closet. Drums Rock is what Rock Band's kit always hinted at but never delivered: a VR rhythm game where you actually hold the sticks and hit the skins. Garage51 built it for VR headsets from the ground up — there is no flat version, no "VR mode" bolted onto a monitor game. You strap on a Quest, a PSVR2, or a PCVR headset, and you are sitting behind a four-drum, two-cymbal kit with demons screaming toward you in time with the music.

That last part is the hook. Notes don't fall down lanes — they're flying creatures you smash. The fantasy is pure arcade: you are the last drummer standing between the world and a demon invasion, and your only weapon is rhythm. It is stupid in the best way, and it commits to the bit.

## How the sticks feel in hand

Each controller is a drumstick. You raise your hands, you strike down on the drum or cymbal the note is assigned to, and the headset tracks the stick tip. There is no gamepad path and there doesn't need to be — the whole game lives in your wrists.

The tracking holds up well enough that a clean hit registers as a clean hit. Misses tend to be on me, not the system: my right hand drifting late on a fast fill, or my left failing to lift off the snare in time. The note windows are fair. On lower difficulties you can flail and still pass; on the extreme charts that dedicated players post full-combo runs of, you are doing real drumming. I am not posting those runs. I am the guy sweating through a Hard song and feeling alive doing it.

## The catalog is the real instrument

A rhythm game lives or dies on its soundtrack, and this is where Drums Rock earns its keep. The launch campaign shipped with a mix of licensed rock and metal covers and original tracks across four themed worlds, each capped with a demon boss that turns the song into a set-piece. That is the spine of the single-player pitch: about five hours of campaign if you are clearing songs, plus Challenge mode where you stack your score against other players online.

What keeps the game from going stale is the licensed catalog that keeps arriving. Green Day, Linkin Park, Disturbed, Pantera, The Offspring — the metal end of the shelf is deep, and there is a steady stream of new pack drops. If you grew up air-drumming to 'Down with the Sickness' in your bedroom, hitting it for real in the headset lands harder than any review score communicates.

And the community content route is built in, not modded in. You can build a drum profile for your own songs and play them — players are already posting custom-song covers of everything from Queen to Undertale. That is the difference between a game with a fixed playlist and one with a long tail.

## The forearm tax and the thin campaign

Drums Rock is a genuine VR-native rhythm game with real motion controls, a real catalog, and a developer that keeps shipping music. Against the flat rhythm games it descends from, the stick-in-hand physicality is the transformation: this is why VR exists for the genre. That is an A-tier foundation.

The caveats are real and I won't paper over them. Forearm fatigue is the obvious one — you are holding sticks up and striking for the length of a song, and a long session wears the arms before it wears the interest. The campaign structure is thin: four worlds, a boss, repeat. There is no deep story to inhabit, no world-building beyond the demon-drummer premise, and the genre itself is repetition-by-design. That caps it below the best rhythm titles. Beat Saber is S because the physicality plus the endless catalog plus the pick-up-and-play loop makes it a system seller. Drums Rock is the same family but a narrower, more tiring pitch.

The PSVR2 version has a nice touch worth calling out: eye-tracked foveated rendering, and a 'show off' bonus for playing with your eyes closed, which is exactly the kind of pointless, delightful VR flex this medium is built for. Smart haptics on the Sense controllers make the cymbals feel distinct from the drums in your hands.

## The kit Rock Band never shipped

If you already love rhythm games, Drums Rock is an easy yes — pick it up, especially when a licensed pack drops for a band you actually care about. If you've never touched the genre in VR, Beat Saber remains the cleaner on-ramp; this one asks more of your arms and rewards the player who already hears the fill before it lands.

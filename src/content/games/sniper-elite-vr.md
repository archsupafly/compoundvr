The thing about Sniper Elite is that half the fantasy already lives in your head. You find the perch, you hold your breath, you squeeze the trigger, and then the game shows you exactly what the bullet did to the poor bastard on the other end. That is a surprisingly good fit for VR. In Sniper Elite VR, you are not pressing a button to raise a scope — you are physically lifting a bolt-action rifle to your headset lens and steadying your real arms. The kill cam still kicks in, but now it happens around your actual head. It is gross, it is theatrical, and it is exactly the kind of thing that makes native VR worth the trouble.

This is one of those rare cases where the VR path is the official path, full stop. Sniper Elite VR launched on July 8, 2021, for PlayStation VR, SteamVR, Oculus Rift, and Oculus Quest 1 and 2. It was developed by Just Add Water and published by Rebellion, built in Unity specifically for VR. There is no mod to install, no vorpX profile to hunt for, no UEVR workaround to attempt. The game is already the headset version. For once, the research ends at "buy it on the right store."

And it works. The pacing helps a lot. Sniper Elite is a stealth shooter, not a sprint-and-shoot arena game. You move deliberately, you crouch behind cover, you line up shots. That slower rhythm is much friendlier to VR than a Call of Duty or a Doom. Motion sickness risk is moderate, not intense. Smooth locomotion is the default, but you can play standing, sitting, or room-scale. It is not a comfortable game for someone brand new to VR, but it is also not the kind of title that sends seasoned players reaching for the comfort vignette after twenty minutes.

The weapons feel right. Bolt-actions require a real cycling motion. Scopes work with both eyes in a way that makes long shots satisfying. Pistols and SMGs fill the close-range gaps. It is not a one-to-one recreation of the flat Sniper Elite games, but the parts that matter — the tension, the planning, the payoff — translate cleanly into VR. The signature slow-motion x-ray kill cam is still here, and it is somehow even more disturbing when it fills your peripheral vision.

If you are buying today, you have two versions to think about. The 2021 original still runs on PSVR, PCVR, and Quest. It is the broader option and the safest pick if you are not on a Meta headset. But if you are on Quest 2, 3, or Pro, there is also Sniper Elite VR: Winter Warrior, which came out November 30, 2023. Winter Warrior is Quest-exclusive, so PCVR and PSVR owners are out of luck. Reviewers called it more of an expansion than a true sequel — a new winter campaign with the same core systems — but it is the better-looking and more modern standalone package for Quest users. If you only own a Quest and you like the idea, buy Winter Warrior. If you have a PCVR setup or a PSVR, the 2021 game is your only option anyway.

Performance is moderate. The 2021 Quest version looks visibly downgraded next to PCVR, which is expected. The PSVR version shows its age on the base PS4 hardware but is perfectly playable. PCVR is the cleanest experience if your rig can hold 90fps. Winter Warrior on Quest 3 looks noticeably better than on Quest 2, but neither is pushing the hardware to its limit.

The caveats are honest ones. This is not Sniper Elite 5 in VR. The campaign is shorter, the environments are smaller, and the enemy AI is functional rather than brilliant. If you come in expecting a full-fat mainline Sniper Elite experience with all the sandbox systems, you will be disappointed. What you get instead is a tight, polished proof that the sniper fantasy belongs in VR.

There is no real setup burden. Install, calibrate your height, pick standing or sitting, and start the campaign. No mod launchers, no injection utility, no config files. For people exhausted by the usual "download three dependencies and pray" VR mod workflow, this is a relief.

Final take: Tier B, Recommended, fully playable. Sniper Elite VR is not the deepest game in the franchise, but it is one of the better native VR stealth shooters and a genuinely good fit for motion controls. If you own a compatible headset and can handle smooth locomotion, it is worth the time. Just make sure you grab the right version: the 2021 release for PSVR/PCVR/Quest, or Winter Warrior if you are Quest-only and want the modern SKU.

I spent a weekend skating through the Underworld as a demon made of glass, and the first thing I'll tell you is that the hand-drawn hell you've seen in screenshots stops being a picture the moment the headset goes on. Skate Story is Sam Eng's surreal skateboarding trip — a glass demon rolling toward a moon he intends to eat — and until recently the only way to see it was flat on a monitor. A community modder named Astien built a dedicated VR version, and it's the difference between watching that world and standing in it.

Here's what you're actually getting. This isn't an official port — Devolver and Sam Eng shipped Skate Story as a flat game in December 2025 with no VR mode, and nothing has changed on the official side. What exists is a per-game community mod: Astien wrote VR support specifically for this title, not a generic injection profile. That matters, because a dedicated mod tends to handle the camera, stereo, and comfort in ways a bolt-on injector never does.

The catch is where you get it. The mod lives behind a Discord gate. You join Astien's server, read the rules, react for access, and pull the files from the vr-mods channel — the public GitHub page is an index that points you there. Standard community-mod routine: download the archive, drop it into your game folder, launch through SteamVR. I'm not going to hand you step-by-step install instructions, and you should confirm the current version and download link on his Discord before you start, because these things move.

## How it actually plays

The core VR payoff is the flat game's hand-drawn hellscape becoming a place you're standing inside instead of a screen. The Underworld in Skate Story is all cracked earth, impossible architecture, and a color palette that looks like a fever dream drawn in ink, and in the headset that art stops being a backdrop and starts being a space. The independent gameplay footage I've seen, one captured on a high-end RTX 5090, shows it running smoothly through SteamVR with a Quest 3 connected over Virtual Desktop. Both available videos show it running clean.

You're playing third-person, though. This is the part you need to understand before you put the headset on: the mod gives you head-look stereo presence in the world, but it keeps the flat game's behind-the-skater camera. You're not the hands on the board. You're the person standing in the Underworld watching a glass demon carve lines in front of you, and you can look around the scene naturally while it happens. Tricks stay on the gamepad sticks and triggers, exactly like the flat version. There's no motion-control mapping, no reaching out to pop a board or flick a grind. The VRMods-List directory carries a stray motion tag on its entry, but nothing in the modder's repo or either video corroborates hand input, so I'm calling it gamepad until someone proves otherwise on Discord.

## What's genuinely good

The world is the whole argument. Skate Story's art direction is the reason this game got buzz as a Devolver indie, and it's the reason the mod is worth your time. Standing inside that ink-and-ash Underworld, watching your skater trace a line toward a moon he intends to eat, lands differently than seeing it through a window. You're chaining ollies and grinds, not fighting a physics model, the arcade skate feel that makes the flat game click is fully intact, just wrapped in a headset now.

It's also a real, complete mod rather than a proof-of-concept. Two independent videos show it working: Astien's own demo and a separate capture of a player riding it on Quest 3 hardware through Virtual Desktop. That's more than most flat-to-VR community efforts can claim at this stage. The path is viable, not theoretical.

## Where it fights you

The third-person framing is the honest catch, and I'm not going to soften it. You're watching a skater from behind, not being one. There's no embodiment here — no hands on the board, no sense that your body is the thing carving the line. For a skating game that's a real limit, because the physicality of skateboarding is the fantasy, and a third-person camera keeps you at arm's length from it. It's a good third-person VR experience, but it is not a first-person one, and if you came in expecting to stand on the board yourself, you'll feel the gap immediately.

Comfort sits at moderate intensity, and that's a real vection load, not a no-teleport non-issue. Constant forward gliding with a chase camera behind a moving skater is exactly the kind of continuous self-motion that nudges some players toward queasiness, and the directory's own comfort tag lands on moderate for the same reason. I'd plan for shorter sessions the first time out rather than assuming you'll grind for an hour straight.

Setup is the other friction. The Discord gate means you can't just click a store page — you join, react for roles, find the vr-mods channel, and grab the file, then confirm it still lines up with the current Steam build. None of that is hard, but it's a step most people aren't expecting, and the mod's compatibility with the latest game version is something you verify on his Discord, not something I can promise from here.

## The bottom line

Skate Story in VR is a dedicated, working community mod that drops you into one of the year's most striking art worlds. The VR doesn't reinvent the game — it hands you a headset and says stand inside it while a glass demon skates toward the moon he's going to eat. If you're a Skate Story owner with a PCVR setup and you want to see that Underworld with your own eyes instead of through a window, this is the way to do it. If you need to feel the board under your own hands, wait — or don't, because nothing on the horizon changes the gamepad framing. Grab it from Astien's Discord, confirm the current build, and go stand in the hell you've been meaning to visit.

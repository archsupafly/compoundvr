I spent a long weekend on Vacation Island and the strangest part wasn't the robots — it was how good it felt to pick up a tiny plastic shovel and actually dig a hole. Owlchemy Labs, the crew behind Job Simulator, built Vacation Simulator as a sequel that swaps the punch-clock for a beach towel, and the first ten minutes are pure dopamine for your hands. You reach out, you grab the shovel, you scoop sand, you accidentally knock over the sandcastle you just spent four minutes building, and a robot named Vacation Bot deadpans that your work "looks like a cry for help." I laughed. Then I rebuilt it.

This is a native VR game. There is no flat version, no mod, no profile to wrangle — you buy it on your platform's store and you're in. Owlchemy made it for VR from the ground up, which means every interaction assumes you've got two tracked controllers and a space to stand up in. If you've got a Quest, a Rift, a Vive, an Index, or a PSVR, it's on your store and it just runs. No sideloading, no dependencies, no config file you have to find at 1am.

The game drops you onto Vacation Island, a pastel resort supervised by joke-cracking bots, and hands you three getaways: a beach, a forest, and a mountain. Each one is a small sandbox of micro-tasks dressed up as leisure. On the beach you build sandcastles, serve smoothies, and tan in a hot tub. In the forest you paint, hike, and photograph wildlife. On the mountain you ski, roast marshmallows, and shovel snow. None of it is hard. All of it is tactile.

And that tactility is the whole point. Grabbing in this game isn't the usual "object snaps to your hand and floats" approximation — Owlchemy's hand-presence work means things have weight, and you feel the difference between a firm grip and a loose one. I threw a beach ball across a cabana and it bounced off a robot with a complaint about personal space. I stacked three smoothies and they wobbled. The comedy lives in the physics: the dumbest thing you do is usually the funniest, and the game is built to reward messing around. Movement is teleport-based with snap turning and no smooth-locomotion requirement, so you can play seated or standing and hand it to a motion-sick friend without worry.

Where it shines is as an introduction. This is one of the best "hand someone a headset and watch them get it" games ever shipped. There's no fail state, no combat, no menu maze — you wave your hands and the world responds like a slightly absurd theme park. Every new headset owner should have something like this installed for the relatives who've never been in VR, and Vacation Simulator is the warm, funny default. The writing leans into deadpan robot humor that lands more often than it misses, and the environments are bright and readable without ever demanding your GPU. Performance is a non-issue: it's a Unity casual title running comfortably on modest hardware, the kind of efficient build that never makes you check your frame rate.

There's also more here than the base island. The free "Back to Job" content update folds Job Simulator's office, kitchen, and convenience-store bits into the resort — a meta joke where your robots take their own vacations from work. It's the same grab-and-giggle loop, but it extends the toybox without asking for another dollar. If you liked the first game, this is a free victory lap.

The loop is the loop. Once you've served the smoothies, built the sandcastle, painted the rock, and triggered every robot's best one-liner, there isn't a reason to come back. There's no progression system that pulls you forward, no story that deepens, no world that rewards a second week the way Skyrim or Half-Life: Alyx do. It's a brilliant toy, not a place. The jobs in the first game had a loose narrative frame; here the framing is "you are on vacation," which is relaxing for about as long as an actual vacation is — and then you want to go home and do something with consequences.

That's the honest ceiling. Vacation Simulator is the most polished comedy playground for your hands in VR. If that's what you're shopping for — a gentle, funny, zero-stakes afternoon that shows off why VR is special — it's close to perfect. If you're an enthusiast who already owns a dozen deep VR games and wants the next one to live in, this isn't it. It's the game you install for your cousin, play for an hour, and smile at the memory of. That's not a small thing. But it's not a reason to return.

For a new Quest or Rift owner, or anyone building a headset-loaner library for friends and family, Vacation Simulator earns its spot on the short list — grab it alongside Job Simulator and Beat Saber and you've got the perfect three-game "here, put this on" kit. For the rest of us it's a warm, funny weekend that we'll recommend fondly and rarely replay.

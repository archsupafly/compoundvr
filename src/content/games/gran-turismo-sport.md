I climbed into the cockpit of the Mercedes F1 W08 at the Nürburgring Nordschleife and the first thing that hit me wasn't the speed — it was the windshield frame. In VR, that carbon-fiber pillar sits exactly where it does in the real car, and when I turned into the karussell I could place the apex over the A-pillar the way you do in an actual race car. That's the moment Gran Turismo Sport's VR stops being a tech demo and becomes something a flat screen never handed me.

The VR in Gran Turismo Sport is not Gran Turismo Sport. It's a separate mode Sony shipped on day one — October 17, 2017 — baked right into the PlayStation VR build. There's nothing to install, no patch to wait out, no community profile to hunt down on Discord. You plug in the PSVR, you pick VR Tour from the menu, and you're in the car.

What it actually gives you is three things. VR Drive is a one-versus-one race against a single AI opponent. VR Time Trial is you against the clock. VR Showroom is a place to just sit in the cars and look around. That's the whole menu. What it does not give you is the rest of the game. There is no online Sport Mode in VR. There are no multi-car grids. The campaign, the livery editor, the daily races, the entire meticulously balanced online economy — none of it crosses the line into VR Tour. You are not racing Gran Turismo Sport in VR. You are hot-lapping and duking it out in pairs inside a beautifully rendered cage.

Where it works, it works beautifully. The sense of speed comes from the right place: your brain reading depth and parallax, not a number crawling across a HUD. On corner entry at the Nordschleife you feel the drop into the downhill sections because the world falls away in front of you the way it does from a real cockpit. Running it on a Logitech G29, the force feedback and the headset reinforce each other — the wheel tells your hands what the car is doing and the headset tells your eyes where the car is going. You're placing the car the way you would in real life, not steering a camera floating three feet above the roof.

VR Tour excludes almost everything that makes Gran Turismo Sport the game people actually bought. The headline feature of the whole package — the FIA-certified online Sport Mode ladder, the ranked daily races, the rivalry with strangers across the planet — is flat-screen only. You cannot qualify for a Nations Cup round in VR. You cannot line up against a full grid and feel the chaos of a twenty-car start. The mode hands you a single AI rival or a stopwatch and calls it a day.

That's a real wall, not a nitpick. Gran Turismo Sport was built as an online racing platform first and a driving simulator second, and VR Tour surgically removes the part the game was actually about. What's left is the purest expression of the driving — and nothing else. If you came for the competition, the mode doesn't just disappoint you; it doesn't even pretend to offer it.

The controls are exactly what you'd expect from a PlayStation racing title. You steer with a DualShock 4 or, far better, a force-feedback wheel like the G29. There are no motion controls and none are needed — driving doesn't have a hand-tracking surface to exploit, and the genre lives or dies on the wheel. Comfort is a non-issue: you're seated in a fixed cockpit with real car controls under your hands, so there's no artificial locomotion to fight and no teleport to fuss over. It's about as physically benign as VR gets.

Performance holds up well for a 2017 PSVR title. The headset's 120Hz reprojection does the heavy lifting and the frame pacing stays solid even on the Nordschleife, where a flat-screen Gran Turismo would be throwing detail at you by the truckload. The cost is resolution — PSVR's screen door is visible if you go looking for it — but the sense of being in the car survives the pixel count. It's a clear trade: you lose sharpness, you gain presence, and for hot-lapping that trade is worth making.

It's an official VR mode that is genuinely great at what it does and genuinely limited by what it is. It is not a way to play Gran Turismo Sport in VR. It is a way to drive a handful of cars on a handful of tracks with one opponent or no opponent at all, rendered with a conviction the flat game's cockpit cam never matched.

Gran Turismo Sport's VR Tour is the best cockpit you can sit in on PSVR, and the hot laps make the headset worth it — just don't expect to race the full GT Sport in VR. For Sport specifically, this is a brilliant bonus mode wearing the name of a much bigger game: absolutely worth a session if you already own the disc and a wheel, but not the reason to buy the game, and not the reason to buy a headset.

I've thrown a lot of swords at a lot of demons in VR. Most of them clatter to the floor because the tracking stutters, the animation lags, or my own reflexes give out first. Hellsweeper VR is the first time the swords felt like they were actually mine — weight, reach, and all — and the first time I came out of a session sweating and grinning instead of rubbing my wrists.

## What You're Actually Buying

There is no flat version of Hellsweeper to mod or inject into. This is a VR-native title, built from the ground up for the headset by Mixed Realms — the Singapore studio behind Sairento VR — and published by Vertigo Games. It landed in September 2023 on Quest 2, SteamVR, PSVR2, and Pico, and it plays as full roomscale 6DOF with motion controllers on every one of those platforms.

Because there is no flat game underneath, you aren't weighing a VR version against a better flat one — this is the experience, full stop. You buy it on your store of choice, download it, and you are in hell. No mod manager, no injector config, no profile to hunt down. Comfort is intense by design: wall-running and mid-air backflips mean constant smooth, acrobatic motion, and there is no seated way to play it.

## Sword in One Hand, Hell in the Other

The combat is the reason this game exists. Weapons are bound to one of four gestures and conjured at will — draw a sword from thin air, nock a bow, pull a pistol, and you are fighting with your hands in a way flat shooters never permit. Guns, blades, bows, and magic all live in the same loadout, and switching between them is a flick of the wrist, not a menu dive.

Then the game asks your whole body to join. You run up walls. You trigger slow-motion backflips mid-air to line up a shot. You hang upside down and still find the opening. UploadVR called the combat "addictively varied and thoroughly satisfying" and said the short action vignettes "stand toe-to-toe with any other combat experience in the medium" — and after a few runs I'm not inclined to argue. You swing, you duck, you feel the weight shift when you commit to a heavy strike, and the demons react like they noticed.

## Magic That Actually Means Something

Hellsweeper's seven-deadly-sins bestiary isn't just flavor, and your answer changes depending on what you've summoned. The elemental magic is a real loadout decision, not a cooldown you forget to press. Tossing a fire burst into a pack, then closing with a blade while the survivors burn, is the kind of layered play that keeps a single run from feeling like one trick repeated. The spells scale with your build the same way the swords and guns do, so a magic-focused sweep plays nothing like a blade-focused one, and the unlocks actually change how a fight breathes.

## The Loop Starts to Show Cracks

No game this acrobatic gets a free pass, and Hellsweeper has real seams. UploadVR's reviewers noted that the mission-type variety "could definitely use some expansion," and after a dozen runs I felt it too — the vignettes that make each encounter sing start to repeat their shapes faster than the randomized enemy mixes can disguise them. The sheer range of weapons, traits, and skills is a strength that cuts the other way at first: it "can actually be overwhelming," as the same review put it, and new players will spend their earliest runs unsure what half their unlocks even do.

Neither of those is fatal. The combat is strong enough to carry repetition that would sink a slower game, and the roguelike structure means every run hands you a different toy to try. But if you arrive wanting a story to inhabit or a world that deepens between fights, you'll find a combat sandbox with a hell-themed coat of paint — brilliant to swing through, light to live in.

## Two Bodies in the Pit

The 2-player co-op is where the repetition loosens its grip. You can host or matchmake within your region, and a second body in the room changes the math — one of you pins a brute with gunfire while the other flanks with a greatsword, or you trade off who's mid-backflip lining up the clean shot. Fighting beside someone turns the repeating encounter shapes into a shared choreography problem instead of a solo grind, and the buddy I ran it with spent the first ten minutes just cackling at the physics of two people sword-fighting on a wall.

## Why I Keep Coming Back to Hell

I'll be straight about the ceiling. Hellsweeper VR is not a world you move into — it's a workout you book, and the story is thin enough that I stopped looking for one after act one. What it gives instead is the best pure-combat motion-control loop I've put on a standalone headset or a PSVR2: swords that are actually yours, magic that matters, and acrobatics that make every dodge feel earned. The studio has kept building since launch, and the v2.0 Obscura update added toxic magic and Endless Mode to co-op.

If you want a VR game that makes your body do something real and you don't need a narrative to drag you through it, this is an easy yes. If you're here for atmosphere and story, look elsewhere — but don't blame the combat, because that part is the real thing.

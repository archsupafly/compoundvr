I've played a lot of shooters where the background is wallpaper. Hyper Void makes the background the game. You're a tiny ship inside a tube that bends, splits, and lights up like a laser show someone forgot to switch off, and in VR the whole thing wraps your field of view at 120 frames per second.

When PlayStation VR launched in October 2016, Hyper Void was already on PS4 as a flat arcade space shooter — and within weeks IN|Framez pushed a free update, "VR Levels," that pulls all 29 levels into a headset. This isn't a separate VR edition you buy. The developer added VR to the existing flat game: own the base title (or the Hyper Void Complete bundle) and the VR mode is a free download, same campaign with a VR camera bolted inside the cockpit. Before you strap in, know it's a gamepad game. DualShock 4 only. No PlayStation Move, no motion controls, no reaching out to grab anything — you pilot with sticks and buttons while the headset handles where you're looking.

The pitch is "a space shooter that happens inside wormholes," and it's not marketing fluff. Each level is a twisting corridor — sometimes a tight tube, sometimes a wide kaleidoscopic tunnel — and your ship rides down the center while enemies spawn from the walls, the dark, and occasionally what looks like reality peeling open. You fire forward, bank to dodge, and try to read the next bend before you're already in it. Some levels are light-speed races through enemy lines that play like a sci-fi Battletoads speed bike; others are mazes that test whether you can thread a moving ship through a closing gap.

In the headset, Hyper Void got easier in a way I didn't expect. On a TV the wormholes are a flat light show and incoming fire blends into the noise. In VR, enemies separate from the background — you see the bullet pattern, you see the gap, and dodging stops feeling like luck. When a wormhole spirals, your stomach doesn't, because you're not moving — the tube is. You're a passenger with a gun, and the cockpit stays put: no artificial locomotion to fight, so comfort is a non-issue for an arcade session.

The frame rate is the headline. Most PSVR games of this launch window were fighting for 60 or praying for 90; Hyper Void runs at a native 120 on a base PS4, and it shows. IN|Framez built a custom engine for this game and spent a talk at MIGS 2017 explaining how they hit that rate on PSVR — the effort is visible in every level. The motion is clean, and for a twitchy shooter that readability is the difference between "I can do this" and "why am I dead again." The visuals are the other win: up close in VR, the procedural wormhole skins have detail a TV crushes, and flying through a tunnel folding in on itself while you track three enemy types at once is the moment the game earns its VR tag. It's also free if you already own the game, and the challenge is real — over half the 29 levels throw a boss at you, three of them mega-encounters, and Hyper Mode cranks the difficulty with a faster ship if you want to suffer on purpose.

The thin parts show fast. The story is background noise — something about a virus in the year 2960 — and the game never asks you to care, so you won't. The loop is shoot, dodge, repeat, and the enemy variety thins after a few hours; at roughly three hours to credits, "tech demo" is a fair word for how it ends. Some of the pastel-colored wormhole backgrounds track oddly in VR — when the walls are busy pastels, your eyes grab the wrong thing and the easy tracking advantage evaporates for a level or two. The honest ceiling is that this is a flat shooter with a VR lens. No motion controls, no camera agency beyond your head — you're on rails inside the tube, and the body never gets involved.

If you owned a PS4 and a PSVR this launch window and you like arcade shooters, the free DLC is an easy yes — drop in, fly a wormhole, see if the challenge sticks. If you need your VR to use your hands or your legs, this isn't that ride.

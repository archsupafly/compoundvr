I wanted to wall-run through Nuketown in VR. That's the whole fantasy: Black Ops 2's tight gunplay, the scorestreak chaos, the Zombies round-tense panic — all of it inside a headset with real aim, real reloads, and the sick satisfaction of slamming a fresh magazine home. The bad news is that the actual Call of Duty: Black Ops 2 PC game has no real VR path. The good news is that, if you're willing to give up the original binary, the Zombies side of the game has been rebuilt well enough inside a native VR shooter that it almost scratches the itch.

Black Ops 2 runs on Treyarch's iteration of the IW engine, not Unreal, so UEVR is off the table. There is no official VR mode, no full community mod with 6DOF motion controls on the original game, and nothing on the horizon that fixes that. The only way to run the real PC release in a headset is vorpX, and even that is not a working profile out of the box — it's a manual injection-driver setup that a few dedicated people dialed in back in 2014.

What vorpX gives you, when it works, is stereoscopic 3D and head tracking on a virtual screen. That's it. No motion controls, no hand presence, no VR UI, no comfort options beyond what vorpX itself can fake. For a fast, smooth-locomotion FPS like Black Ops 2, that's a hard sell. The head tracking can be mapped so you turn 1:1, but the sensitivity has to be tuned down aggressively or the motion will tear your stomach out. The Z-buffer 3D adds depth to the environments, but you're still playing with a gamepad or keyboard and mouse while a headset sits on your face. It is the definition of "TV strapped to your head." It runs, it technically counts as "in VR," and if you are specifically curious about the campaign or multiplayer in stereoscopic 3D, it is the only door. But it's intense, fiddly, and not the experience most people picture when they say they want Black Ops 2 in VR.

The better route, by a wide margin, is not the original game at all. It's the fan-made Black Ops 2 Zombies content built inside Contractors VR.

Contractors VR is a native PCVR and Quest FPS with full motion controls, and the modding community has rebuilt chunks of Black Ops 2 inside it: Nuketown Zombies maps, BO2 weapons, perks, wall buys, Pack-a-Punch machines, and the round-based survival flow. You subscribe to the mods through mod.io or grab them from the in-game mod manager, then drop into a map that looks and plays close enough to the real thing that the nostalgia hits immediately. You physically grab your weapon, physically reload, physically pump a shotgun before the next wave crashes through the windows. That is the Black Ops 2 Zombies fantasy in actual VR.

The catch is obvious: this is a recreation, not a port. You're not running the 2012 game. You're running community-built maps and weapons inside a different engine, with all the inconsistency that implies. Some maps are polished; others are rough. Some weapons feel right; others feel slightly off. Setup is moderate — own Contractors VR, find the right mod combination, possibly hop into a Discord for the current best practice — and the whole thing depends on modders staying interested. But the experience itself is real VR. It has 6DOF, hand presence, room-scale movement, and the social co-op energy that made Zombies mode famous in the first place.

Comfort sits at moderate intensity. Contractors VR gives you smooth locomotion, snap or smooth turning, and the usual comfort toggles, but Zombies is still Zombies: tight corridors, sudden turns, and the pressure of a horde closing in. The vorpX route is worse — flat-out intense for anyone with motion sensitivity. Either way, this is not a gentle introduction to VR.

Performance is moderate demand. Contractors VR runs well on mid-range hardware at reasonable settings. The vorpX route adds the overhead of the injection layer on top of a 2012 DX11 shooter that was never meant to render twice, so results vary more with your tolerance than your GPU.

Here's the honest bottom line: there is no clean way to play the actual Call of Duty: Black Ops 2 in proper VR, and anyone promising otherwise is selling a headache. If you are specifically chasing the campaign or the competitive multiplayer, vorpX is the only path and it is a rough one. But if what you really want is the Zombies experience — the rounds, the perks, the wall weapons, the co-op panic — the Contractors VR recreations are the closest thing that actually delivers. They're not the original game, but they're the only version that makes putting the headset on feel worth it.

For another route to the same Zombies itch, [Pavlov's Workshop](/articles/best-pavlov-mods) hosts its own Call of Duty Zombies recreations — Der Riese, Nuketown Zombies, and more — if you already own Pavlov rather than Contractors VR.

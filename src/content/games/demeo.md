There's a moment in Demeo that no flat-screen tactics game can hand you. You lean in over a softly lit table, pinch a miniature rogue between your fingers, and slide her toward a doorway — then you wind up with your other hand and trace a glowing arc to drop a fireball on the goblins clustering beyond it. Nobody is moving a cursor. You are literally at the table, and the dungeon unfolds at eye level.

That's the whole pitch, and it's a good one. Demeo is a VR-native dungeon crawler built like a physical board game. You and up to three other players — online, with cross-play across Quest, SteamVR, PSVR2, and Pico — take turns moving heroes, drawing cards, and resolving dice-driven combat on a modular board. It launched on Meta Quest, and it's the rare VR title that feels conceived for the headset rather than ported toward it.

## A Board Game That Forgot It's Software

The genius is in the physicality. Each hero is a painted miniature you pick up, turn, and place. Spells are cast by drawing gestures in the air — a wizard isn't selecting "Fireball" from a radial menu, he's winding up and flinging it. When a monster dies, the piece topples and gets whisked off the board. When you draw a new tile, the dungeon physically extends in front of you, revealing the next corridor. Resolution Games built the kind of tactile feedback you remember from actual tabletop nights, and in VR that translates directly to presence: you're not managing a grid, you're running a campaign on a table you can lean over.

That presence is why Demeo works where a flat tactics game wouldn't. The camera is fixed at the table edge — you can stand, sit, or use a bit of room-scale to circle the board, but you never walk through the world. Comfort is a non-issue: there's no artificial locomotion to manage, so even players who normally bail on smoother VR games can settle in for an hour.

## Picking Up the Pieces

Setup is the easiest kind. Buy it on your platform's store, install, and you're at the table. No mods, no injection drivers, no config files to babysit. Controls are native motion controllers across the board, and a later update added hand tracking on Quest so you can point and grab without touching a grip, plus a mixed reality mode that anchors the board to your real coffee table.

The actual loop is turn-based tactics with a cozy CRPG heart. Each run drops your party into one of several dungeon modules — the launch adventure plus later ones like the rat-king's sewer realm and a roots-of-evil forest crawl — and asks you to survive floor by floor. Heroes have distinct kits: the rogue scouts and backstabs, the wizard controls space with area spells, the warrior holds a line, the assassin deletes single targets. Combat is dice-plus-positioning rather than twitch skill, which keeps the pressure on decisions, not reflexes.

Where it sings is with people. Demeo has a single-player mode and even a PvP option, but the game was clearly built for the four-seat table — the jokes, the groans when someone rolls a one, the quiet scheming over who tanks the doorway. There's also a "Heroes' Hangout" social space that's VR-only, a place to loiter with your party between runs. This is a game that wants you to have friends in the headset, and it delivers that better than almost anything else on the platform. Resolution has kept building on it since launch too, carrying it to PSVR2, Pico, and Apple Vision Pro without asking players to rebuy the experience.

## Where It Drags

Be clear about what Demeo is not. It's a board game's pace, not an action RPG. If you put the headset on expecting to swing a sword or duck a fireball, you'll be confused — you're moving pieces, not a body. The tactical depth is approachable, even gentle; strategy veterans hunting a Crusader Kings-scale system will find the decision space light, and the runs are self-contained modules rather than one sprawling, branching world.

Solo play works, but the magic is social, and a lone player will feel the absence of the table talk. And while the art is charming — rounded, storybook, legible at a glance — nobody is going to mistake these dungeons for a grimdark epic. That's a feature for most players and a ceiling for others.

None of that is a defect in the experience Demeo is actually selling. It's a digital board game with a headset on, and it's the best one VR has produced.

## The Table Worth Sitting At

Demeo isn't trying to be Skyrim. It's trying to be the best possible reason to put a headset on with three friends and lose an evening to a dungeon, and it nails that. The VR transformation here is real: it takes something flat — a tactics grid — and makes it a place you're standing in. If you like co-op tactics, tabletop RPGs, or just want a VR game your less-hardcore friends can actually enjoy, this is an easy yes. If you need adrenaline and embodied combat, look elsewhere — but you'd be skipping one of the smartest VR-native designs on the platform.

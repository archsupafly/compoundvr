There's a moment about ten minutes into Castlevania: Simon's Destiny where you're holding the Vampire Killer in one hand, cracking it across a skeleton's ribs in first person, and the side-scroller you remember is gone. You're standing in Castle Dracula's entrance hall, and the whip is actually in your hand. That's the pitch: a free, fan-made, first-person rebuild of the 1986 NES Castlevania, and it plays in VR.

There is no official VR mode. What you get instead are two community ways to play it in VR, and both are real. On PC, you run the game's files through a GZDoom build with OpenVR support — the Fishbiter weapon-pack lineage that gives you stereo rendering and a tracked controller in your dominant hand. On Quest, Team Beef's QuestZDoom launcher downloads the mod and runs it standalone, with two-handed grip and haptic feedback. The entire game lives inside a native VR engine.

Both deliver full 6DOF head tracking, stereo depth, and motion-controlled weapons. The Quest build trims normal and specular maps and drops a few props to keep the framerate honest on standalone hardware, but you honestly won't notice the difference while you're cracking whips at skeletons.

Getting there takes patience. The game is a GZDoom total conversion, which means it needs the GZDoom engine and a Doom II IWAD file to launch — you're expected to own Doom. On PC that's manual file placement and pointing the OpenVR build at the right folder. On Quest it's sideloading QuestZDoom through SideQuest, then pulling the mod from the launcher. None of it is hard, but it's a tax: if you've never touched a Doom engine port, budget an afternoon of reading and folder management before the castle opens.

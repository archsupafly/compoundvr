I spent a night in Venice with a crossbow bolted to my forearm and a body pressed against a wall — because that's what Justice asks of you in its first ten minutes. Vampire: The Masquerade – Justice doesn't ease you in. You're a Banu Haqim assassin, the bloodline built for killing, and the headset puts you inside that role before the tutorial finishes explaining itself.

## A Vampire Assassin In Venice

Justice is a native VR game, and there is no flat version of this title to stand next to it. Fast Travel Games, a studio that only builds for VR, took the World of Darkness license from Paradox and made something that exists only in a headset. You don't mod it, you don't inject it, you don't wrestle a profile into cooperating — you buy it on the store and you're in. On Quest, PSVR2, PCVR, and Pico it's the same game on the shelf.

You play Justice (the name is the bit), a vampire hunting the murder of your sire and the theft of a relic, which drags you from New York into the Hecata-controlled back rooms of Venice. It's a first-person stealth-action RPG with open-ended missions fed from a hub called the Inner Sanctum. Think Dishonored with fangs — UploadVR's Gamescom hands-on made that exact comparison, and it earns its keep. You're not shooting through levels. You're choosing when to be seen, when to blink across a courtyard, and who gets to keep their blood.

Locomotion is smooth stick movement with snap and smooth turning plus vignette options; the blink dash covers most traversal.

## The Disciplines Make It

The thing that separates Justice from a dozen other VR stab-em-ups is the discipline system. These are your vampire powers, and they're all motion-controlled.

Cloak of Shadows is your invisibility: hold a gesture and the room loses interest in you, which turns a guarded space into a puzzle you solve by leaning out of cover and timing a dash. The blink dash is traversal — a quick directional warp that closes distance without exposing you. Cauldron of Blood and Shadow Trap let you mess with enemies directly: lure, trap, drain.

Then there's feeding. You grab a body with both hands, pin it, and drain it — and on PSVR2 the Sense controllers sell the moment with adaptive trigger resistance and palm vibration you feel. The crossbow is forearm-mounted; you aim by raising your arm and drawing, which sounds like a gimmick until you're lining up a shot across a Venetian balcony and the tension in the trigger means something. It also supports the bHaptics vest on Quest and Steam, so the bite and the bolt land on your chest if you own the gear.

This is the power fantasy the game promises, and it mostly delivers: you feel like a predator who has decided the room belongs to you.

## A World Worth Haunting

Justice is the rare VR title that builds a world instead of a shooting gallery. The World of Darkness carries decades of lore, and the game drops you into an original story with faction politics, persuasion-based dialogue, and an XP skill tree that genuinely changes how your disciplines behave. You make calls about who to trust and who to feed on, and the writing treats you like an adult in a cruel city rather than a tourist with a headset strapped on.

The campaign runs roughly eight to fifteen hours depending on how many side contracts you chase and how thoroughly you clear each location. That's a real game's worth of content, not a two-hour tech demo with aspirations. For a VR-only release, the scope is surprising — and the fantasy landed commercially too, with the studio calling it their fastest-selling internally developed project.

It also pulled a D.I.C.E. Awards finalist nod for Immersive Reality Game of the Year. The nomination doesn't mean it's flawless, but it means the industry noticed a VR game that tried to be a game first and a headset showcase second.

## The Cracks In The Masquerade

Justice launched with a collection of bugs that tarnished what should have been a clean win — PlayStation Universe called it "hugely empowering" in the same breath it admitted the bugs undercut the experience. 6DOF Reviews described it as "visually striking and ambitious" while being "held back by PSVR2 performance issues and stiff animation."

Performance is the inconsistent part. The Quest build is visibly softer than the PSVR2 and PCVR versions — the standalone build crops detail to keep running, and you notice the difference if you've seen the other builds. On PSVR2 the frame pacing could stutter during busier sequences, which is the last thing you want when a stealth approach depends on reading a room. None of it breaks the game, but it keeps you aware that you're on a VR budget.

The animation work is stiff in places too. Bodies don't always react the way they should, and the physicality that sells the feeding can lapse into awkwardness a beat later. For a game whose entire pitch is "you are the predator," those moments pull you out.

None of this is fatal. The bones are strong, the fantasy is real, and the bugs that shipped have seen patches on the active platforms. But go in knowing the polish isn't uniform, and the headset you own changes how good it looks.

## The Final Bite

Justice is the best vampire-assassin power fantasy VR has handed us, and that's not faint praise — almost nobody else has tried. When the disciplines click and you blink across a courtyard, Cloak of Shadows humming, to drain a guard who never saw you move, it does something flat stealth games can't: it lets your body be the kill. That's the VR transformation the medium keeps promising and rarely delivers this cleanly.

It stops short of essential because the execution is uneven. The bugs, the platform-dependent visuals, the occasional stiff animation — they keep it from the effortless recommendation I wanted to give a game this ambitious. But "Recommended with Caveats" is honest: if the World of Darkness interests you, or you've ever wanted Dishonored from inside the mask, this is worth your time and your headset.

There's a free trial if you're on the fence — a full hour, and your save carries over. Then decide whether Venice at night is yours to take. For most VR enthusiasts who like their stealth personal and their protagonists monstrous, the answer is yes.

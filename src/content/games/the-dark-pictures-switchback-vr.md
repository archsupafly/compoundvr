I almost missed the first scare because I was too busy lining up a headshot, and honestly that's the whole deal with Switchback VR in one careless moment. Supermassive built a rollercoaster that periodically stops being a rollercoaster so you can shoot the monsters off the track. You are not exploring a haunted house. You are being dragged through one at speed with a gun in your hand, and that is exactly the kind of dumb, specific fun this headset is for.

## Until Dawn: Rush of Blood grew up

Switchback is the spiritual sequel to Until Dawn: Rush of Blood, Supermassive's 2016 PSVR rail shooter, and it inherits that game's entire DNA: you sit (or stand) still, the world moves you along a track, and your only job is to aim and fire before whatever's coming around the bend gets you. Because the movement is on-rails, there's no free locomotion for your stomach to argue with — the ride does the walking. What's new is the setting. Instead of the Until Dawn lodge, you're running through locations pulled straight from the Dark Pictures Anthology — Little Hope, House of Ashes, Man of Medan, The Devil in Me — stitched together as a single descent into a nightmare carnival with branching paths.

This is a native PSVR2 exclusive. There is no flat version, no PC build, no other headset to compare against. You buy it on the PlayStation Store, put the headset on, and you're in. Setup is a non-event, which is exactly how it should be for a first-party-style PlayStation VR title — no dependencies, no config, no prayer to the modding gods.

## Motion aiming that actually rewards you

The core loop lives or dies on the aiming, and here Switchback is genuinely good. You aim with the Sense controllers like pistols — dual-wield if you want, point and squeeze. The motion tracking is accurate enough that landing a headshot feels earned rather than lucky, and the game leans into that with score multipliers for clean kills. When a pale face lunges out of the dark and you put one between its eyes without thinking, that little hit-confirm buzz through the controller is the exact thing flat horror can't give you.

The PSVR2 hardware is doing real work, not just showing up. Headset haptics tickle the back of your skull when something crawls past. Adaptive triggers add resistance on the heavier weapons. Eye-tracked foveated rendering keeps the image sharp where you're looking even as the coaster throws the scenery around. None of this is gimmick-for-gimmick's-sake; it's the difference between a tech demo and a ride that knows what it is.

## The horror branding oversells it

Here's the honest catch, and it's the one every reviewer landed on: Switchback is billed as horror, but it's really an arcade shooter wearing a horror costume. The carnival-of-death framing, the familiar Anthology locations, the jump-out faces — none of it ever builds the sustained dread the Dark Pictures name promises. The scares are the funfair-haunted-house variety: quick, loud, and over before they land. If you came for the slow-creep tension of House of Ashes, you will not find it here. If you came to blast your way through a haunted funhouse while the track yanks you upside down, you're in the right seat.

That mismatch matters for who this is actually for. Supermassive knows how to make you afraid — they've done it across an entire anthology. Switchback deliberately trades that for throughput. It wants you firing constantly, not creeping. The result is a better arcade game than it is a horror game, and I'd rather have an excellent arcade game than a timid horror one.

## Short, but it wants you back

The other ding everyone throws is length, and it's fair: this is a short campaign, built around branching paths you can clear in a single sitting. But Supermassive built in reasons to run it again — branching routes mean you don't see every location in one pass, and there's a score-attack loop with leaderboards and unlockable weapons (shotgun, an Uzi-style automatic, and more) that genuinely change how a run feels. The first playthrough is the tutorial; the second is where you start chasing clean headshot streaks and better branching choices. I wouldn't call it deep, but it's not a one-and-done either.

## Worth the headset time

Switchback VR is a PSVR2 owner's arcade shooter, full stop. If you already have the headset and you like the feeling of a well-aimed shot paying you back with a controller buzz and a clean-kill combo, this is an easy recommend — especially at the kind of price a short arcade game should command. If you were hoping the Dark Pictures name meant genuine horror dread, look to the flat anthology entries instead; this one wants you to grin, not grip the armrests. And if you're not on PSVR2, there's simply no way in — it's a native exclusive, and that's the only door.

The bottom line is simple: it's a short, shallow, gloriously confident funhouse shooter that nails the one thing it needs to — making you feel like a goddamn marksman on a haunted coaster. That's worth the headset time, caveats and all.

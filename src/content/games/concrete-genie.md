I spent a Tuesday evening standing in an abandoned fishing town, waving two PlayStation Move controllers like a slightly drunk Bob Ross, and for about ten minutes I forgot the rest of the room existed. Concrete Genie's VR modes do one specific thing, and they do it with both hands: they put a paintbrush in your actual grip and let you bring a dead town back to life, one glowing stroke at a time.

Here's the part that matters up front. This isn't a flat game with a VR toggle bolted on as an afterthought checkbox. Sony and Pixelopus shipped **two** distinct PSVR modes bundled in free with the PS4 game on day one — no paid add-on, no separate download, no "VR edition" upsell. You buy Concrete Genie, you own the VR. That's a rare bit of generosity for a 2019 release, and it sets the tone: these modes were made by the same people who made the game, and it shows in the way the painting feels.

**What you're actually getting**

The first mode, called **VR Experience** (internally "Splotch and the Crystal Canvas"), is a guided little quest. You follow Splotch — a curious genie — down beneath Denska's lighthouse to unlock a mysterious power using your Living Paint in a way the flat game never lets you. You start in a dark, empty cave. Splotch pops up on screen and walks you through decorating the space around you.

The control split is the whole trick: your brush lives in your right hand, and your paint selections show up on a canvas strapped to your left forearm. You pick what you want to paint, then physically sweep the brush across the wall. No menu diving, no cursor. Your arm is the interface. The second stage moves you outdoors, and this is where it clicked for me — you paint flowers, plants, stars, a moon, a sun. Paint the sun and the world warms to daytime colors; paint the moon and it cools to night. The environment literally rearranges itself around your strokes, and Splotch just... hangs out. He rolls in the grass, munches an apple off the ground, never rushes you. There are no fail states. None. You cannot lose a painting game, and that's the point.

The second mode, **VR Free Paint**, is the sandbox. Pick one of four Denska locations and go wild with every Living Paint and genie brush you collected during the flat campaign, all in first-person, both Move controllers live. It's the "stand in the alley and make it yours" fantasy the developers said they wanted to build from the start.

**The catch, stated plainly**

You don't get Free Paint for free-free. You have to finish VR Experience first — that's a hard gate, straight from the devs. And Free Paint leans on brushes you only earn by playing the actual third-person game, so the VR content sits behind flat-game progress. This isn't a standalone VR toy; it's a bonus attached to a bonus. If you buy Concrete Genie purely to paint in VR, you're signing up to play the whole adventure first. That's a real ask, and I'm not going to pretend it isn't.

Comfort is a non-issue: both modes keep you planted in a fixed spot, painting from where you stand, with no artificial movement to speak of — so if a headset fits your face, you're fine for as long as you want to keep stroking.

**Why it works**

The presence is the sell. I'm not using that as a lazy adjective — I mean the specific thing that happened when I painted the night sky and watched aurora ripple across it from my own hand. The flat game is a lovely third-person painter, but third-person is watching yourself be creative. First-person, brush-in-hand, is *being* creative in a space that responds to you. That's a different experience, not a reskin, and it's the difference between a nice game and a "why don't more games do this" moment.

It's also genuinely calm. No enemies, no timer, no score. Splotch never judges your composition. For a headset that too often demands reflexes and stamina, a mode built entirely around slow, deliberate making is its own kind of relief. And it costs nothing extra.

**Where it falls short**

Let me be honest about the ceiling. This is a side mode, not a game. Both VR experiences are short — the guided quest is over before you're ready for it to end, and Free Paint is exactly as deep as your own imagination and the brush set you brought in. One player's biggest gripe landed squarely with me: of the four Free Paint locations, only one really opens up into full three-dimensional painting. The others keep you working in a more limited plane, and after the sky-painting high of VR Experience, the smaller canvas feels like a step back.

There's also the hardware wall. You need a PS4, a PSVR headset, the PlayStation Camera, and — non-negotiable — two Move controllers. If you've got the full PSVR rig, that's just Tuesday. If you don't, this content is invisible to you, and Concrete Genie never left PlayStation, so there's no PC or other-headset path to fall back on. That's the platform you bought, not a quality complaint, but it draws a hard line around who gets to try this at all.

**Who it's for**

If you already own Concrete Genie and a Move-equipped PSVR, this is a no-brainer evening — load it up after the kids are asleep and paint something glowing. If you don't own the game, the VR modes are a delightful reason to pick it up, but know you're buying the adventure to unlock the bonus, not the other way around.

The headset earns its place here not because Concrete Genie reinvents VR, but because for ten quiet minutes it lets you make something beautiful with your own hands in a world that listens. That's worth the zero dollars it costs — and worth the playthrough it takes to get there.

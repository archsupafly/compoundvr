I spent a weekend with Raicuparta's ShipbreakerVR mod in the headset, and here's the thing: the moment I pressed F3 and the salvage yard snapped into stereoscopic depth, Hardspace: Shipbreaker finally felt like the space job it always promised to be. A dead carrier hung in front of me, slowly rotating, and the distance to that hull was no longer a flat-screen guess — it was real parallax. That's the highest this experience reaches, and it's genuinely good.

Then I reached for the cutter. And remembered I don't have hands in this mod.

## A VR camera bolted onto a flat game

ShipbreakerVR is a free community mod by Raicuparta — the same modder behind NomaiVR, the well-loved Outer Wilds VR conversion. It is not an official Blackbird Interactive mode; Blackbird hasn't announced a VR mode for it. This is one person's Unity-XR camera mod, handed out through itch.io and installed with the author's RaiManager tool. You launch the game normally, wait until you're floating in the zero-g workspace, and hit F3. VR turns on. Hit F3 again and you're back on a monitor. The mod runs on the PC build, which is where the game launched.

That's the entire pitch: a toggleable VR camera. There are no motion controls. You don't grab, slice, or shove with controllers. The aiming direction is deliberately decoupled from the head camera — a comfort move so the view doesn't lurch when you glance around — but the actual salvaging still runs through mouse and keyboard, or a gamepad. The author is blunt about the edges: some parts of the game need VR switched off entirely, and a few features stay "a bit broken" even then. Install difficulty is easy; the honesty about scope is the rare part.

## Floating is the win, cutting is the catch

The win is scale and depth. Hardspace's whole loop is spatial problem-solving — where's the thruster you can safely pop, which bulkhead fails without flinging you into the sun, how do you read a hull you've never seen. Doing that with real 3D depth changes how you judge distances and angles. For a game this obsessed with layout, that's not a gimmick; it's the part the flat screen was always fudging. I caught myself leaning to check a weld line on a hull section that was actually three meters away — the kind of instinct the monitor version never triggers.

The catch is that you're a passenger at the interaction layer. You're looking around a real space while your inputs stay on the desk. Cutting a panel, grabbing a component, nudging yourself with the EVA thrusters — all of it is flat input. So the presence is real and the agency is not. You feel like you're inside the salvage yard; you don't feel like you're doing the salvaging.

Performance holds up well enough. The author built in two escape hatches: F5 drops to an ultra-low-quality mode, F6 kills the lights in the current scene. They exist because VR overhead on a Unity title is real, but I rarely needed them for long stretches. They're a safety net, not a daily driver.

## The headset-off tax

The second hit is the stop-start rhythm. UI sections — the corporate menus, the job briefings, anything the mod can't render in VR — force you to press F3, lift the headset, and finish on a monitor. Some players reportedly push through large portions this way; others hit features that stay broken regardless of the toggle. Either way, the one thing VR is supposed to protect — the feeling of being there, uninterrupted — is exactly what leaks out.

This is the line that separates a camera mod from a conversion. NomaiVR turned Outer Wilds into something you inhabit with your body. ShipbreakerVR turns Hardspace into something you watch with your eyes. Both are valuable to the right person; only one is a transformation.

## Worth the float?

If you already love Hardspace: Shipbreaker on a monitor, the mod is a cheap, painless way to feel the scale you've only been inferring. Float in the yard once with two eyes and you'll understand why people keep coming back to it. If you've never played it, start flat — this is a novelty layer on top of a game, not a reason to buy in, and it assumes you already know the systems.

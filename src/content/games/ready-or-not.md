I spent a Saturday night clearing the same office building four times. Not because the AI was smart — because the door frame felt different every time I leaned around it with a rifle planted against my shoulder, and I kept dying to the guy I swore was a civilian.

That's Ready or Not in VR, and it's the closest I've come to actually doing the job short of a real academy. VOID Interactive's hardcore tactical SWAT sim has been the go-to for players who wanted SWAT 4 back since it hit early access, but it was always a flat game. The VR version isn't an official port and it isn't a lazy injector slapping 3D onto a monitor. It's a proper community rebuild — and it changes what the game is.

## What you're actually installing

The way to play this in VR is the Ready Or Not VRO Mod, built by KITT and Mike VRO. This isn't a generic UEVR profile with head tracking bolted on — it's a dedicated full VR mod that treats the game like it was meant for a headset. You get tracked weapons you physically hold, two-handed grips, manual magazine swaps, holsters, gesture support, left-hand aiming, and roomscale movement. It runs through OpenXR or OpenVR, so whatever SteamVR headset you own is covered. It's free, it's actively maintained, and it covers the full game plus DLC.

The mod didn't sneak out quietly, either. The coverage reached well past the usual modding crowd

There's a second path for the framework crowd: a UEVR community profile with 6DOF weapon handling, visible hands, recoil, haptics, and manual reload behavior. It's real and it works, but it's the secondary option here — the VRO mod is the one you want. VOID has never announced official VR, so this community work is the whole show.

## How it actually plays

The gun handling is the entire reason to bother. You grab the rifle with your support hand, plant it against your shoulder, and lean. When a mag runs dry you physically swap it — no button prompt, no animation you wait through. Breaching a door means letting go of the weapon with your support hand to work the handle, which sounds minor until you're clearing a hallway and realize your hands are actually busy. That's the kind of systemic detail that makes a flat shooter feel like a physical discipline.

Comfort sits at moderate intensity

Performance is the real tax. This is a UE4 game with dense interiors, dynamic lighting, and a lot of AI pathing, and pushing it at headset resolution is heavy. You want a high-end rig and you'll still drop frames in the brightest, most open scenes. The mod is demanding by design — it's rendering a full tactical environment with real weapon meshes and interaction systems, not a stripped viewport.

Stability is solid for what most people want: solo play and co-op against AI. The one hard rule is anti-cheat. This is a mod on a game with anti-cheat protections, and you do not run it inside the game's protected online modes. Play offline or only with people you trust on a known config. That's not a knock on the mod — VR modding on a multiplayer-protected game works this way, and the mod's own documentation is clear about it.

## What's genuinely good

The transformation is the headline. Ready or Not is already a game about patience, angles, and the terror of not knowing which figure in the room is going to comply. In VR that tension lands in your chest. You're not watching a SWAT operator clear a room; you're the one craning your neck around the corner. The audio, the dark, the moment a suspect spins with a pistol — it hits differently when your body is the one in the doorway.

The base game earns the VR treatment too. This isn't a thin arcade shooter with a headset welded on. There's real depth: gear loadouts, entry planning, non-lethal options, and missions built around multiple valid approaches. You plan a breach with your hands the way you'd actually plan one.

## What'll grind your gears

Setup is advanced, full stop. This is a PAK-file drop into the game's Paks folder plus three launch flags: -usehmd, -VRTweaks, -VRMappings, run in DirectX 11 mode. If you've modded UE4 games before it's an afternoon; if you haven't, follow a guide and don't improvise. The UEVR option adds its own wrinkle — you have to match the profile to a specific UEVR build, which is fiddly if you're used to one-click installs.

Performance, again, is the other wall. A mid-range PC will run it, but you'll be dropping settings and resolution hard to keep it smooth, and the bright exterior scenes are where it buckles. This is a know-your-hardware experience.

The anti-cheat rule only rules out protected online modes — co-op against AI with friends you trust still works, and that's the mode the game is built around.

## The deal

If you've ever wanted the SWAT fantasy without the arcade gloss, this is the most convincing version that exists in a headset. It asks for a strong PC and some modding patience, but it pays both back the moment you kick your first door.

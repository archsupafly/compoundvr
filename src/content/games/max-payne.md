There's a specific feeling Max Payne sold better than almost any game of its era: kick a door, let the world drop to syrup, and walk forward firing two pistols into a room that can't move fast enough to stop you. I chased that feeling across two decades of flat screens. evilSheep's VR mod for the original Max Payne is the first time I've actually felt it in my arms.

## What evilSheep actually built

Max Payne never had an official VR mode. Rockstar shipped three games — Remedy's two noir shooters and the later Rockstar Vancouver sequel — and none of them ever looked through a headset. For years the only way to "VR" Max Payne was generic injection that dumped a third-person game onto a floating screen with head tracking and no hands. That changed when evilSheep rebuilt the 2001 original as a native-code PCVR conversion: stereoscopic rendering, tracked hands, roomscale movement, and a visible body that follows your head and hands.

This isn't an injector with a profile. It's a hand-written mod that reaches into the game and reworks how it draws, how it aims, and how you hold a gun. You bring your own Steam copy of Max Payne — version 1.05 — run a one-click installer, and a few minutes later you're standing in the cold open with pistols on your hips. If you want the sharper look, the installer can also pull in the "25th Anniversary Remaster" 4K texture pack by andrew22 (included with permission), along with the 4 GB memory patch it needs.

evilSheep gave Max Payne 2 the same treatment earlier as its own separate mod — a full tracked-hands conversion you can find on its [own page](/games/max-payne-2/). This page is about the original game's PCVR beta: [evilSheep/max-payne-1-vr](https://github.com/evilSheep/max-payne-1-vr), first released 2026-11-14. The third game has a teaser and an update video showing Max Payne 3 running in VR, but no build exists yet. Don't go looking for it — it's a teaser, not a playable mod.

The whole thing is free, and it runs on SteamVR, which means any PC-connected headset with two tracked controllers works — PSVR2, Quest through a PC VR link (development testing used PSVR2 and Quest 3 through Virtual Desktop), Index, whatever you've got tethered to a Windows 10/11 gaming PC. This is not a standalone Quest application.

## Holding the guns

The first thing you notice is that the guns are real objects on your body. Pistols ride in jacket holsters on your lower chest; long guns hang on the shoulder holster on your right shoulder. Ammo lives at either hip, painkillers sit at your upper chest, and grenades and Molotovs wait on your left shoulder to be thrown by hand. You draw, you aim with your actual hand, you dual-wield when the moment calls for it — left and right fire independently, so a slow-mo hallway becomes two streams of lead you're steering with separate arms.

Reloading is physical: magazines, slides, pump and shell loads depending on the weapon, plus a dedicated motion for popping a painkiller when the screen goes red — bring the bottle to your mouth and take one. It sounds like busywork until you're crouched behind cover mid-bullet-time, swapping mags with both hands while the room drips around you, and realize no flat version of this game ever let you do that.

evilSheep also solved the problem every third-person-to-VR conversion trips over: where's the camera? The mod gives you a visible body that follows your head and hands, and renders the original graphic-novel cutscene panels and menus on a virtual screen instead of trying to shove flat comic frames into your face. Max Payne's story was always told through those moody, rain-soaked panels and a voiceover that borders on self-parody; seeing them framed on a screen in the world, rather than as a hard cut to a flat panel, keeps the noir tone intact instead of breaking it.

There's more in the hands than just the basics: punches and gun melee, enemy disarming, grabbing and throwing ragdolls (a body you hold up even stops enemy bullets), a physical rifle scope with an eye-lock setting on by default, gun spinning, an optional weapon wheel, stick actions for jumping, crouching and bullet time, and a wrist HUD with weapon ammo displays. Gore — bullet wounds, blood, and dismemberment — is there if you want it, and can be turned off if you don't.

## Bullet time is the whole point

Here's the thing — every VR shooter promises you're the action hero, but Max Payne was built around the one fantasy VR does better than any screen: time slows, you don't. The mod maps bullet-time and the shoot-dodge to roomscale movement, so you can physically lean, sidestep, and throw yourself into a dive while the world crawls. That's not a gimmick bolted on for the headset. That is the game's entire identity, finally matched to a body that can inhabit it.

What surprised me most is how readable combat becomes when I'm the one aiming. The original Max Payne is a tight, deliberate shooter, but in flat it's a cursor and a crosshair. In VR the room is a real space — I'm judging distance to the door, tracking which goon is flanking, deciding whether to burn bullet-time or save it. The physicality turns a twenty-five-year-old game into a tactic I'm performing, not watching.

## The rough edges are real

Look, I'm not gonna lie: this is a mod for a game old enough to rent a car, and it shows. This is a beta, and evilSheep is upfront that there will be bugs and jank — the kind of thing that varies by headset and PC setup, and exactly what a beta is for. You will hit moments where an animation pops or an interaction refuses to cooperate. It's the kind of jank that reminds you someone rebuilt a 2001 engine by hand, not the kind that makes the whole thing pointless.

The dependency list is gloriously short — just the installer and your own game copy, no framework, no loader chain. Setup is moderate rather than beginner-easy: install the game through Steam, run it once, then close it; download `MaxPayneVR-v<version>.zip` from the [releases page](https://github.com/evilSheep/max-payne-1-vr/releases) (and, if you want the 4K textures, the three `MaxPayneVR-4K-Textures-part*.zip` files into the same folder); extract the version ZIP there but leave the texture ZIPs zipped; double-click **Install Max Payne VR.cmd** and press **Install**. Unpacking the textures can take a few minutes, and without the texture ZIPs the 4K option stays greyed out and the game keeps its original textures. Start SteamVR, then launch Max Payne from Steam, and open **Options > VR Settings** in-game to tune the experience.

A couple of practical gotchas are worth knowing up front. If the SteamVR menu is stuck in front of you, Steam is showing Max Payne in Desktop Game Theatre — turn off **Use Desktop Game Theatre while SteamVR is active** for Max Payne in Steam > Library > Properties > General, then relaunch. And if you run "Verify integrity of game files" in Steam, it puts back its own `maxpayne.exe` without the 4 GB patch the 4K textures need; rerun the installer and press **Install** to fix it. For the sequel's conversion, see [Max Payne 2 VR](/games/max-payne-2/), which has its own install and release track.

Performance is a non-issue, which shouldn't surprise anyone — a 2001 game is lean by modern standards. A mid-range gaming PC pushes this with headroom to spare. The demanding part is never the renderer; it's your own reflexes when three guys come through the door at once.

## How it stacks up against the flat game

I don't want to undersell the source material to make the mod look bigger. Max Payne is a genuine classic — the game that taught a generation what bullet time felt like, all noir narration, snow, and slow-motion dives. The VR version doesn't fix the things the flat game was already light on, and it doesn't need to. What it does is take a game I've replayed for the atmosphere and give me a body inside it. The bullet-time dives, the dual pistols, the slow walk through a room you've already cleared in your head a dozen times — those moments land harder when your arms are doing the work.

## The verdict I'd give a friend

If you own Max Payne on Steam (v1.05) and you've got a VR headset wired to a Windows PC, this is a free beta download that turns a noir masterpiece into something you physically perform. The rough edges are real but part of the deal, and they're the kind you report and play through, not the kind that make the idea fall apart. It's the rare mod that doesn't just preserve a great game — it retrofits the one fantasy the original was always reaching for. Skip it only if you can't abide beta jank in exchange for hands on the guns. And keep one eye on the rest of the series: [Max Payne 2 VR](/games/max-payne-2/) is already its own separate conversion, and Max Payne 3 is still just a teaser.

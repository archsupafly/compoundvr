I spent a weekend with High On Life strapped to my face, and here's the thing: the game was always about the guns. Not the shooting — the guns. Kenny screams at you, Gatling Jr. mocks your aim, the knife has abandonment issues. On a flat screen that's a great comedy bit. In VR, with Kenny literally in your right hand and the knife strapped to your left, it stops being a bit and becomes a weird little world you're standing inside of.

## The talking guns were always the point

High On Life is Squanch Games' 2022 bounty-hunter comedy — Justin Roiland's brand of rapid-fire, gross-out, self-aware writing bolted onto a first-person shooter. You're a nobody recruited into alien bounty hunting, and your weapons are characters who complain, flirt, and narrate the whole mess. The shooting is serviceable arcade fare: a pistol that talks, a shotgun that talks louder, a rifle, a whip, a knife. The hook was never the gunplay. It was the gun *personality*.

That distinction matters for VR because it tells you what the headset is actually buying you. Most flat-to-VR shooters are selling you better aiming or better scale. High On Life is selling you presence inside a joke — a brightly colored, deeply stupid, oddly charming alien world where the thing in your hand won't shut up and somehow that's the best part. If the comedy landed on your monitor, the VR version doesn't reinvent the writing. It just moves you from the audience into the bit.

## The UEVR path that actually works

The way to play this in VR today is praydog's UEVR injector plus a dedicated community profile built for the game — the CYB3R-JUNKI3 "High-on-Life-VR" release. This isn't bare injection with you hoping the camera cooperates. The profile does the work: it locks the weapons to your controllers, pulls the crosshair, fixes weapon scale and angle, and frees head and controller movement through the menus, pause screen, inventory, and cutscenes. That last part is the quiet win — High On Life never stops talking, and the profile means you're not yanked back to a flat panel every time a cutscene or shop screen pops.

Setup is moderate, not expert. You install UEVR, grab the profile release, and apply the settings it lists — rendering method, aim method, the decoupled-pitch UI adjustment, UI distance and size are all handled by the mod rather than left for you to guess. If something snags, the Flat2VR community is the place to confirm the current profile version. Recenter is a trigger-pull on both controllers. None of it is one-click, but none of it is a weekend of troubleshooting either. This is a community-maintained project, not a studio product, and it's sitting in the stable-and-quiet zone: it works, it's documented, it isn't under daily active development.

## Holding Kenny changes the joke

This is where the profile earns its keep. Motion controls mean you're not waggling a thumbstick to fire — you're raising your right hand, and Kenny is there, swinging his mouth in your peripheral vision as you line up a shot. The knife lives on your left controller, so melee is a literal stab forward. The aiming feel is the kind of thing that doesn't sound like much in a written description but reads completely differently the moment the gun in your hand is actually your hand. Movement orientation is set to the left controller by default, which the profile notes you're free to change, and the crosshair removal forces you to aim with the weapon rather than a floating reticle.

What surprised me is how much the dumbest parts landed harder. A giant alien shopkeeper looming over you in first person. A talking toilet. The game's constant, breathless commentary about your own performance. None of it is *scary* or *tense* — this is comedy, not survival — but the scale and the proximity make the writing hit like you're in on it rather than watching it. That's the transformation the tier system cares about: VR didn't make the game smarter, it made me a participant in the bit.

## The spectacle holds up

High On Life is a UE5 game, and it looks the part — bold colors, chunky alien architecture, creatures that are gross in a way flat-shaded indie VR rarely attempts. On a VR-ready PC this is a heavy title to run, but it's the good kind of heavy: the engine is doing real work on lighting and density, and the result is a world that feels built rather than assembled. Performance sits in the demanding tier, so a high-end rig will give you the cleanest session, but the visual payoff is the reason you'd pick this over a lighter VR shooter in the first place.

Stability is the other pleasant surprise. UEVR on a modern Unreal title can be a coin flip, but this profile is one of the cleaner ones — I didn't fight crashes or rubber-banding menus, and the documented settings mean you're not reverse-engineering the right config from a YouTube comment. For a community mod, that reliability is half the battle.

## Where it shows the seams

Two honest catches. First, this is still a mod, and that means a mod's responsibilities are yours: keep your UEVR build and the profile version in sync, and don't expect a studio to patch a broken interaction. The community has it in good shape today, but the safety net is a Discord, not a support desk. If you want zero friction, the flat version is one click away.

Second, the shooting underneath the comedy is thin. High On Life was never a deep shooter — the enemies are fodder for the writing more than real tactical threats — and VR doesn't deepen the gunplay, it just makes the gun funnier. If you came for a skill-based FPS, this isn't it. If you came for a bizarre road-trip comedy where the weapons argue with you mid-firefight, the VR version is the best way to take that trip. Comfort is the usual first-person intensity: you're moving through the world on smooth locomotion with the weapon in your hand, which is exactly what VR enthusiasts signed up for and exactly what someone prone to sickness should respect.

## The bottom line

High On Life in VR is a great example of what a focused UEVR profile can do for the right game. The comedy was always the draw, and putting the screaming guns in your actual hands is the kind of transformation that justifies the headset for an afternoon. You'll spend a few minutes getting it running and a high-end PC pushing the frames, but once Kenny's in your grip the flat version feels like a trailer for the thing you're already standing in. If the joke landed for you on a monitor, grab the profile and step inside it — and if you've got a friend who thinks VR is all treadmills and horror games, this is the one to hand them.

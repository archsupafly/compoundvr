The first time Lady Dimitrescu leans down to peer through that wine-cellar door, I physically leaned back before my brain caught up. That's the moment Village stops being a game you're watching and becomes a place you're stuck in.

Capcom shipped a free PSVR2 VR Mode for the PS5 version on launch day for the headset, and it's not a glorified panoramic mode—it's the full campaign, rebuilt around the Sense controllers. You hold the weapons. You aim them with both hands. You stab with the knife manually. Ethan's hands aren't on-screen abstractions anymore; they're your hands, and when a werewolf is in your face, you feel the distance wrong in a way flat screens never delivered.

## What you're actually getting

This is a first-party VR mode, not a community patch, and it shows in the polish. Download the free DLC on the PlayStation Store, put on the headset, and the campaign plays in VR—castle, village, reservoir, the Heisenberg factory. Capcom included comfort toggles (snap turning and a vignette among them), but let's be honest about what this is: an intense survival-horror game where the scariness is the point, not a bug to design around.

The headline trick is dual-wield aiming. Each Sense controller maps to a hand, and you raise a pistol in your right and a shotgun in your left, sighting down them by actually bringing them up. Reloading is a manual motion. The knife is a real blade you swing. For a series that spent decades grounding you in fixed camera angles and tank controls, watching Capcom hand you full 6DOF presence in its most modern entry is the kind of leap that justifies the hardware.

## Scale is the whole game

Village lives and dies on scale—the absurd height of Dimitrescu, the cramped terror of the crib, the sudden vastness of the factory floor—and VR is merciless about scale in the best way. Standing under Dimitrescu in that first castle sequence, the game's signature "oh, I am small and in trouble" beat lands with a physical jolt flat play never earned. The merchant's den, the doll house, the flooded town square: every set piece that flat Village sold through art direction, VR sells through your own body being there.

Performance on PSVR2 is the other pleasant surprise. Capcom could have shipped a soft, reprojected mess and called it VR. Instead the mode holds a clean framerate through the action sequences, and the headset's foveated rendering keeps the center of view sharp where it matters. You'll notice the resolution isn't native-4K-crisp at the edges—this is still a PSVR2 panel—but the image is stable and readable, which matters more in a horror game than pixel density does.

## Where it earns the praise

The reception wasn't quiet. jacksepticeye's VR playthrough cleared 2.6 million views under a title that promised it would "change your life," and a VR-dedicated creator flatly called his "Resident Evil in VR is Perfect." Digital Foundry's tech review framed the mode as a "massive VR upgrade," and the launch-window roundups treated Village as a must-play flagship alongside Horizon Call of the Mountain. None of that is faint praise from a niche that's quick to punish lazy ports. The reason is simple: this is one of the rare flat-to-VR conversions where the VR version is objectively the better way to play, not a novelty tax.

The manual weapon handling is the specific thing that sells it. Flat Village had you tap R2 to fire and watch Ethan's arms animate. VR has you physically raise, aim, and fire, and the act of clearing a room changes from "manage ammo" to "I am clearing this room." That's the transformation the tier system looks for—remove the headset and you've lost something real.

## The honest catches

It's horror, and it's intense. If the genre doesn't do it for you, no amount of presence will convert you—this game wants your pulse up and built the whole experience around delivering that. Sessions are gripping in a way that wears you out, which for this genre is the compliment, not the complaint.

The other catch is platform. The definitive version is locked to PS5 and PSVR2. If you're on PCVR, you're not shut out—praydog's REFramework mod adds full 6DOF and motion controls to the PC build, drop the framework into the game folder and launch through SteamVR or OpenXR, and it's a genuinely good way to play with manual aiming of its own. It's free and open-source and the install is straightforward, but it's a community mod: you trade Capcom's day-one polish for a higher-fidelity image on a strong rig and the occasional tinker. For most PCVR owners who want the experience, it's worth the few minutes of setup.

## Bottom line

This is the VR mode that makes the PSVR2 purchase feel rational. Village was already a great horror game with a memorably weird world; in VR it becomes a great VR horror game, the kind of experience the headset exists to deliver. If you own a PSVR2 and haven't put this on, you're sitting on one of the best things the platform has shipped. PC players can get most of the way there with REFramework. Either way, the castle is waiting, and it knows you're coming.

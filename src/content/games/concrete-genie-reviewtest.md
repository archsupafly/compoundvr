I spent a Tuesday evening waving two PlayStation Move controllers around like a slightly drunk Bob Ross, and for about ten minutes I forgot the rest of the room existed. That's the good news about Concrete Genie's VR modes, and it's also most of the good news, because ten minutes is close to all the structured VR content Sony built.

Two PSVR modes shipped bundled free with the PS4 game on October 8, 2019. No paid add-on, no separate download, no VR edition upsell. You buy Concrete Genie, you own the VR.

**The two modes**

The first is VR Experience, credited as "Splotch and the Crystal Canvas." You follow a curious genie named Splotch beneath Denska's lighthouse, decorating a cave and then an outdoor scene with the same Living Paint from the flat game. Brush in your right hand, selection canvas strapped to your left forearm, so your arm is the interface and there's no menu diving. Outdoors you paint flowers, plants, stars, then a moon and a sun over the sky. Paint the sun and the world warms to daytime colors; paint the moon and it cools to night. Splotch rolls in the grass, picks up an apple, munches it. He never rushes you.

VR Free Paint is the sandbox. Four Denska locations, every Living Paint and Genie brush you collected during the flat campaign, both Move controllers live. It came from a simple pitch the developers described: standing in a Denska alley with a paintbrush, bringing the surroundings to life.

Neither mode is free-free. You have to finish VR Experience before Free Paint unlocks, straight from the devs, and the brushes Free Paint wants come from playing the third-person game. Buy Concrete Genie for the VR and you're signing up for the whole adventure first.

**Where it runs out**

Players put VR Experience at about ten minutes. That's the entire guided experience, and it ends before you've stopped wanting more of it. Free Paint has room to move, but only one of its four locations opens into full three-dimensional painting. The other three keep you working on a flatter plane, which feels like a step down after the sky-painting in VR Experience.

Players have landed in the same place. One Reddit thread described it as a mode that's "very cute but more of an extra than a game," and that matches what I found. The painting is real, the VR content is thin, and the charming flat game underneath is the part that actually deserves your evening.

You'll need a PS4, a PSVR headset, the PlayStation Camera, and two Move controllers. Concrete Genie never left PlayStation, so there's no PC or Quest path. Both modes keep you planted in one spot painting, so comfort isn't a factor once a headset fits your face.

**Who it's for**

If you own Concrete Genie and a Move-equipped PSVR, load it up and paint something glowing. It costs you nothing and it works. Just don't buy the flat game expecting a VR experience, and don't expect the sandbox to hold your attention the way the campaign does. The heart of Concrete Genie is a third-person story about a boy who paints monsters that come alive and help him, and the VR modes can't touch any of that. Take the ten minutes. That's what they're worth.

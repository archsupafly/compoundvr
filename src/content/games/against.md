I came into AGAINST expecting a Pistol Whip clone with a fresh coat of paint. Forty minutes later I was vaulting a desk, sword in one hand and a Tommy Gun in the other, lining up a headshot so it landed on the snare — and I stopped caring whether it was original. It was *fun*, and in rhythm VR that's the only question that matters.

Joy Way — the studio behind STRIDE and Outlier — built AGAINST as VR-only from the ground up. There's no flat version and no "VR mode" bolted onto a monitor game. You buy it on SteamVR, put the headset on, and you are Detective Black, working through a stylized 1930s America where the mob wears fedoras and Dr. Vice runs the city like a cartoon kingpin. The story arrives in comic-book cutscenes between chapters, all hard shadows and sharp dialogue. It isn't Shakespeare, but it gives every level a reason to exist beyond the next high score. It's a native PCVR build — run it on any SteamVR headset, or stream to a Quest over Link if that's your setup. No mods, no community patches, no framework hoops. You install and play.

**What you're actually doing**

The loop is simple to describe and harder to pull off. Enemies and obstacles rush you in lanes, set to music. You slice henchmen with your sword, mow them down with the gun, dodge what you can't kill, and occasionally run up a wall because the rhythm demands it. Everything is scored on timing. Hit your beats and the screen rewards you with slow-mo flourishes and combo multipliers; miss them and the music keeps going without you, which feels worse than taking damage. Locomotion leans on dodge and wall-run rather than free walking, which keeps the intensity moderate, and the tracking keeps up with your swings and shots without fighting you.

This is where AGAINST earns its "Beat Saber meets Pistol Whip" reputation honestly. The sword work has the spatial precision of a rhythm slicer; the gunplay has Pistol Whip's forward momentum and swagger. Brass knuckles are in there too — Joy Way wants you to feel like a one-man prohibition-era wrecking crew, and the motion controls deliver that. You aren't pressing buttons; you're swinging and firing. Performance stays steady throughout — the comic-book shading keeps the draw calls light, so it holds framerate on a modest PC without fuss.

**Where it sings**

The noir comic-book look is the first thing that grabs you and it holds. Joy Way committed to a specific aesthetic: high-contrast ink lines, period suits, and musical climaxes that sync to a boss fight at the end of every chapter. Seven levels, each with three difficulties, means there's a real curve to climb rather than one-and-done novelty. The bosses land precisely because they're timed to the music's peak — you aren't just surviving a fight, you're performing one.

The free Beatmap Editor is the feature that pushes AGAINST past a weekend distraction. You place enemies, weapons, and obstacles against your own music and build custom levels. That's the difference between a game you finish and a game you keep. Pistol Whip has its own community maps; AGAINST hands you the toolbox on day one.

**Where it strains**

Here's the honest catch: AGAINST is a slick imitation of better games. The sword-and-gun rhythm loop works, but it rarely surprises you. Once you've learned to read the lanes, the levels start to feel like variations on a theme rather than distinct set pieces. The comic-book story is charming in short bursts and thin over the long run — you'll remember the boss you shot in time with the horns, not the plot about Dr. Vice.

It also launched in Early Access, which means the structured content you get is the content you get. Seven chapters is a respectable amount of game, but a rhythm-genre player burns through structured content fast and then lives or dies by the custom-map scene. If that scene doesn't populate, AGAINST becomes a tight but finite experience. None of that breaks it. The VR is real, the motion controls are native and responsive, and the presentation has more personality than most indie rhythm titles. It just isn't going to dethrone the games it clearly learned from.

**Who should put the headset on**

If you already love rhythm VR — if Pistol Whip is installed on your machine right now — AGAINST is an easy yes. It scratches the same itch with a different costume and a freer creative toolset. If you're new to the genre, start with Pistol Whip or Beat Saber and come back when you want more noir and more gunplay. Either way this is a native VR build that respects the headset, not a flat game hoping a VR version saves it.

Joy Way didn't reinvent the rhythm shooter with AGAINST, but they built a confident, stylish one. The motion controls are native and responsive, the noir presentation has more personality than most indie rhythm titles, and the beatmap editor means the experience doesn't end when the seven chapters do. It borrows heavily from better games and the story won't win awards, but for rhythm VR fans it's a worthwhile recommendation with one honest caveat: more homage than innovation.

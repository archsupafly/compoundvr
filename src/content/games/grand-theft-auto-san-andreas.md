I spent a stupid amount of my twenties in San Andreas. Grove Street, the countryside, Las Venturas at night — I knew that map better than my own neighborhood. So when a community mod dropped this month that finally puts you inside it in first person with real 6DOF and motion controls, I didn't hesitate. This is the GTA VR experience we were promised, and it didn't come from Rockstar.

## The Mod That Finally Puts You in San Andreas

Holydh's 6DoF UEVR plugin for Grand Theft Auto: San Andreas — The Definitive Edition is the real deal. The Definitive Edition is the 2021 Unreal Engine 4 remaster, and because it runs on UE4, Praydog's UEVR injector can hook into it cleanly. What Holydh built on top is a full first-person conversion: you're not watching CJ from behind anymore, you're standing in his shoes, head tracking and all, with motion-controlled weapons and camera work that survives the cutscenes.

One hard requirement up front — this is Steam-only. The plugin targets the Steam build of the Definitive Edition specifically. If you bought it on the Epic Games Store or through the Rockstar Launcher, it won't inject; you need the Steam version. That's the first filter, and it weeds out a surprising number of people who assume any copy will do.

## What "Full VR" Actually Buys You

Full VR here means exactly what it means in a native title. Six degrees of freedom — you lean, crouch, and look around naturally instead of orbiting a floating camera. The camera improvements carry across both gameplay and cutscenes, so the story beats play out in your face rather than through a third-person wide shot. And the aiming is genuine motion control: you point and you fire. There's no flat-screen crosshair you're dragging around with a stick.

GTA was built as a third-person game, which means the aiming model is the classic stress point for any VR conversion. Holydh's approach maps aimable weapons to your controllers, so shooting reads as shooting. It's the difference between "I'm playing a GTA mod in VR" and "I'm in GTA."

## Driving and Owning the Streets in First Person

This is where San Andreas clicks. Cruising the strip in Las Venturas with the wheel in your hands and the world rendering in proper stereo around you is a feeling the third-person camera never delivered. The scale of the map hits differently when you're standing at street level — the distance between the hood of your car and the next intersection is real distance now, not a minimap dot.

Carjacking, lining up a drive-by, popping off shots with a controller-tracked pistol — the physicality makes the chaos land. San Andreas is one of the most complete sandboxes ever built: three cities, a sprawling countryside, a story with actual weight. VR doesn't just add presence to that, it changes how you move through it. You're not managing a character on a screen, you're occupying the space, and the map's density rewards exactly that.

The conversion's staying power is real too. The VRMods-List directory logs 30-plus hours of playtime on this one, which tells you something: people aren't booting it for a novelty drive and quitting. They're running missions, exploring, and coming back. For a mod, that's the signal that matters more than any screenshot.

## Getting It Running

This is a mod, so setup is a step above double-clicking a store page. The path is: install UEVR, pin the matching nightly build, import Holydh's profile, and inject into SanAndreas.exe. There's no installer that holds your hand through all of it — you're fetching the framework, dropping in a profile, and launching the injector against the running game. If you've done any UEVR mod before, this is the rhythm you already know. If you haven't, budget a focused evening the first time and follow a guide rather than freewheeling it.

The comfort note is honest but manageable: directory listings put this at a moderate intensity, which tracks for a third-person game reconstructed into first person with continuous locomotion. It's not a nausea minefield, but it's also not a slow-paced standing experience. Know your own threshold and ease in.

## The Definitive Edition Is the Price of Admission

To play San Andreas in VR, you go through the Definitive Edition, not the 2004 original. The DE is the UE4 build, which is exactly why UEVR can touch it — but the DE also carries its own reputation. The 2021 remaster drew heavy criticism for its art direction, lighting, and assorted technical rough edges at launch. You're getting CJ's world rebuilt in a newer engine, and some of that rebuilding shows in ways purists still argue about.

For VR purposes, that's mostly fine. The geometry, the cities, the mission structure are all there and intact. But if your mental image of San Andreas is the original PS2-era look, the UE4 version will read differently, and not everyone loves the difference. The trade is non-negotiable: no UE4, no mod, so this is the San Andreas you get in VR.

## What You're Not Getting

Two things are worth stating plainly so nobody walks in surprised.

First, the officially announced San Andreas VR port never shipped. Rockstar and Meta unveiled a Quest 2 adaptation back in 2021, and it was later delayed indefinitely. That version would have been a purpose-built standalone experience. This mod is not that, and there's no sign of the official build arriving to compete with it. What we have is community work on the PC Definitive Edition — and it's good enough that the absence of the official port stings less than it would have two years ago.

Second, the classic 2004 PC release has no full VR route — VorpX gives you geometry 3D and head tracking, nothing more.

## Why This Earns a Spot on Your Drive

San Andreas is one of the great open worlds in the medium's history — three cities, a real story, and enough side activity to lose a month. Putting it in first person with motion controls isn't a gimmick bolted onto a flat game; it changes how the world reads. Standing on a street corner in Las Venturas, looking up at the signage, then walking into a lowrider and feeling the steering under your hands — that's the game doing something flat San Andreas never could.

The caveats are real and I'm not burying them. You need the Steam Definitive Edition, which means accepting the remaster's look. You need to wrangle UEVR, which means a setup evening, not a launch button. And the comfort load is moderate, so this isn't a comfort-first pick for someone still building their VR legs. None of that touches the core truth: this is a complete, playable, first-person VR conversion of a legendary game, made by someone in the community because Rockstar wouldn't.

If you've already got the Steam Definitive Edition sitting in your library and you don't mind a night of modding, this is an easy yes. If you've never played San Andreas at all, there are cleaner entry points to the series — but there is no cleaner way to be inside this particular map. For VR enthusiasts who remember the original and want to walk its streets for real, Holydh's plugin delivers exactly the experience the official port promised and never shipped.

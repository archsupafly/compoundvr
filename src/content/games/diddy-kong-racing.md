I climbed into the cockpit of a 1997 Rare kart racer last night, and the first thing that hit me was how right it felt to be sitting there. Diddy Kong Racing was the N64's answer to Mario Kart with an adventure mode and three vehicle types bolted on — and now a community mod puts the whole thing inside your headset with real head tracking. This isn't a flat game stretched onto a virtual screen. It renders natively in stereo, and the difference is immediate.

## The Diddy Kong Racing VR mod is a native OpenXR patch on a PC port

There has never been an official VR version of Diddy Kong Racing. Nintendo never shipped one for the N64, and the 2007 DS remake didn't either. What exists instead is DiddyKongRacing-VR, a mod by RaYRoD that bolts a native OpenXR layer onto Golden Balloon — akratch's PC port, built from the community decompilation of the original game. You supply your own US 1.1 or EU 1.1 ROM dump; the mod does the rest.

The build is real, and it's listed in the Tier-1 PCVR Mods Installer Hub, which is itself a strong signal that a working path exists rather than a half-finished GitHub curiosity. It ships through RaYRoD's Multiverse VR Hub, a small Windows app that pulls the official Golden Balloon release, applies the VR patch, and remembers your ROM after the first run. Headset on at launch and it boots straight into VR; headset off and it's the normal flat game. That's a cleaner handoff than most community VR efforts manage.

It also isn't a stereoscopic injection job. The whole game renders natively inside the headset with full 6DOF head tracking, and there's an in-game VR menu sitting inside the pause screen where you tune world size, stereo depth, seat height, camera distance, and HUD placement live — nothing needs a restart. For a 1997 kart racer, that's a surprising amount of proper VR plumbing.

## Diddy Kong Racing VR controls are pad-based with full head tracking

Controls are pad-based, and you should know that going in. The mod maps motion controllers to the N64 pad with rumble, but there's no steering-by-hand or item-throwing with your actual hands — this is a racing title with no aim axis, so your inputs are the original's button-and-stick layout, translated. A gamepad or keyboard runs alongside the controllers without friction, and for a kart racer that's the natural way to play anyway. ## The diorama view mode is the standout

Four view modes cycle mid-race with the D-pad, and each keeps its own settings. Third Person is the classic chase camera at life size. First Person drops you into the driver's seat, seat height and slide adjustable. But the one that made me stop and stare is Diorama: the entire track shrinks to a tabletop sitting in front of you, and you watch your kart buzz around it like a toy. It's a genuinely VR-native way to experience a racer — something flat Diddy Kong Racing could never do — and it turns a lap of Ancient Lake into a diorama you're leaning over.

That's the core appeal here. The VR doesn't just present the game; it changes how you relate to it. Being in the cockpit while Timber's Island whips past, or hovering over a miniature race as an observer, is a different stare than a TV ever gave you. The in-game settings let you dial world scale and depth until the proportions feel right, which matters more than you'd expect when a 1997 low-poly kart is suddenly life size in your living room.

## Diddy Kong Racing VR keeps its 1997 rough edges

None of this erases what the game is underneath. Diddy Kong Racing is a beloved cult classic, but it's still a 1997 arcade racer: chunky low-poly visuals, floaty vehicle physics, and an adventure structure that's more fetch-quest than epic. The VR makes it present, not modern. If you're hoping for a simulation-level racing experience in the headset, this isn't it — it's a cheerful, slightly janky nostalgia trip with the year's rough edges fully intact.

The pad-only input is the other honest catch. You're not physically turning a wheel or throwing items; you're pressing the same face buttons and stick the N64 gave you, tracked through a controller the game treats as a pad. For kart racing that reads as natural, but don't come expecting the kind of hand-presence physicality you'd get from a purpose-built VR racer.

## Diddy Kong Racing VR runs on both Quest and PCVR

The same mod spans two surfaces. On PCVR it runs on any OpenXR runtime — Quest Link, Virtual Desktop, or SteamVR — so whatever headset you're on, the patch meets it. On Quest there's a standalone APK sideloaded through the Hub's Send to Quest, no PC in the loop. Both are the same build, so you're not trading features between them; you're just picking where the headset lives.

What you won't find is a PlayStation or Pico build, and there's no store-listed Quest version — this lives in the community Hub, not a storefront. That's the nature of a mod like this, not a mark against the experience itself.

Here's the thing: Diddy Kong Racing in VR is a small, specific joy. It's a real native OpenXR mod of a game people have wanted back for years, and the diorama mode alone justifies the headset time if you ever loved this racer. But it's a pad-driven nostalgia piece, not a new VR racing game, and it asks you to bring your own ROM before it'll even boot. If you're a VR kart-racing fan or a DKR loyalist with a dusty cart, put this near the top of your mod list. If you've never cared about the game, the headset won't manufacture a reason to start.

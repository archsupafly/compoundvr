The first walker I killed in Retribution went down to a rusted screwdriver, and I felt the tip catch on bone before it slid through. That half-second of resistance is the whole reason this series matters in VR. Skydance didn't bolt a headset onto a flat game — they built a melee system where stabbing, choking, and cracking skulls is the verb, and your arm is the weapon. Chapter 1 earned its spot on every best-of-VR list because of that feel. Chapter 2 hands you the same toolset, turns the city up, and introduces a man with an axe who wants you dead.

This is native VR from the ground up. No flat version exists, no mod scene, no compatibility lottery — you buy it on your store, install it, and you're in. It shipped first on Quest 2 in December 2022, hit PICO 4 later that month, and landed on PlayStation VR2 and PCVR (SteamVR) in March 2023, with the original PSVR carried over too. Every version is the same game with motion controls: two-handed grabs, stab, shoot, climb, craft. Comfort is covered — smooth and teleport locomotion, snap-turn, and vignette are all in the menu — so the walking stamina and close-quarters stabbing won't wreck anyone who's put an hour into a headset.

You're back in flooded New Orleans as the Tourist, and the map is wider this time, split into day and night missions that change which threats are loose. The loop is unchanged in the good ways: scavenge, break down junk for parts, craft blades and ammo at a bench, then decide whether the walker in front of you gets a quiet stab to the skull or a loud shotgun blast that draws ten more. The physics melee is still the headline. There's no button-mash kill animation — you push the blade in, the body reacts, and a bad angle means a struggle instead of a kill. A shove sends a walker staggering into a wall, and a well-placed kick buys you the half-second to reload.

The new toys are where Retribution earns its shelf space. The chainsaw is ridiculous in the best way: brace it two-handed, pull the starter, and walkers come apart in a spray you feel in your wrists. Night missions flip the tension because visibility drops and sound carries, so every groan behind you matters. And then there's the Axeman — a recurring human antagonist who closes distance fast and punishes the same stab-and-hope routine that clears walkers. He's the clearest signal that Skydance wanted the human threats to matter more than the horde this time, and in the set-piece confrontations he lands.

On PCVR the whole thing holds 90fps through packed streets and never breaks the spell. The PSVR2 build, with the Sense controllers and headset rumble, is the version I'd point a console player at — the haptics on a chainsaw pull or a walker's grip on your arm are exactly the kind of feedback that makes the headset feel earned. This is a heavy title for what it is: dense walker crowds, real-time physics on every body, and a big environment. A mid-range PC runs it; a high-end rig pushes it without reprojection. Quest 2 at launch is the exception, and not the good kind.

Here's the honest catch. The Quest 2 build that launched first struggled — frame dips, hitches during heavy combat, and the usual day-one bugs that come with a big native release. Skydance patched, but if you're buying on Quest 2 you are buying the compromised version of an experience that sings on a wired headset. And the deeper critique from players who loved Chapter 1 is fair: Retribution refines rather than reinvents. The world is the same drowned city, the bench is the same bench, the melee is the same melee. It's bigger and it's meaner, but it does not move the medium the way the first game did. If you came looking for a sequel that redefined what VR survival horror could be, you'll find more of the best thing we already had, not a new high-water mark.

That's a narrower complaint than it sounds. "More of the best thing" is still the best thing. The physics combat has no real equal in VR horror — nothing else makes a simple walker kill feel this physical, and the Axeman fights plus night missions give the campaign shape beyond scavenging loops. The story is pulp, but it gives you reasons to move through the map, and the flooded-street atmosphere with headphones on is the kind of presence that justifies the headset for an evening.

If you played Chapter 1 and liked it, Retribution is a continuation you'll want, especially on PCVR or PSVR2 where it runs clean. If you skipped the first one, this is a fine place to start — it stands on its own, and the melee alone is worth the install. Just know what you're buying: a bigger, bloodier, slightly rougher chapter of the same game, not a reinvention. On the right hardware, that's more than enough.

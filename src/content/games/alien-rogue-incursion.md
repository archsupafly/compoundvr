Finally, an Alien game that treats VR like the point and not the afterthought. Alien: Rogue Incursion is not a modded flat-screen port, not a vorpX science experiment, and not a framework rescue job. It is a native VR survival-horror shooter built from the ground up by Survios on Unreal Engine 5, and it is the closest any of us are getting to actually being trapped in a Weyland-Yutani nightmare with a xenomorph between us and the exit.

That matters because Alien is almost too perfect for VR. The narrow corridors, the motion tracker, the moment you hear a ceiling vent rattle and realize you are not alone — that stuff only works if the game is built around your body being in the space. Rogue Incursion gets that. You hold the motion tracker in one hand and a pulse rifle in the other. You physically reload. You reach to your chest, hips, and back for ammo, tools, and weapons through an on-body inventory that several reviews call among the best in VR. When a xenomorph drops from a duct, you do not press a button to look up. Your actual head tilts back. Your actual hands fumble for the trigger.

The game launched on December 19, 2024, for PlayStation VR2, SteamVR, and Quest 3. The Quest 3/3S version slipped to February 13, 2025, and it shows. UploadVR's platform comparison makes the gap clear: PSVR2 and PCVR carry the atmospheric lighting, dense environments, and creature detail that make the horror land, while the Quest version trims enough fidelity that some of the dread gets shaved off with it. It is still the same game mechanically, and it still works, but if you own a PSVR2 or a decent PC headset, that is where this belongs.

Comfort is the real gatekeeper. Rogue Incursion uses smooth stick locomotion by default and, as of my reading, still has no teleport option. A Steam Community thread asking for teleport is essentially the community waving a white flag at the developers. There are no comfort crutches strong enough to save a motion-sensitive player from a game designed to make you sprint backwards while firing at something climbing the wall. The horror tension plus the smooth movement puts this firmly in moderate-to-intense territory. If VR smooth locomotion has ever made you queasy, this one will test you.

Setup is as simple as it gets for a native VR game. Buy it on your platform store, install, and play. No sideloading, no injection profiles, no launcher tricks, no Discord hunts for the right mod version. That alone puts it in rare company among the games I have covered in this series. It is also a first for the Alien franchise: the first actual VR game in the canon, set between the original Alien and Aliens, starring Zula Hendricks, a Colonial Marine pulled from the comics.

There is an odd footnote here. In June 2025, Survios announced it was stopping VR work on Rogue Incursion to focus on a flat-screen port, the Evolved Edition, which shipped September 30, 2025, for PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Steam, and Epic. That is the opposite direction most VR-adjacent articles cover: this time the flat version is the spin-off, not the original. For VR players, the message is mixed. On one hand, the native VR version is complete and playable. On the other hand, Survios has publicly deprioritized it, so do not expect major new VR content. What is on the store is what you get.

Performance demand is moderate-to-high depending on platform. PCVR and PSVR2 push the UE5 lighting and creature density without much mercy; the Quest 3/3S version is clearly optimized down. A mid-range gaming PC handles the SteamVR version well at reduced settings, but this is not a lightweight Quest title you can run for three hours on battery without noticing the drain.

The verdict is straightforward. Tier A. This is a real VR game from a real studio with real production value, and it delivers the Alien fantasy better than anything else in a headset. The platform choice matters — PSVR2 or PCVR is the right home for it — and the comfort threshold is real. But if you can handle smooth locomotion and you want to know what a xenomorph actually feels like in your personal space, this is the one.

I squeezed the trigger on the pistol in my left hand while my right hand racked the slide on a fresh one, and the guy in front of me lost an arm at the shoulder, stumbled, and got launched backward into a wall by the round I put through his chest a half-second later. Nobody told me to do any of that. The game just handed me two guns, a slow-mo button, and a room full of men built out of wet noodles and consequences. That's HARD BULLET in one exhale.

This is a VR-native title from GexagonVR — not a flat game with a VR toggle bolted on, not a mod, not a profile. It was built for the headset from the ground up and launched into Early Access on Steam. If you've got a PCVR setup, the "way to play in VR" here is just: buy it, install it, put the headset on. It's also made the jump to Quest and PSVR2, so the chaos isn't locked to a tethered PC. There is no mod scene to chase, no injector to configure, no dependency list to untangle. For a VR enthusiast who's used to earning their fun through three hours of setup, that alone is a relief.

And it shows its independent-development bones in the best way: the ambition is weird and specific. Most studios wouldn't ship a game that's barely a game — no campaign, no story, no progression system you're obligated to care about. HARD BULLET is a physics sandbox dressed as an action movie, and it knows exactly what it is.

## What you're actually holding

The core loop is simple to describe and hard to stop doing: spawn enemies, grab weapons, kill them with as much style as your reflexes allow. The genius is in the input. Both controllers are tracked, and both are live at once — you dual-wield from the first second. Reloading is physical: magazines drop, slides rack, you shove a new mag home with the motion that feels right, not the one a prompt tells you to use. I spent my first ten minutes just picking up pistols, ejecting mags mid-air, and laughing because the system let me.

There's a slow-mo toggle that turns every firefight into a choreographed sequence. Hit it, and the world drops to a crawl while your hands stay fast — so you can line up a two-gun execution, throw a knife into someone's throat, or just watch a ragdoll ragdoll in beautiful, stupid detail. It's the closest I've come in VR to feeling like the director of my own terrible action film.

Beyond guns, you get melee — pistols-as-blunt-objects, swords, anything you can grab — and a throw system with real weight behind it. I chucked a chair at a guy and he folded around it. The dismemberment physics are the headline act: limbs come off, heads come off, and the game does not shy away from it. There's a gore toggle in settings if you'd rather keep the blood at a manageable level, and I appreciate that the option exists instead of being forced one way.

## The part where I admit it's janky

HARD BULLET is an Early Access physics sandbox built by a small team, and it feels like one. The enemies are not smart — they walk at you, they shoot, they die in ways that range from satisfying to baffling. There is no narrative pulling you through, no mission structure giving your chaos a point. If you need a game to give you a reason to keep playing, this one shrugs and says "you're the reason."

The physics, which are the entire appeal, are also the source of the rough edges. Bodies clip. Weapons occasionally refuse to seat in a hand the way you expect. A throw goes wrong and launches a corpse through a wall instead of at the guy you aimed at — funny the first ten times, slightly annoying the eleventh, when you meant it. None of this breaks the experience, but it means the game lives or dies on whether you find the sandbox itself endlessly entertaining. I do. You might, after an hour, decide you've seen the trick.

Performance sits in the moderate-demand band. It's a Unity title leaning hard on physics simulation, and dense crowds of ragdolls will ask more of your rig than a flat shooter would. On a mid-range PC it runs cleanly with reasonable headroom; push the enemy count and the physics load and you'll feel it. It's not a beast, but it's not a potato game either — the simulation is doing real work every frame.

Comfort is worth a sentence: it's a full-motion sandbox, and the chaos plus free movement is intense but not a sickness machine.

## Why it earned its keep on my drive

I keep coming back to one question: is this worth putting a headset on for? For HARD BULLET, the answer is yes, with a condition — and the condition is you.

What it does well, it does better than almost anything else in VR. The physicality is the point. Your hands are doing real things — racking slides, catching thrown weapons, steadying a two-handed aim while time crawls. That's presence you can't get from a flat screen, and it's the kind of presence the best VR earns rather than borrows. The slow-mo system turns gunplay into choreography, and the dismemberment isn't shock-value gore so much as it's the physics engine finally having something honest to say about violence. You did that. The ragdoll didn't decide to fall apart — your shot did.

The community clearly agrees. Six and a half thousand Steam reviews land at Very Positive, and the VR coverage isn't a single launch-week spike — it's repeated, sustained enthusiasm from channels that cover this space seriously, with the "John Wick VR we needed" framing showing up over and over because it's the most accurate shorthand that exists. That's not a tiebreaker I'm leaning on; it's a signal that the fun I'm describing isn't just my weird taste. People who put headsets on for a living keep coming back to this one.

HARD BULLET is for the VR owner who's tired of being handed a story and wants to be handed a gun and a room instead. If you've ever watched a hallway shootout and thought "I could direct that better," this is your headset. It's not for someone chasing a campaign, or a tight competitive shooter, or a polished AAA production — go elsewhere for those, and you won't be disappointed here because you'll know what you're buying. GexagonVR has kept the thing alive with steady updates through Early Access, so the jank you meet today isn't the jank that shipped, and it keeps getting better at being itself.

Put the headset on, grab two pistols, hit slow-mo, and find out if you're the action hero you think you are. I was, for about forty minutes, until a ragdoll stole my gun and I laughed harder than the kill deserved. That's the whole game, and for the right person, it's exactly enough.

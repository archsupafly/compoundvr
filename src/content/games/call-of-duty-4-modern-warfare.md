There's a sequence in the "All Ghillied Up" mission where you go prone in waist-high grass and watch a convoy crawl past, your spotter whispering which targets actually matter. On a monitor it's a great scripted moment. In the headset, with a rifle you're actually holding and a scope you raise to your eye, it stops being a cutscene-with-guns and becomes a held breath. That's the entire pitch for playing Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare in VR, and the community mod that makes it possible mostly delivers on it.

## What you're actually getting

Call of Duty 4 never got an official VR mode — not on PC, not on any headset. What exists instead is KisakCOD VR, a free, open-source OpenXR conversion of the original 2007 Windows release. It bolts stereoscopic rendering, 6DoF head tracking, and motion-controller weapon handling onto the single-player campaign. You supply a legitimately installed copy of the 2007 game; the mod supplies everything that makes it VR. It's in active public beta, which matters for how you should approach it.

There's also a second way in: a vorpX profile that's been sitting in the injection-driver community for years. Treat that as the fallback it is. vorpX gives you stereoscopic 3D and head tracking by injecting into the flat game — no motion controls, no room-scale weapon handling, you're still aiming with a gamepad or mouse while wearing the headset. It's a way to look around the campaign in 3D, not a way to be in it. If the native mod is the real answer, vorpX is the "well, at least it runs" answer.

## How it plays

The mod hands you the campaign with motion controllers mapped to your rifle. Aiming is two-handed with calibrated relative steering, so shouldering the weapon and lining up a shot feels like shouldering a weapon. There's a physical scope — raise it and the world narrows the way it should — and aim-down-sights works. Snap-turn is supported, but this is a smooth-locomotion military shooter, so plan for that. The HUD gets placed in VR space and can be scaled down for wide or canted-FOV headsets, which is the kind of detail that tells you someone actually wore a headset while building this.

A 2007 game isn't going to tax a modern GPU even rendered twice for stereo — I ran it on a mid-range PC without a hiccup. The catch isn't horsepower, it's polish. This is beta software with known open issues and per-headset quirks. On a Quest 3 streamed over Virtual Desktop it validated clean: stereo, tracking, controllers, firing, ADS, two-hand aiming all passed. OpenVR hand paths on an Index worked too. But "validated on one config" and "rock solid on yours" are different claims, and the gap is exactly where the beta label lives.

## What's genuinely good

The campaign is the draw, and it holds up as a thing to inhabit. CoD4's single-player is a string of famous set-pieces — the ship-board breach, the nuclear aftermath, the sniper overwatch — and VR doesn't just resize them, it changes who you are in them. You're not watching Price clear a room, you're the person with the rifle. The physical scope on the overwatch sections alone justifies the mod for anyone who remembers why those levels worked.

Motion-controlled aiming is the difference between a tech demo and a shooter. When you're working a corner with both hands on the stock and the scope, the gun stops being a reticle and becomes a tool you're manipulating. That's the presence dividend, and for a game this tightly designed, it lands hard.

## Where it falls apart

It's a beta, and it reads like one. Expect bugs, expect a config tool you'll actually need to open, expect the odd texture or interaction that doesn't survive the translation into VR. The author is iterating fast — that's a point in its favor — but "fast iteration" and "finished" are not the same status.

The bigger structural limit: single-player only. The mod covers the campaign and stops. If your CoD4 attachment is the multiplayer — the sniper lines, the killstreaks with strangers — none of that is here. This is a solo romp through a 2007 story, not a return to the lobbies.

And you have to own the original 2007 Windows release. The mod carries no game data; you're bringing the disc or the Steam install. That's standard for this kind of conversion, but it's a hard gate: no copy, no VR.

Then there's the vorpX path, and I'm not going to soft-pedal it — as a way to play CoD4 in VR it's thin. You get depth and head look, you lose everything that makes VR matter for a shooter. Use it only if the native mod won't run on your setup, and go in knowing you're getting a flat game with a headset strapped on, not a VR game.

## Should you bother

If you loved this campaign and have ever wondered what it felt like to actually be in it, this is the version that answers that. Bring patience for beta rough edges and your own copy of the game, and the trade is worth it.

Call of Duty 4: Modern Warfare was never supposed to be a VR game, and the community mod that fixes that is still finding its feet. But the feet are under a legendary campaign, and the motion-controlled shooting is real, not faked. Bugs and the single-player-only scope aside, this is the way to stand inside one of the best shooters ever made.

I stepped off the edge of a Florentine rooftop, arms out, and fell. Not a cutscene fall — my own fall, my body committing to the leap before my brain caught up. Ezio's shoulders rolled, the hidden blade caught the haystack, and I was still standing in my living room grinning like an idiot. That's the pitch for Assassin's Creed Nexus VR in one motion: a decade of watching these jumps from a couch, now something my body actually does.

This isn't a port with a VR camera bolted on. Red Storm Entertainment built Nexus from the ground up for the Quest, and it shows in the right places. There is no flat-screen version, no PC build, no emulator path — the only way to play is the official Meta Quest release, and it's a complete game, not a tech demo. You get about twenty hours across three protagonists: Ezio, Kassandra, and Connor, all voiced by the actors you remember. For a standalone headset title that's a sprawling campaign, and it's the closest the series has come to letting you inhabit the fantasy instead of directing it.

How it plays is where the build earns its keep. Combat is hand-tracked — you draw the hidden blade by reaching to your wrist, swing a sword with your actual arm, nock and loose a bow by pulling the string. There's no button-mash auto-kill; you commit to the strike and the game reads your motion. Climbing is grab-by-grab: you reach for the next handhold, pull yourself up, and the world slides past your shoulders. It's the parkour system the series always implied finally made physical. Early on I over-reached for a ledge and slipped, and the panic of the fall was real before the catch saved me. That's the texture flat Assassin's Creed never had.

The three-protagonist structure is the smartest call here. Ezio's missions are pure Renaissance sneaking and rooftop chases, Kassandra brings the Sparta-set open combat, Connor wraps it with the colonial America setting. None of them is a full open-world like the mainline entries — these are tighter, mission-shaped levels — but hopping between three distinct eras and voices keeps the twenty hours from dragging. Android Central called it what an Assassin's Creed game is supposed to be, and the hand-tracked movement is the reason why.

Comfort is handled with unusual care for a Quest title: four presets run from fully immersive (continuous locomotion, free turn) down to teleport-with-vignette for the motion-sensitive, plus a fear-of-heights floor grid and aim stabilization, so sickness is a settings toggle rather than a wall. I ran the immersive preset and the rooftop leaps were the only moments that pushed me; everyone else has headroom below that.

The weaknesses are mostly the ones you'd expect from a standalone headset game, not from the design. The visuals are Quest-scale — readable, styled, and a long way from the PC games' density. Crowds thin out, draw distances compress, and the "open" spaces feel authored rather than alive. That's the chipset talking, not laziness, and after ten minutes I stopped looking for the missing fidelity and started enjoying the movement. The bigger creative caveat is scope: this is a spinoff, not a mainline entry. The missions are good but contained, the systems are streamlined, and if you came wanting the full economic sandbox of a numbered Creed you won't find it. What's here is polished and confident, just smaller than the name on the box implies.

It's also Quest-only. If you're on PCVR or PSVR2, there is no path in — no streaming workaround, no PC release, nothing. That's a reach limit, not a quality judgment; the game is exactly as good as it is on the headset it targets. For the Quest owner it's a non-issue, and the Quest 2, Pro, and 3 all run it.

Where it lands is simple. Nexus is the first Assassin's Creed that feels like Assassin's Creed in VR rather than a game about Assassin's Creed. The hand-tracked blade and the physical parkour are the transformation, and twenty hours of three protagonists is a generous helping of it. UploadVR handed it their 2023 Quest Game of the Year, and having put the time in, that's not charity — it's the most fun the series has been in a headset. If you own a Quest and ever liked this franchise, this is the one to grab.

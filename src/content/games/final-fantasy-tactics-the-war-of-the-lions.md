There is a very specific kind of comfort in sitting down to play a grid-based tactics RPG the same way you might read a book in a hammock. Final Fantasy Tactics: The War of the Lions is slow, deliberate, and built around thinking three turns ahead. In VR, through PPSSPP VR, it becomes exactly that: a portable classic on a floating stereo screen in a dark virtual theater, controlled with a gamepad or emulated touch input, running at a compatibility rating the emulator calls Perfect. It is not a VR transformation of Ivalice. It is a really nice way to play a PSP game on your headset.

That distinction matters, because this is a single-path situation. The War of the Lions was released for the PSP in 2007, later ported to iOS and Android, and never came to PC. That immediately removes the two tool families most VR articles lean on. There is no Windows executable for PC injection drivers to hook into, and the engine is Square Enix's proprietary PSP tech, not a mainstream PC engine, so framework-based injectors are irrelevant. No community mod exists to magically pull a 2D isometric sprite board into 3D space with motion controls. The only real option is the official PPSSPP VR build, an OpenXR fork of the PSP emulator for standalone headsets.

PPSSPP VR landed in its modern form in 2022, when Luboš V's OpenXR work merged into the project, adding 6DoF head tracking and stereoscopic rendering. The standalone APK runs on Quest 2, Quest 3, Quest Pro, Quest 3S, PICO Neo3, PICO 4, PICO 4 Ultra, and a few other Android-based headsets. You sideload it, load your legally dumped PSP ISO, and play. For The War of the Lions specifically, PPSSPP's compatibility database marks the USA and Europe versions as Perfect. That is not marketing language; it means the game boots, runs, saves, and plays as intended with no known major issues. The only wrinkle I would note is a reported Vulkan intro crash, which is minor enough that most users switch to OpenGL and never think about it again.

What you get inside the headset is a fixed virtual screen with depth. The 2D isometric battlefield sits in front of you like a virtual monitor, slightly curved, rendered twice so it has a stereo sense of space. You can lean in, look around the void, and position the screen however your neck prefers. The game itself is unchanged. You are still moving cursor tiles, selecting abilities, watching attack animations, and groaning when a 95-percent hit chance misses. There is no world-scale presence, no motion-controlled sword swings, no walking through the castles of Ivalice. The screen is just bigger, more private, and easier to disappear into.

Comfort is the strongest argument for this route. The War of the Lions has no camera whip, no smooth locomotion, no artificial turning, and no motion at all that does not belong to the game's own menu transitions. It is very comfortable in VR, probably one of the safest classic games to try if you are sensitive to motion sickness. You can play a full battle without your inner ear filing a complaint.

Setup burden is moderate. Sideloading the APK through SideQuest or ADB is the hardest part, and that is a ten-minute task if you have done it before. After that, loading the game is the same as any PSP emulator. Performance demand is low. The game is a 2D sprite RPG from 2007; even the Quest 2 can run it without raising its pulse. Battery life becomes your real session limiter.

The honest verdict depends on what you want. If your goal is to experience Final Fantasy Tactics as if it were built for VR, stop here. It cannot be done. The source material is a 2D isometric tactics board, and no injector or emulator is going to invent depth data that does not exist. But if your goal is to replay one of the best tactical RPGs ever made in a comfortable, private, big-screen headset setup, PPSSPP VR is a genuinely pleasant way to do it. It works, it is stable, and it respects the game.

I would call this a niche recommendation. Buy it if you already love the game and own a Quest or PICO. Skip it if you are hunting for a native-feeling VR strategy game, because this title will never become one.

I put the headset on, stepped out of the house on Evergreen Terrace, and the first thing I did was walk up to Homer's car and grab the wheel. Not a menu prompt — I reached out, the wheel appeared in my hands, and suddenly I was driving down a Springfield street that wrapped all the way around me. That's the pitch for The Simpsons: Hit & Run in VR, and it's a better pitch than a 2003 licensed game has any right to be.

## What this actually is

There's no official VR mode and no publisher-backed port. What exists is a community-built standalone VR conversion for Quest 3, reconstructed from an existing Android version of the game and reworked for OpenXR. You sideload an APK, copy your legally owned PC game files into a folder on the headset, and the whole thing runs standalone — no PC tethered at playtime. The project is free; what costs you is the original copy, which has been delisted from digital storefronts for years, so you need one you already own or a secondhand disc.

That's the first honest catch. If you've never sideloaded anything, the copy-files-and-install-APK sequence is a moderate hurdle, and the delisted-source requirement means this isn't a casual store pickup. But the steps are documented and the payoff is real, and once it's on the headset it just runs.

## How it plays

The port maps the original's third-person action-and-driving hybrid into room-scale VR. You move through Springfield on foot with room-scale tracking and recentering, and when you get behind the wheel the game hands you a virtual steering wheel you actually grip and turn. Missions, collectibles, the phone-booth objectives, the candy-from-babies absurdity — it's all there, rendered in stereoscopic 3D with the HUD pulled into the world instead of pasted flat across a screen. Comfort is handled with a sane set of options — seated mode, both smooth and snap turning, adjustable refresh rate and render scale — and I never felt the game working against me on it.

On Quest 3 standalone the 2003-era engine is featherlight. Performance is efficient, holding framerate without the thermal drama you'd brace for from a heavier conversion. You're not fighting the hardware to stay in Springfield.

## Driving is where it clicks

Here's the thing: Hit & Run was always a love letter to the show's world, not a deep game. The missions are thin arcade loops — drive here, collect that, chase this — and they were in 2003. What VR adds isn't deeper systems; it's presence. Springfield is one of the most recognizable fictional places in pop culture, and standing in it, turning to watch a car barrel past, reaching for the wheel — that's the "I can't believe I'm inside this" moment the medium exists for. The port even bumps materials and lighting and layers in cascaded shadow maps, so it looks sharper than the flat original ever did on a television.

The on-foot sections are more fun than they have any right to be. Third-person VR character control feels awkward in bad ports, but the room-scale movement and recentering make walking the neighborhood natural, and the humor lands harder when you're physically poking around the world instead of watching it through a camera. Grabbing a vehicle mid-stride and snapping into the driver's seat with the wheel in your hands is the single best trick here, and it doesn't wear out.

## The honest catch

This is still a community port of a 2003 game, and the age shows in the systems. Mission variety is shallow, the satire is kid-gloved licensed fare, and there's no narrative arc reeling you forward — it's a sandbox of gags you wander until you've seen every corner. The VR conversion is impressive but it isn't a from-the-ground-up VR title; you'll hit occasional jank, and the platform story is narrow. Quest 3 is the verified, fully playable target. A PCVR build is circulating in beta on the project's Discord for anyone who'd rather run it tethered, but that's exactly as settled as a Discord beta sounds — worth knowing about, not something to plan hardware around.

None of that undermines the core trick. The world is the draw, and the world is here, in your living room, wheel in hand.

## Should you put the headset on

If you grew up quoting this game or you just want to wander a cartoon you know by heart, this is an easy yes with one condition: you need a Quest 3 and a copy of the PC version to feed it. Everyone else should keep an eye on the PCVR beta rather than buy hardware on hope. Hit & Run in VR doesn't reinvent the game — it finally lets you stand inside it, which for a Springfield fan is most of the battle.

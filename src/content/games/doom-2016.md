I loaded into the first corridor of the UAC facility, a demon shambling toward me, the pipework on the left, the body on the floor, the hellspawn filling the frame. I reached for the shotgun — and my hand closed around it. The pump racked against my controller grip. That is the whole difference KHARVOX makes.

id Software never shipped a VR mode for Doom (2016), and the game's id Tech 6 engine — Vulkan and modern OpenGL — kept injection tools at arm's length. For a long time the only ways in were stereoscopic-3D wrappers that put the game on your face while your thumbs did the work. KHARVOX, by CactusVRStudios, is different: a total VR conversion of the original campaign with room-scale 6DoF movement, tracked motion controllers, first-person stereoscopic rendering, and OpenXR. It is free, but it requires a legal Steam copy of Doom (2016), Windows 10 or 11 x64, a working OpenXR runtime, and tracked controllers. Install is moderate — extract the launcher, point it at your Steam copy, and launch through KharvoxLauncher.exe. Expect a few clicks and calibration before the facility opens.

## Rip and tear, with your own hands

The UAC corridor now reads as a real space because your body is inside it. You lean around corners, step back from explosions, and physically raise your weapon hand to steady the shotgun. The demons have volume; their claws swing past your head instead of across a screen. Glory kills keep you in first-person stereoscopic VR instead of flattening to a cinema window. The first time you rip a pinky's jaw off with a controller-driven motion, the fantasy lands the way the marketing always implied. Melee and chainsaw work through tracked hand motions or stick inputs, depending on how aggressive you want to be with room-scale swinging.

## A total conversion, not a shader

What separates KHARVOX from the older options is depth of integration. It is not reading the depth buffer after the frame is drawn; it is hooking the Vulkan pipeline and presenting the game as a native OpenXR application with separate eye buffers, hand poses, and weapon calibration. You can choose between two renderers — SFS is the default, AER is the fallback — and adjust render scale and upscaling to fit your GPU. The launcher handles calibration, handedness, and comfort options including smooth turn, snap turn, head-directed locomotion, or off-hand-directed movement. It even ships its own VR intro sequence. This is the kind of work that turns a flat campaign into a VR campaign rather than wrapping a flat screen around your head.

## The injection fallbacks

If KHARVOX breaks, or if you cannot run a community conversion, the old routes are still there as fallbacks. ReShade with the SuperDepth3D shader remains the accessible post-process stereoscopic-3D path: install ReShade with add-on support, load the Depth3D package, and pipe side-by-side 3D to your headset with head tracking. It is gamepad- or mouse-bound, but it works. Vk3DVision is the sharper fallback: it intercepts at the Vulkan driver level for geometry-correct 3D instead of a depth guess, and it is still maintained. The setup is heavier — you are swapping in a Vulkan driver layer and tuning game-specific fixes — but the output is more technically correct than ReShade's depth-buffer approximation. Then there is VorpX. Don't bother. The commercial injection driver still cannot hook id Tech 6 cleanly; broken or wrong-depth geometry keeps it off the table.

## Controls that match the fantasy

KHARVOX maps Doom's arsenal onto your hands with care. One hand holds the weapon, the other handles equipment and two-hand support points. Trigger fires, grip toggles weapon mod or alternate fire, and a hand raised behind the shoulder pulls the next long gun. Weapon switching uses a stick-driven wheel or quick taps. Face buttons cover jump, crouch, dossier, and mod switching. The scheme is dense — this is a fast shooter with a lot of verbs — but after a few encounters it becomes natural to pump the shotgun, lob a grenade, and punch out a glory kill without looking down at your hands. Physical melee is optional and configurable; set the velocity threshold higher if normal hand movement keeps triggering accidental punches.

## The place is still the payoff

Where Doom 2016 always won in VR was atmosphere, and KHARVOX only deepens that. The cold UAC labs, the molten hell-incursion set pieces, Mick Gordon's score kicking in the second combat starts — all of it reads differently when the room has real depth around you. Demon design, lighting, and the sense of a facility being torn open hit harder in a headset than on any monitor. KHARVOX does not just preserve that; it makes you stand inside it. The corridors feel tall. The hell portals feel close. The heavy metal and red lighting finally match the physical threat of the thing in front of you.

## Three weeks from beta to 1.11

The other thing worth knowing about KHARVOX is how fast it is moving. The first public build landed in September 2026 as a 0.5 Beta; it was at 1.11 within about three weeks, which is not the cadence of a mod that shipped and stalled. That pace cuts both ways. It means the rough edges in this article have a decent chance of being gone by the time you read it, and it means anything written about the current build has a shelf life. If you hit a problem, check the releases page before assuming it is still a problem — the version you install today is probably not the version this was written against.

## Beta edges and Doom's stomach

KHARVOX is a small-studio project with beta-level roughness, and that shows. Some users report Windows Defender false positives on the launcher and intro executables. A few GPU and headset combinations need extra setup. Changing graphics quality while a level is loaded can freeze or crash the game. And a known issue remains: some systems hit low FPS on the very first launch after booting Windows, though it usually stabilizes afterward. None of this is a dealbreaker, but it is the honest texture of a community conversion rather than a polished commercial VR release.

Motion controls also do not erase Doom's pace problem. The game is still brutally fast, and the glory-kill close-ups still shove the camera into demon faces with authored motion. Snap turning and comfort options help, but this is an intense ride for the stomach and the reflexes. The speed that makes Doom great on a monitor is the same speed that makes it punishing in a headset.

This is the Doom 2016 VR conversion the game deserved. KHARVOX puts the gun in your hand, the facility around your body, and the campaign in stereoscopic depth. It is good enough to recommend, provided you are comfortable with beta roughness and a moderate install. If you would rather not run a community conversion, the injection fallbacks still offer a stereoscopic window — just know the window is exactly what they are. And if you want an official id Software VR product, remember that DOOM VFR is a separate, shorter PSVR and SteamVR spin-off, not a VR mode for this game. For everyone else, the UAC facility is now open in real VR.

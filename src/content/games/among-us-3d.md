I've been in a lot of VR rooms where something wanted to kill me. Most of them were monsters, or bullets, or the void. Among Us VR is the first one where the thing that wanted to kill me was my friend Dave, standing three feet away, smiling, and lying directly to my face.

That's the whole pitch, and it works better than it has any right to. The 2D Among Us was a screen you watched. This is a room you stand inside.

Among Us VR is the first-person, native rebuild of Innersloth's social-deduction hit, built by Schell Games — the Pittsburgh studio that does VR for a living — with Innersloth and Robot Teddy. It landed in November 2022 on Quest 2 and PC through SteamVR, and it's since shown up on more headsets. There's no mod, no injector, no framework trick here. You buy it on the store, you install it, you're in. Setup is about as light as VR gets.

You spawn as a Crewmate in a chunky little spaceship, and the first thing that lands is scale. The task stations are physical. You walk up to a wire panel or a fuel port and actually reach out with tracked controllers to drag, plug, swipe, and calibrate. Tasks that were taps on a flat screen are now small manual chores, and they feel good — fiddly in the right way, like you're really keeping a ship running while someone behind you might be sharpening a knife.

Then there's the voice. Proximity voice chat means the person accusing you sounds like they're in the room, because they are, virtually. When the lights drop and you hear soft footsteps behind you and a voice say "hey, wait up," your brainstem does not care that this is a cartoon bean game. It reacts. That's the transformation: the same lie-detection loop you've played flat a hundred times suddenly has a pulse.

If you draw Impostor, the kill is in your hands — a lunge, a stab, a body on the floor — and reporting it is a button you press while keeping your tone casual. Die first and you're a ghost: you float, you watch, you can still poke the living through comms. The loop is the one you know. What changed is that now it's happening to your body, in a space, with real proximity.

After a body drops, the Emergency Meeting pulls every bean into a circle, and this is where VR quietly wins again — you're literally standing in a huddle, reading faces, watching who won't look at who. The flat game gives you a text chat and a timer. This gives you a room full of suspects who can't hide their tells.

The presence is the point and it delivers. Being in the room turns "who's acting weird in chat" into "who's standing too close to the reactor and won't make eye contact." That's a different read, a different tension, and it's more fun than watching a minimap. The motion-controlled tasks give your hands something honest to do between the paranoia, so you're never just parked waiting for a meeting. And the comedy writes itself — a bad lie told face-to-face is funnier and more damning than a bad lie typed in a chat box.

Here's the catch, and it's the only one that matters: this game is nothing without the other nine people. Boot it solo and there's no world to inhabit — no campaign, no story, no reason to be on that ship except the humans you fill it with. A good lobby is electric. A quiet one, or a lobby full of kids screaming or players who'd rather troll than play, is dead air with a headset on. That swing is real, and it's the gap between a night you'll talk about for a week and twenty minutes you'll want back.

It's also, under the hood, the same Among Us. The VR doesn't add mechanical depth — the tasks are tactile but shallow, the maps are the ones you know, the strategy is the strategy you learned flat. What VR adds is intensity and intimacy, not new systems. If the core loop never clicked for you on a phone or a laptop, first-person won't fix that.

Comfort's a non-issue — it's a slow walk-and-talk with no movement spikes to trip your stomach.

Among Us VR is the most fun I've had with Among Us, and it's also strictly a group activity. Grab three or four friends, or a reliable public lobby, and it's a genuine laughing, finger-pointing blast. Go in expecting a single-player world and you'll be the ghost with nothing to do. Put a headset on with people you trust to betray you, and it earns its time.

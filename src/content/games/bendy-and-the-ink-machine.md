There is a specific kind of dread that only kicks in when you can look down and see your own cartoon gloves. I played Bendy and the Ink Machine flat, and the studio always felt like a diorama I was watching through glass. Team Beef's BendyVR first broke that glass in early 2023 with full 6DOF VR, and InkVR now supersedes it as the working PCVR path — putting Henry's oversized shoes under your feet and his ink-stained hands at the end of your arms.

## Inside Joey Drew Studios

Bendy and the Ink Machine never got an official VR mode, and it is not coming to PSVR2 or running natively on a Quest. What it has now is InkVR, the current working PCVR mod that supersedes Team Beef's earlier BendyVR release. InkVR is the real thing: full 6DOF, Henry's hands and body tracked in the world, and motion controls wired into just about everything. It is a Unity game, and the mod bolts on OpenXR, so it runs through Virtual Desktop, Quest Link, or straight SteamVR with bindings for Quest Touch and Valve Index controllers. I ran it on a Quest 3 over Virtual Desktop, which is exactly the setup the author built and tested against.

The one thing you need to know before you go looking: this is a mod, not a store page. There is no official path, no console version, no standalone port. If you want Bendy in VR, you are booting Steam on a PC and installing InkVR.

## What Henry's hands change

Look down in InkVR and you are Henry. Cartoon gloves, arm braces, those dumb big shoes, all rendered in the game's own rubber-hose style. That sounds like a cosmetic flex until you actually start working the studio. Valves twist in your grip as you crank them. Levers swing when you pull. Switches flip under your fingers. The axe is two-handed and swings where your arm swings. Guns aim at where your hand points, with a second hand steadying the grip. Every one of those used to be a mouse-click abstraction; now they are your hands doing a job.

That is what VR changes. Bendy was always a game about atmosphere — the dripping ink, the flickering projector, the sense that the cartoon characters on the wall are watching you. Flat, that is a mood the game suggests. In VR, with Henry's body anchored to yours, it is a place you are standing inside. The weapon wheel lives on a card in your hand. The inventory is something you hold up and read. The whole studio stops being a level and starts being a location.

## Two ways into the studio

Bendy in VR has had two shots. The first was Team Beef's BendyVR, which landed in early 2023 (v1.1.0, 2023-01-11) as a full-motion-control PCVR mod — melee with the axe, the works — and for a while it was the only way in. Then the floor dropped out: as of mid-2025 the author confirmed it stopped launching on the current Steam build, and the fix was not a quick patch. The repo's last word was that they were looking at either repairing it or porting the effort to UUVR. As it stands, on an up-to-date Steam install, BendyVR is a dead end.

InkVR supersedes BendyVR as the working option. It shipped in October 2026 (v1.0.0, 2026-10-27) with a launcher that finds the game through Steam, pulls down Unity's OpenXR plugin on its own, and drops you into PLAY IN VR. Setup is low-to-medium — the launcher does the heavy lifting, but you are still running a modded Unity game, so expect to baby it a little. For a horror game whose original VR path is currently bricked, "low-to-medium and it actually runs" is a gift. Both are full VR mods, but in practice there is one door open right now, and it is InkVR.

## Where the ink thins

Let me be straight about what this is not. Bendy and the Ink Machine was never about combat depth. The axe and the occasional gun are there to punctuate the scares, not to carry the game, and that does not change in VR. If you are hunting for a meaty VR action loop, you will bounce off fast — this is a slow, puzzle-and-creep through a studio that went bad, and the fight moments are punctuation, not the point.

It is also a mod, and a young one. InkVR's repo is days old at this writing, which means the support story is "active and promising" rather than "battle-tested." That is a different risk profile than a studio-backed port. And underneath all of it sits the flat game itself: an episodic cult horror title that is relatively short and light on systems. VR does not pad the runtime or add depth the original does not have. If the flat game's pacing left you cold, the headset will not warm it up. The headset side is stable — no world tilt or spin in cutscenes, snap or smooth turning, automatic resolution — so any intensity you feel is the horror, not the VR.

## A cult horror worth inhabiting

Bendy and the Ink Machine in VR is for the people who already loved what Joey Drew Studios was selling: that specific 1930s-cartoon-gone-wrong aesthetic, the ink-everywhere dread, the story that unfolds in smears on the wall. The VR version does not reinvent that game. What it does is let you stand inside it with Henry's hands, and for a world this distinctive, that is the difference between admiring a diorama and being caught in it.

This is not a must-own system-seller, and it should not be your first horror game in VR. But if you are the kind of person who has quoted Bendy's intro monologue unironically, InkVR is the "finally" you have been waiting for — a working, full-motion-control way into a studio that always deserved to be inhabited. Get the mod, turn the lights down, and play it.
